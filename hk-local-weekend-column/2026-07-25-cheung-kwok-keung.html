The Monkey That Out-Disrupted a Typhoon

Typhoon Noul took 29 hours, a grounded airline and a live Signal No.10 watch to rattle Hong Kong; a monkey shut the Tsuen Wan Line twice for free.
張 國 強 · Cheung Kwok-keung HK Desk, Senior Correspondent Sunday, July 26, 2026

Twenty-Nine Hours Of Escalation

Typhoon Noul did not waste time. Hong Kong Observatory hoisted Standby Signal No.1 at 8:40pm Friday, jumped to Strong Wind Signal No.3 by 1:20pm Saturday, hit Gale Signal No.8 at 10:10pm Saturday, and rang in Sunday with Signal No.9 at 1:10am, the fastest run to that level all year. Whether Signal No.10 got hoisted was still an open call as the Observatory watched Noul track toward a landfall between Huizhou and Shanwei, roughly 100km out.

Cathay Pacific grounded every flight in and out of Hong Kong from 1:15am to 6pm Sunday and waived rebooking and change fees, the same machinery this desk flagged last Saturday: fast, generous, automatic for passengers. Hong Kong Express pulled about 30 flights, Greater Bay Airlines cut two Taipei runs. Wind hit past 100km/h at Cheung Chau, Green Island, Waglan Island and Tate's Cairn. By 1am Sunday: seven fallen trees, over 190 people in shelters, no injuries logged for the storm itself.

The one injury on the books happened Saturday afternoon, a construction gate collapsed on a man in Kwun Tong, under Signal No.3. The mildest signal of the weekend did the damage the strongest one didn't.

No Signal For The Monkey

None of that apparatus applies to what actually stopped the Tsuen Wan Line on Friday. A monkey got onto the tracks, service halted for about an hour, and the MTR sorted it the way MTR sorts most things: someone went down and dealt with it. No Observatory bulletin, no colour-coded signal, no press conference. It was the second wildlife hold-up in two days, after a macaque turned up at Kowloon Bay on Thursday.

Compare the paperwork. A typhoon gets a name, a ten-step colour system, a live broadcast every time the signal changes, and an airline that refunds you before you've finished reading the cancellation email. A monkey gets a guy with a broom and a radio. Both problems got fixed. Only one of them needed 29 hours of institutional choreography to do it.

The crew cleared the track in under an hour with no warning. It's a note on what this city builds sirens for, and what it just quietly goes and handles.

Noul was tracking toward a landfall between Huizhou and Shanwei, roughly 100km out, as this filed. Peak signal level and duration at Signal No.8 or above, not a gut call, are what set the Ragasa comparison. The monkey question needs no such wait: two incidents in two days, no system watching for the next one, just whoever's on shift. Watch the Tsuen Wan Line at Monday's first rush hour for whether MTR has anything more than a guy with a broom staged for a third incident. The Observatory has not confirmed landfall.
