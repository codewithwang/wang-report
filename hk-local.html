Court Sends Evergrande's Hui Ka Yan To Prison

The Shenzhen court took one afternoon on August 20 to finish something that took years to build and years to collapse: Hui Ka Yan, the man who ran Evergrande, is going to prison. He built the biggest property empire in China. Now it's gone, and so is he. Beijing didn't need a press release. A verdict does the job.

Here's why a Sham Shui Po dai pai dong owner should care about a mainland tycoon's court date. Every big Hong Kong developer watched that trial too. The message wasn't subtle: build too big, borrow too much, leave buyers holding empty flats, and the government will eventually stop protecting you. Doesn't fix our own housing mess. But it tells every landlord in this city that the free pass has a limit, even if nobody's tested where ours is yet.
