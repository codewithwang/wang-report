Colombia's Earthquake Toll Climbs as a New President Faces His First Crisis

A quake reshapes a new presidency, a plane story becomes a punchline, and Hong Kong watches its own week of typhoon cleanup and shipping costs from the sidelines.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Colombia woke to its worst earthquake in years. More than a hundred people are dead and the toll is still climbing, a brutal opening chapter for a president barely settled into office. In Washington, a separate story is making the rounds: the president, it turns out, left last month's NATO summit in Turkey by hiding in an airport catering cart, decoy plane and all, which is either sound tradecraft or the plot of a film nobody asked for. He is unharmed. Off the coast of Dover, a single small boat carried 230 people across the Channel, a record that the Home Office is calling reckless, though reckless has been the operating word there for some time. Thirty years on, a former gang member finally faces trial for Tupac's murder, proof that some cases simply wait for their moment. Closer to home, the Panama Canal's fees have hit a record as Iran's standoff with Washington keeps squeezing shipping lanes, one more line item in a week already shaped by Dolphin's aftermath.
