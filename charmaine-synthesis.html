Nepal Buries Its Dead in Mud While Washington Renames a Lake

A glacier collapse leaves more than a thousand missing on the Nepal-Tibet border, Meta pays up on child safety, and a Great Lake gets a new name nobody asked for.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. The number out of the Nepal-Tibet border keeps moving in the wrong direction, more than 1,300 still missing after a glacier let go and sent a wave of mud through villages that had no warning system built for something that fast. Scientists now say the ice snapping off, not rainfall, triggered it. Search teams are still working through the mud this morning to reach anyone who might be alive. Separately, the government spent part of its week on cartography: Lake Ontario is now, by presidential order, Lake America, a rebrand that pleases no one except whoever prints the new maps. In tech, Meta agreed to pay up to eighteen billion dollars over claims its platforms harmed children, a number large enough to count as an admission even without one, while a wave of restrictions on youth social media use keeps building across multiple countries behind it. Nvidia posted numbers strong enough to lift the whole AI trade, and China's Zhipu unveiled a model that ran on entirely domestic chips. Watch whether the Nepal death toll rises again by evening.
