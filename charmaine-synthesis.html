A 7.7 Off Indonesia Reframes a Region Still Counting Its Last One

A second major earthquake in the region in a week tests how fast rescue systems can move, while Hong Kong calls a longer housing queue good news.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Indonesia woke to a magnitude 7.7 offshore, at least 47 dead and buildings down, an echo of Colombia's quake the week before. Rescue teams are still reaching sites, and the toll is expected to climb through the day. Closer to home, the Housing Bureau looked at a public housing wait that crept to 4.8 years and called it a positive sign, which is one way to describe a queue getting longer. A new study measured indoor heat in Hong Kong's subdivided flats at 52 degrees this summer. Beijing handed Deutsche Bank a renminbi clearing mandate in Frankfurt, building settlement capacity somewhere other than here. In sport, Liverpool's much reported $7 billion sale turns out to be a twelve month option, not a sale. That detail says more about who actually runs Anfield than any press release will. The Grammys built a diversity category with a language quota attached, then watched the artist with the most leverage read the fine print and leave.
