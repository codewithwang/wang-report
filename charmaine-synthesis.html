An AI Writes New Viruses While Washington Rewrites Its Own Rules

A lab creates life that has never existed, Meta pays for the harm it did to children, and a typhoon strands Hong Kong travellers with nowhere better to be than Okinawa.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. The most consequential story of the day did not come out of a courtroom or a trading floor. It came out of a lab, where an AI model was given libraries of DNA and asked to design new viruses, sixteen of which turned out to actually work. Scientists call this a milestone. It is also, depending on your temperament, the plot of a film you already regret watching. Meta was ordered to pay 567 million dollars in the largest child safety ruling ever brought against a social media company, a number large enough to make headlines and small enough not to change behaviour. Washington had its own theatre: an appeals court blocked the White House ballroom project pending congressional approval, proving that even a president can be told to submit a permit. In Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed won the Democratic Senate primary, a genuine result for the party's left flank. And in the Ryukyus, Typhoon Dolphin closed Okinawa's airport and stranded Hong Kong travellers who had, until yesterday, very reasonable holiday plans. Watch whether the virus research triggers a regulatory response before it triggers anything else.
