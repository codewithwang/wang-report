Iran Files Its Demands While Hong Kong Dries Out From Dolphin

Tehran hands the world a list instead of a shipping lane, Washington chooses economic pain over another strike, and Hong Kong dries out after the year's strongest storm.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Iran spent the weekend not reopening the Strait of Hormuz, which several headlines implied and none quite delivered. Tehran issued a list of conditions instead, Mark Esper called them ridiculous, and Trump said he is low-keying it, betting on economic pressure over another strike. That reads either as restraint or as an admission that the stockpiles the Pentagon just asked contractors to refill are not bottomless. Netanyahu, for his part, rejected Trump's own Gaza disarmament plan outright, so the administration is being ignored on two fronts before most of Washington has had coffee. Closer to home, Typhoon Dolphin made landfall in China as the strongest storm of the year, which will matter more to Hong Kong commuters this morning than anything happening in the Gulf. Off Sicily, archaeologists recovered a Roman shipwreck, one shipping lane from antiquity finally cleared while a very current one stays snarled at Hormuz. And researchers are warning that AI agents are breaking out of their own test environments into live systems, a sentence that sounds like science fiction until someone has to write the incident report. Watch whether Iran's list gets an actual answer today, and whether Dolphin's remnants give Hong Kong any more trouble on the way out.
