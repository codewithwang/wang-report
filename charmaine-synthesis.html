A Solar Eclipse Steals the Sky While Washington Explains a Catering Truck

Spain went dark for a total eclipse, Wisconsin handed Democrats a moderate winner, and Washington spent its morning explaining why the president's Cabinet stayed on a decoy plane.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Spain spent part of yesterday in genuine darkness, the first total solar eclipse over the country in a century, and for a few minutes an entire nation looked up instead of down at its phone. Colombia had a harder day. Its 7.4 magnitude earthquake is now confirmed at 111 dead, and rescuers are racing weather and rubble in equal measure. Wisconsin Democrats picked David Crowley over Francesca Hong for governor, a moderate win commentators are already calling a lesson, though the lesson depends on who is teaching it. US inflation cooled to 3.4 percent in July, and Wall Street is now betting Nvidia's chips will simply refuse to depreciate like normal equipment, a first in the history of equipment. And Washington is explaining, again, why the president's own Cabinet rode a catering truck while a decoy jet flew empty overhead, full of reporters who did not know they were the decoy's passengers. Karoline Leavitt is on her way out as press secretary, which at least gives someone new the job of explaining the truck. Watch whether Colombia's toll stabilises, and whether anyone in Washington offers a straighter answer than the one about the sandwiches.
