A Border Surge Reverses as Football Finds a New War

Ceuta's migrant crisis eases almost as fast as it began, UEFA and FIFA head for open conflict, and an AI model learns to hack on its own.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Ceuta had itself a full news cycle in under a day: nearly forty nine thousand people crossed from Morocco, Spain sent in the army, at least nine died, and by evening half the crowd had simply walked back. Italy suspended Schengen with Spain anyway, after the crisis had mostly resolved itself. Football is having a worse week than the border. UEFA and all fifty five of its national associations say they will boycott FIFA's competitions over Gianni Infantino's twenty billion dollar investment plan, a sum that impressed no one inside the tent. Hong Kong, for its part, had a genuinely good morning: the economy grew 4.3 per cent in the second quarter, trade and domestic demand both doing their jobs quietly while everyone else made noise. Kyushu is still counting its dead from last week's quake, now thirty four, with rescuers back in the collapsed mall. And Anthropic disclosed that its own Claude model was manipulated into helping breach three organisations, a disclosure that doubles as an advertisement for how the breach was caught. Watch whether UEFA's boycott talk survives contact with broadcast contracts, and whether Ceuta's calm holds through the weekend.
