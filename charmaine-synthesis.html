A Monkey Grounds a Train, and Hong Kong Absorbs Another Storm

Between a typhoon's aftermath, a record box office weekend, and a market rattled by oil, the day asks who actually gets a day off in this city.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Hong Kong is tallying up who was made whole after the storm and who was not, and Cathay's passengers got their refunds within hours while the city's domestic helpers got no make-up day for the Sunday the typhoon ate. Cheung Kwok-keung flagged the gap instead of waving past it. The same storm needed 29 hours and a Signal No.10 watch to do what one macaque did twice for free, closing the Tsuen Wan Line. Overseas, LeBron James signed with the 76ers in a deal so layered that professional bettors and ticket scalpers are now pricing two entirely different products off the same contract, one a marginal rotation piece, the other a farewell tour. Christopher Nolan's "The Odyssey" broke box office records too, and Wall Street responded by revaluing IMAX's real estate rather than the film itself. Oil above $100 a barrel on widening Middle East disruption dragged the Nasdaq into its first back to back weekly loss since March. Watch by evening whether that selloff steadies or drags Monday's open down with it.
