Canada Calls It War While Hong Kong Counts Its Waivers

A trade fight goes rhetorical in Ottawa, a health reform quietly works as advertised in Hong Kong, and a fined footballer costs FIFA less than the ad break he interrupted.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Mark Carney did not hedge yesterday, calling Canada "at war" with Washington over trade, and a fresh fifty percent tariff wave gave the phrase some backing. Beijing has its own supply headache: the H200 chips China won this month are sitting in Hong Kong because the mainland's data centres cannot absorb them, a plumbing problem dressed up as a trade victory. Closer to home, Hong Kong's healthcare fee reform produced a number worth printing, full waivers up twentyfold to 280,000 patients, a policy that did what the pamphlet promised. Less flattering is the AFCD's dog registration system, which logged whatever breed an owner claimed and checked nothing, until a misclassified dog killed someone. In sport, FIFA fined a World Cup player 90,000 dollars for choking an opponent, a sum smaller than what Fox charged for the ad break that followed. Add a research vessel drifting near Taiwan's largest naval base, and there is plenty for institutions to answer for today. Watch whether Ottawa's retaliation lands as announced on September 8, and whether Hong Kong's proxy voting rules get an actual decision instead of another round of weighing.
