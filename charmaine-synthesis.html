A City Reopens, a Rocket Delivers, and Berlin Grieves

Hong Kong dries out and reboards its trains as the day's real headlines run through a Berlin crowd, a satellite factory, and a very large chip listing.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Hong Kong wakes to something closer to normal, the T8 signal finally down after a weekend that strained the transport network and, once again, its domestic helpers, who got refunds for cancelled flights and no equivalent make-up day for a lost Sunday. Overseas, the darker story is Berlin, where a van drove into a Pride crowd, killing one person and injuring more than a dozen; the suspect was shot dead by police, and investigators have not named a motive. In science, Starship's 13th flight moved the bottleneck in the satellite business from the rocket to the factory that has to keep pace with it, either the best problem SpaceX has had or the next one. Markets got their own jolt: a Shanghai-listed chipmaker's stock rose 472% on debut, briefly the most valuable company on the mainland boards, a number that says more about scarcity than fundamentals. Watch whether that valuation survives its first full trading week, and whether Berlin's investigators name a suspect motive by nightfall.
