A King Dies, A Gene Edit Holds, Nepal Keeps Digging

Norway crowns a new king, a one-time gene edit proves it lasts a year, and Hong Kong sends money north while the flood count keeps climbing.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Norway's King Harald V died at 89 overnight, and his son becomes King Haakon VIII by breakfast. In Nepal, rescue teams are still counting the dead and missing. Hong Kong has separately pledged HK$50 million toward the relief effort. Science had the better morning: a one-time liver gene edit from CRISPR Therapeutics held cholesterol and triglycerides down a full year after a single infusion, the first real evidence that the effect does not fade within months. A world-first trial found statins safely cut heart attack and stroke risk in healthy people over seventy. And Milo Yiannopoulos was deported from the United States to Britain. Watch Nepal's toll and the border reopening.
