Washington Bombs Iran, Then Sends Its Aircraft Carrier On Holiday

The strikes on Iran dominate the wires and the bond market, but Hong Kong's morning also holds a flood toll still rising, a botnet finally taken down, and Joshua Wong's second guilty plea.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Washington struck Iran overnight, and Tehran struck back at Gulf bases and, for good measure, a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz. Two Filipino crew did not make it home. Bond markets sold off hard on the news. The USS Abraham Lincoln, separately, pulled into a Thai port after 270 days at sea. Closer to home, Joshua Wong pleaded guilty in a second national security case. In Nepal, the flash flood toll passed a thousand, with roughly eighty Americans still unaccounted for. There was better news from the cyber desk: a joint US-European operation dismantled the long-running Sality botnet. And in the Netherlands, dozens were arrested after a fatal pre-dawn shootout in a quiet village. Watch the Hormuz shipping lanes and Nepal's rescue teams both, by tonight.
