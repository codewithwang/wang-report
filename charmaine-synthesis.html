Nepal's Flood Toll Climbs Past Bolt's Robot Record

Nepal counts nearly 1,400 missing as a new lake threatens more flooding, Norway loses its king, and Hong Kong's insurance industry has a doxxing problem.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. The Nepal-Tibet flood keeps rewriting its own numbers, nearly 1,400 people missing and hundreds dead, and now a newly formed lake threatens a second deluge on rescuers who have not finished the first one. Beijing has pledged emergency aid while weighing whether its rail ambitions in the region need a safety rethink. Norway's King Harald V has died at 89. Closer to home, an AIA staff brawl at a mall has turned into a lesson in doxxing law, with a lawyer reminding the internet that exposing someone's personal details is a criminal offence, not a public service. Hong Kong's courts, meanwhile, heard defence lawyers argue the Tiananmen vigil group's goal was an "empty slogan," a phrase the verdict will have to reckon with. Washington spent yesterday renaming a lake, which Canada rejected as, in an official's own words, real foolishness. Watch whether Nepal's newly formed lake forces the rescue suspension to extend past today.
