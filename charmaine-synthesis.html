Sanctions on Iran, a Tariff Number That Keeps Climbing, and a Robot That Outran Bolt

Washington escalates on two fronts at once while Hong Kong quietly logs a nearly three-million-car recall and a robot posts a number no human ever will.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Washington opened two fronts before breakfast. The White House unveiled economic sanctions on Iran, and in the next breath raised tariffs on Canadian cars to 50 percent, a number that has climbed steadily with no side in the room offering to stop it. Ottawa's premier is now talking about cutting electricity and critical minerals to the United States, brinkmanship backed by electricity Canada actually has to give. Closer to home, Tesla is recalling nearly three million cars in China over door handles that do not open reliably in an emergency, a detail that should have surfaced earlier in the design process. In Beijing, a Chinese humanoid robot broke Usain Bolt's 100 metre world record, either the future arriving on schedule or proof that Bolt's real achievement was doing it with a pulse. Guinea is burying thirty people after a landslide at a waste mound in its capital. Watch whether Ottawa's threat becomes policy or stays a headline, and whether Washington puts a number on how far the Iran sanctions actually reach.
