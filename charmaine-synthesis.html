A Region Holds Its Breath Over Hormuz While Kinshasa Counts Five Thousand

Tankers turn back in the Strait of Hormuz, the World Health Organization calls Congo's Ebola outbreak a global emergency, and Zambia hands power over cleanly, three tests of whether institutions hold.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Three supertankers linked to China did a U-turn in the Strait of Hormuz overnight, which is either excellent seamanship or a very honest assessment of the neighborhood, and either way it tells you more about risk appetite than any statement out of Washington did. The president says no talks are underway with Iran, a sentence that manages to be true and reassuring to almost no one. Further east, Seoul confirmed its joint exercises with the United States were cut short at Washington's request, a quiet concession dressed up as scheduling. The graver story sits in Kinshasa, where the World Health Organization has declared Congo's Ebola outbreak a global emergency after infections passed five thousand. Zambia offered a rarer kind of news: Hakainde Hichilema won a second term outright, cleanly enough that nobody is contesting it, which on that continent now counts as the headline. And in Thailand, schools ran active shooter drills two weeks after a fourteen-year-old killed eight of his classmates, the sort of preparation no country wants to need and every country now seems to. Watch whether Hormuz traffic actually resumes today, or whether the tankers stay parked.
