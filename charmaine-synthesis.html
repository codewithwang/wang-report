A Ceuta Death Toll Rises as Football's Money Men Blink First

Spain counts at least fifty seven dead at its own border this week. Hong Kong's IPO retail buyers absorb losses cornerstone investors never see, and FIFA's president learns fifty five federations can do arithmetic too.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. At least fifty seven people are confirmed dead among the tens of thousands who crossed into Ceuta this week. Spain has deployed troops and blamed traffickers, which is one way of not answering what happens to the sixty thousand who made it across. Closer to home, Rachel Lam's read on Innolight's day one loss is the more useful kind of local story: Hong Kong's IPO machine runs on retail margin debt that the cornerstone investors never touch themselves. Cheung Kwok-keung finds the government tracking its own systems to the decimal while declining to say how many residents have signed the new right to refuse resuscitation, which is itself an answer. In science, Magnus Honeyfield traces how Tether, of all companies, has become a serious funder of brain implants for paralysis, reserve income doing what biotech venture cycles used to. And Dev Chatterjee's column on FIFA is the week's tidiest lesson in power: Gianni Infantino has never lost a vote he controlled the agenda for, until fifty five federations brought their own calculators. Watch whether Ceuta's death toll keeps climbing, and whether UEFA's boycott survives contact with broadcast money.
