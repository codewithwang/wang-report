Iran Sets Terms While Hong Kong Watches Its Own Deals Close

A shipping strait stays shut on Iran's conditions, a warehouse in Guangdong burns, and one Hong Kong housing dispute finally gets a response, three tests of who moves first.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Iran told Washington the Strait of Hormuz opens on its terms or not at all, a negotiating posture from the party with the fewer aircraft carriers. The tanker the UAE says Iran targeted over the weekend suggests Tehran is negotiating and demonstrating at the same time. Closer to home, IBM's open Quantum Advantage Tracker did more for public trust in quantum computing than any announcement could, by daring thirty outside teams to break its published circuits and letting them fail in full view. In football, Newcastle sold Bruno Guimaraes to Arsenal for 75 million pounds, a deal that says less about the player than about a club whose finances did not require the money. In Wong Tai Sin, nine months separated a mediated noise complaint from a cleaver and a body on a podium, a timeline the city's housing bureaucracy will now have to explain. Water utilities got a fifteen-week head start on a warning they mostly ignored. Watch whether Hormuz talk becomes an actual reopening today.
