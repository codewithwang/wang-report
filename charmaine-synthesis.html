Evergrande's Founder Gets Life While Hong Kong's Jobless Rate Just Sits There

China closes the book on its biggest property collapse with a life sentence, while a cancer vaccine trial and a school tragedy in the Philippines round out a day heavier than most.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Beijing handed Hui Ka Yan, the man who built and then buried Evergrande, a life sentence yesterday, which is either justice or a very expensive way of admitting the regulators were also asleep at the wheel. Closer to home, Hong Kong's jobless rate held at 3.7 percent for the eighth straight month, a number the labour chief describes as steady and everyone else describes as the last calm reading before graduation season. There is real hope in the wire too: a personalised cancer vaccine kept patients disease free longer in trial results out of the UK, and Moderna's own mRNA cancer treatment cleared a key test, overnight science that will matter more in a decade than anything on a ballot this week. The darker story sits in the Philippines, where a student killed a schoolmate and himself on a livestream, a horror that will draw the usual promises about school safety and, one hopes, something more than promises. Ukraine buried at least thirteen more of its own after Kyiv took a massive overnight strike. Watch whether Manila says anything today beyond condolences, and whether the vaccine data holds up once it leaves the trial and meets the rest of us.
