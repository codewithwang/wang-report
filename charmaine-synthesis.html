A Fifa Boycott Threatens Its Own $20 Billion Party

Europe turns on the World Cup's commercial plan, a quake rattles Kyushu, and Washington finds new ways to delay its own attorney general.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Europe's football federations have united against Fifa's $20 billion commercial spin-off. The fans and the money are on opposite sides, and the fans are winning the argument. In Japan, a mall in Kumamoto that was rebuilt to withstand earthquakes has collapsed again. In Washington, the Blanche nomination continues its slow-motion unraveling, with senators demanding on paper what was apparently only ever promised in spirit. In the Middle East, strikes between the US and Iran have resumed after the briefest of pauses, and Jordan says it stopped a missile attack of its own. Closer to home, Malaysia's Anwar has found a haj fund scandal conveniently timed to an election. Watch whether Fifa's boycott spreads beyond Europe, and whether the Kumamoto mall's second collapse forces a harder look at what "earthquake-proof" actually means.
