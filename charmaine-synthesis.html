Nepal Digs Through Mud While Hormuz Trades Fire

A flood toll climbs past nine hundred, a strait of oil trades missiles, and Hong Kong's regulators pick thirty-six ways to let a machine help with your mortgage.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Nepal's death toll from last week's Himalayan flood has passed nine hundred, with the missing count now above four thousand. That count has roughly doubled every few days since the glacier gave way, a reflection of how long it takes to search cut-off areas properly. In the Gulf, the United States and Iran traded strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, oil has climbed to ninety dollars a barrel, and the UAE says it intercepted a drone over its own waters. A decade ago that sentence would have been the whole story. Now it barely raises an eyebrow. Closer to home, Hong Kong's financial regulators have signed off on thirty-six AI use cases for the banking sector, a rollout that promises efficiency and, inevitably, a helpline. In health news worth reading twice, a world-first trial found statins safely cut heart attack and stroke risk in healthy people over seventy, good news that arrived without a caveat attached. And Dolly Parton was laid to rest in Nashville beside her husband, which needs no clause of mine at all. Watch whether Nepal's search teams reach the remaining cut-off areas, and watch whether the barrel price keeps climbing too.
