A Truce Nobody Can Confirm Meets a Bomber in Moscow

Washington says Iran is close to a deal it says it never opened, a bombing near Moscow kills three, and Apple takes London to court over a backdoor it will not build.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Start with the contradiction nobody has resolved: Washington says talks with Tehran are nearly there, Tehran says no such talks exist, and oil traders are left betting on whichever version turns out true. Markets are not waiting to find out which one is real. Closer to the ground, a female bomber killed at least three near a Moscow restaurant, the latest sign that the war keeps finding new places to cut. In London, Apple has taken the government to court rather than build the backdoor it was quietly ordered to install, arguing privacy in open court instead of settling it behind closed doors. Sydney had its own fright when a building collapsed mid-morning and injured two, reopening the argument over ageing infrastructure that nobody has funded fixing. And in Washington, the acting attorney general secured his confirmation votes by agreeing to scrap a fund built explicitly to investigate the president's own circle, a tidy way to buy consensus even by that city's standards. Watch whether Tehran's denial holds through the day, and whether Hormuz stays calm enough for the oil market to believe it.
