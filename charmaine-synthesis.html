Indonesia's Earthquake Toll Rises As Hong Kong's Housing Wait Gets Called Good News

A 7.7-magnitude quake kills dozens in Indonesia, Cambridge is asked hard questions about a professor found dead, and Hong Kong's housing bureau finds an upside in a five-year wait.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Indonesia is the story that should hold your attention today. A 7.7-magnitude earthquake has killed at least 38 people, with rescuers still working through the wreckage. Closer to home, Cheung Kwok-keung reports Hong Kong's public housing wait has crept to 4.8 years, and the Housing Bureau called this a positive sign, in the same week the plan meant to fix it quietly closed for submissions. That is either very good timing or very bad timing, and the Bureau has chosen to present it as the former. In Cambridge, hard questions are mounting after the university's youngest Black professor, Jason Arday, was found dead, with the New York Times reporting the school had ignored a series of warnings about his life and his work. In sport, Dev Chatterjee explains why Liverpool's much discussed seven billion dollar sale is in fact a twelve month option, which tells you more about FSG's hesitation than any headline did. And in the science pages, that viral video of a robot climbing stairs is doing the job of a prospectus, standing in for a sales record that does not exist yet. Watch Indonesia's toll through the day.
