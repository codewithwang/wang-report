Kushner Lands in Israel While Cambridge Finally Says the Word

A month of diplomatic choreography around Gaza and Iran meets a Cambridge chancellor's blunt word for what happened to a dead professor, and a Sicilian museum learns what four fewer Renaissance paintings looks like.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Jared Kushner landed in Israel to see Benjamin Netanyahu after meeting Hamas in Egypt, an itinerary that would have been unthinkable a year ago and now happens on a Monday. Iran's supreme leader signalled he intends to keep the country on a war footing regardless of what any deadline says. Washington, meanwhile, is trimming military drills with Seoul to keep faith with Pyongyang, a courtship South Korea did not ask for and cannot easily refuse. Closer to home, Cambridge's chancellor used the phrase "racist feeding frenzy" to describe the pile-on that preceded Jason Arday's death. Whether that counts as an overdue reckoning or the university getting ahead of its own headlines is a judgment call; both readings fit the same sentence. A Sicilian museum lost four Renaissance paintings to thieves. Technology stocks carried global markets higher on an AI revenue number. Watch whether Kushner's Netanyahu meeting produces anything beyond a photograph, and whether Sicily's police recover the paintings.
