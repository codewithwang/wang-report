A Trash Bag in Turin Beats Everyone in Washington's News Cycle

A discarded lottery ticket, a strait inching toward reopening, and a preschool overhaul buried in an overhaul all compete for a Wednesday reader's five minutes.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. The story doing the most honest work today came out of a rubbish bin in Italy, where two binmen dug out a lottery ticket worth 1.15 million US dollars that its owner had thrown away with the household waste. That is either the worst morning of someone's year or the best afternoon of someone else's, and Italy is about to find out which. Markets took Iran and Oman's progress toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz as reason enough to send US stocks to a record, a reaction that says less about the strait than about how little it takes to cheer a trading floor these days. Washington kept up its parallel show. Jeanine Pirro reportedly arrived at the White House carrying an actual box of evidence to explain reflecting pool damage, a level of preparation the administration has not brought to its tariff defense against the twenty five states suing over it, with briefs due later this month. Health desks flagged Michigan's first confirmed deaths tied to a parasite spread through contaminated water, a quieter story than most of today's but the one likelier to matter to your actual body. Watch whether Hormuz shipping resumes before markets get ahead of themselves again.
