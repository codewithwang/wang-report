Infantino Apologizes, Keeps His Chair, and Michigan Votes Left

Fifa's president survives his own investment scandal by saying sorry, Michigan Democrats hand the establishment a fright, and Hormuz gets a shipping lane before it gets a resolution.
Charmaine Lo · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Good morning. Gianni Infantino spent Wednesday apologising for the private investment plans that briefly threatened his job, then kept the job anyway, which is either accountability or its opposite depending on how generous you feel before coffee. In Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed beat back thirty-two million dollars of AIPAC spending to win his Senate primary. Iran says it has agreed a Hormuz shipping route with Oman, which is progress of a sort, though calling a strait "agreed" while warships still loiter nearby is a generous use of the word. England is opening walk-in mental health centres in banks and libraries, nearly two hundred of them, proof that sometimes a government's best idea is simply putting help where people already stand in line. And in Morocco, families keep gathering near the Ceuta border for word of the missing, a story the football boycotts and market moves have crowded off the front page. Closer to home, our own newsroom logged a busier week for Hong Kong's cyber exposure than its finance ledgers. Watch whether Hormuz produces cargo, not just a handshake.
