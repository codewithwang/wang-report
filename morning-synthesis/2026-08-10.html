Trump Trades Strikes for Sanctions as Iran Digs In on Hormuz

The White House shifts from military threats to economic pressure on Tehran, but Iran's list of demands and a fresh Aramco strike say the strait fight has moved, not ended.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

Trump says Washington is 'low-keying it' with Iran, leaning on economic pain over more strikes, the clearest signal yet that this column's week-long Hormuz read (a named route agreed with Oman, not the settled deal Tehran's briefings implied) has hardened into policy on the US side too. Iran's side has not moved: Tehran's demand list, chief among them a US withdrawal from the region, still has the strait closed, and the Houthis just hit Saudi Aramco's refinery, a reminder that de-escalation in one theatre does not buy quiet in another. Brent crossed 80 dollars again overnight per Drudge, the number that settles whether this is posture or a real supply squeeze, and the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve, the emergency stockpile Washington taps to blunt price spikes, has fallen below 300 million barrels, the lowest since 1983, meaning the traditional buffer is thinner than it was during any prior Gulf flare-up. A trading desk pricing tail risk on Gulf crude now has less US reserve capacity standing behind a calm outcome than at any point in four decades. Elsewhere, Wall Street banks signed Nvidia into a 500 billion dollar AI financing structure, and Beijing keeps adding to its own reserves and industrial base, per the Guardian's read. Watch Aramco's damage assessment by end of day.
