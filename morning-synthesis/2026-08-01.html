Washington Blames a Blue State for the Water Hack Iran Likely Ran

As US strikes on Iran widen into open war, the president's own investigators point at Tehran for a cyberattack he pinned on Minnesota's governor, and Singapore just showed Hong Kong what naming names in AI defence actually looks like.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

Trump gathered his cabinet Friday night to discuss the war his own strikes have now made total: heavy overnight raids on Iran, Tehran vowing to punish the aggressor, and Riyadh's Iran-backed militia strikes in Iraq no longer look like a one-off escalation but a war footing across three states. This column called Wednesday's broken pause as Tehran choosing escalation over restraint; the cabinet meeting is that call cashed. Trump is publicly blaming Minnesota's Democratic governor for a cyberattack on the state's water systems that his own investigators believe Iran ran, and federal agencies issued a fresh warning to local water utilities anyway, attribution and politics now running on separate tracks. The falsifiable call: the water systems warning outlives the blame theater by weeks, because CISA does not retract advisories to match a press line. Rachel Lam's IPO margin piece is the sharper regional read against that backdrop, retail investors absorbing losses cornerstone money never touches. Elsewhere, roughly half the Ceuta crossers have already gone back to Morocco, and FIFA's Infantino is folding under a federation revolt that just beat the man who never loses a vote he sets. Watch whether Sunday's cabinet session produces a formal war declaration by Monday.
