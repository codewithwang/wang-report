Iran Hits Two US Bases as the Fleet That Finished the War Arrives Empty-Handed

Washington struck Iran again over Hormuz mines, Tehran hit back at US bases in the Gulf, and the carrier this column tracked for nine months made port in Thailand with nothing left to deter.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

The sequence by 06:01 HKT Thursday: Trump ordered what the White House called large and powerful strikes on Iran, citing mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials say the raids killed civilians at a wedding party. Four people died and dozens were hurt, by their count. Iran retaliated within hours, hitting two US bases in the Gulf and firing on tankers near Hormuz. One of those strikes killed two Filipino crew aboard a Saudi vessel. Moscow moved next: Putin pledged more strikes on Ukraine and, more pointedly, full solidarity with Tehran, while the Telegraph reported Russian engineers secretly helped build the missiles now landing on US positions. This column flagged the same alignment Sunday when Larak reopened the war Washington had called finished; by Thursday, Iran is hitting US forces directly, not just shipping.

The clearest sign of where this stands sits in Bangkok harbor. The USS Abraham Lincoln arrived at a Thai port this week after 270 days at sea, the deployment that was supposed to close out with the Iran file resolved. It made port with the war one strike further from over than when it left. Washington's carrier diplomacy has run out of runway before Iran has run out of targets, and the ship built to project an ending is now just refueling for a war with no announced one.

A New York desk will read the Hormuz mine claim as casus belli; an HK trading desk will read it as a chokepoint reopening under fire, with the strait carrying roughly a fifth of global oil flow. Global bonds sold off overnight on that risk plus separate AI-spending inflation fears. A regional treasury desk funding dollar trade lines now has to weigh war risk and rate risk off the same yield move, same morning, with no time to sequence them.
