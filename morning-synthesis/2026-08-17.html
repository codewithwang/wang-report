Deadline Passes as Iran Talks and Seoul Drills Both Slip

Washington blew through its own deadline for a broad Iran deal while telling Seoul to expect fewer joint drills, and both stories share the same tell: leverage that looked firm in June is thinner in August.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

Trump said in June the ceasefire would produce limits on Iran's nuclear programme; the deadline for that broader deal has now passed with the two sides, per the New York Times, still far apart. This column called the Hormuz statehood claim rhetoric ahead of leverage in mid-August, and a blown deadline is that leverage showing up empty. Khamenei's response was to install hardliners at the top of Iran's military, which reads less like negotiating room and more like a war footing. Seoul gets a parallel signal: Trump is trimming joint drills with South Korea, citing his rapport with Kim Jong Un, even as Pyongyang has grown more openly hostile toward the South under Kim. A Singapore-based FX desk reading the dollar's three-month low this morning has to decide by the open whether that softness is Fed-cut positioning or geopolitical risk discount, because the two trades unwind differently. Elsewhere, Nvidia backstopping $105bn of OpenAI's Ohio buildout is the AI capex story dressed as a chip order, and a hacker's claim of 3.6 million stolen Azure records deserves scepticism until Microsoft confirms scope. Watch whether Washington sets a new Iran deadline by tonight or lets this one lapse silently.
