Riyadh Enters the War Its Tankers Just Survived

Saudi strikes in Iraq mark the kingdom's first offensive move of the war, days after this column flagged a Houthi claim it could not verify.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

Iran broke its own pause Wednesday night and hit US forces; overnight Washington answered with what wires are calling heavy strikes, and Saudi Arabia's defense ministry struck Iran-backed militias in Iraq, its first offensive action since the war began. That is the story this column flagged conditionally eleven days ago: the Houthi tanker claim against Saudi shipping has now produced a direct Saudi military response, a break from the flat, unmoved war-risk pricing this column watched for three weeks running. A drone strike also hit two tankers, including a US gas storage vessel, at an Egyptian port, meaning three chokepoints, Hormuz, the Red Sea and now Suez's Mediterranean approach, are live simultaneously. The GDP print out this morning delivers the first hard number: US growth slowed to 1.5% in Q2, the first hard confirmation the war is dragging down output, not just oil futures. Separately, Cisco disclosed an actively exploited zero day in its firewall management platform, a credential-layer flaw that forces a firewall administrator to patch under live-fire conditions rather than a scheduled window. Watch whether Lloyd's finally reprices Red Sea-Saudi routes separate from Hormuz today; three weeks of flat pricing after two claimed hits was the tell that underwriters saw noise, not war.
