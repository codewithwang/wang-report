Iran Denies Hormuz Statehood as Second Carrier Sits Idle Nearby

Trump's claim that the Strait of Hormuz could become US territory met a flat Iranian rejection overnight, and the carrier group offshore is doing less than the rhetoric suggests.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

Iran called Trump's Hormuz territory claim a "delusion" overnight. The number worth citing is zero: zero US Navy movement toward the strait since the second carrier arrived. This column had the carriers as the tell three days ago. The tell has gone quiet, but quiet is not the same as stood down. Kai Tanner's column on Taiwan's July intrusion carries the sharper Asia read this morning, an attack built on free AI agent frameworks with no vendor behind it, which breaks the assumption baked into Hong Kong's new AI cyber rules that someone answers the phone when something goes wrong. Elsewhere overnight: Coinbase's downloaded Chinese model weights sit outside any export ban Washington can write once the download is local, not a live connection. Bond traders are sitting on $70 billion in AI shadow credit backstops that do not show up on balance sheets, exposure a credit desk finds only when it goes looking. And UAE says an ADNOC vessel was attacked in Hormuz waters, the first physical incident since the rhetoric escalated. Watch whether Tehran's denial holds through Tuesday, or whether the ADNOC incident is the first sign it will not.
