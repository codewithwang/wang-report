Iran Says It Now Talks From Strength as Washington Names Sanctions Its Weapon

Tehran's president called for an end to the war from a position of strength overnight, while Washington countered not with carriers but with sanctions language aimed at Iran's remaining trade partners.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

The pivot overnight is from ships to paper. The USS Abraham Lincoln left the Gulf after nine months on station, and Washington answered by naming sanctions, not warships, as the pressure tool: officials told the BBC the new package aims to 'squash' Iran's economy and 'collapse' its regime, with Trump separately threatening Iran's remaining trade partners according to the Guardian. Iran's president told SCMP Tehran now negotiates from strength. Both cannot be true; a carrier leaving station reads as de-escalation no matter what language Washington puts around it. Elsewhere, two long-running files closed: Hui Ka Yan's life sentence closed the Evergrande file as national debt topped forty trillion dollars, and Microsoft patched a maximum-severity flaw in Entra ID, the identity system that gates login for most enterprise Microsoft 365 tenants, already under active exploitation. A bank CISO running Entra ID needs the patch applied before Monday's login surge, not after. Hong Kong convicted two Tiananmen vigil organisers under the national security law overnight, the kind of docket entry a compliance head screening politically exposed persons for HK-linked mandates should log now, before it surfaces in a client's adverse-media check next quarter. Watch the sanctions text itself by Monday.
