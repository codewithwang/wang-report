Iran Names Its Price for Reopening Hormuz

Tehran's demand list, not Muscat's silence, is now the thing standing between the record equity rally and the strait it was betting on.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

This column had Thursday's Hormuz story as a named route agreed with Oman. Overnight it turned again: Iran says a deal is close but wants concessions first, and the list, which the Drudge-linked New York Times piece reports and Al Jazeera confirms, is still awaiting Tehran's own high-level sign-off. That means Iran is negotiating price, not logistics. The SCMP's read this morning, that Tehran believes it has Trump cornered, matches that mechanism better than the relief-rally framing has allowed. Kai Tanner's water-utility piece is the column to sit with: a federal advisory in April told operators to pull exposed controllers offline, and the 12-state campaign that followed shows the warning changed nothing about exposure, a lesson that applies as directly to Hormuz confirmations as to industrial control systems. Elsewhere, an AI-related breach report puts the average incident at $6 million and breach frequency up 56%, the number a CISO now has to walk into a board budget conversation with. Amazon lifted 2026 AI capex to $220 billion, the spend a competing cloud procurement lead now has to match or explain. Todd Blanche was confirmed attorney general overnight, narrowly. Watch whether Tehran's high-level approval lands by Tuesday.
