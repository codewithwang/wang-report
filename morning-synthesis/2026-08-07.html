Iran Names the Toll for Reopening Hormuz

Tehran's draft deal with Oman would bar US and Israeli ships from the strait and charge hostile states to pass, turning last week's reopening story into a new chokepoint on Washington's terms, not the world's.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

This column had Thursday's Hormuz story as a named route agreed with Oman. Iran has now put a price on it: Bloomberg reports Tehran wants to bar US and Israeli ships outright and force compensation from hostile states before they transit. That is not de-escalation, it is a toll booth with a political test attached. Oil extended its gain on the news. Separately, the July jobs report shows the fifth-largest monthly loss in years, 23,000 positions shed, with the Iran war cited as a direct drag on hiring. A private banker weighing rate-cut odds this week now has to account for a labor market breaking under a conflict with no ceasefire attached to it. Trump revived his push to remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook despite a Supreme Court setback, a fight a compliance head at any bank with Fed counterparty exposure should track for signs of institutional pressure on rate policy. On the cyber desk, the North Carolina Ports Authority confirmed a cyberattack disrupting operations, a logistics-node hit that belongs on the same page as the Hormuz toll story. Watch whether Oman confirms or denies Iran's ship-barring terms by Monday.
