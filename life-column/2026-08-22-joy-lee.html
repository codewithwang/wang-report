BTS Boycotted A Party They Weren't Invited To

BTS's Arirang album is mostly English, meaning the group could not have entered the Grammys' new Asian Pop category anyway, a fact that indicts the category more than any protest.
Joy Lee Life Desk Lead (Culture) Sunday, August 23, 2026

The Party They Skipped

BTS announced on July 29 that all seven members would not submit music for the Recording Academy's new Best Asian Pop Music Performance category, saying they wanted their work 'heard and loved for what it is, rather than being divided by region or language.' The Academy created the category on June 16 to cover K-pop, J-pop and C-pop, with one hard rule attached: 'meaningful use of one or more Asian languages.' BTS's most recent album, Arirang, is mostly in English. Under the category's own language test, it was never eligible, boycott or no boycott. So the biggest protest gesture of this Grammy season was seven men declining to enter a room they had already been locked out of by their own tracklist. That is not defiance, that is a group reading the rulebook a beat faster than the Academy expected. CEO Harvey Mason Jr. called himself 'saddened' by the boycott on August 19, a strange emotion to have about a submission that could not have been filed. The category was built to include Asian pop. It is currently excluding the genre's biggest English-language export by definition, before a single nomination is cast.

Numbers Already Decided This

Billboard's own chart data made the commercial argument BTS never needed to state out loud. Bad Bunny has put 13 non-English songs into the Hot 100 top 10 without asking anyone's permission, and in August 2026 Shakira and Burna Boy's 'Dai Dai' topped both the Billboard Global 200 and the Global Excl. U.S. chart. Asian, Latin and African pop are not waiting in a lobby for a category to let them in, they are already running the building, one that a Bad Bunny and a Shakira-Burna Boy chart-topper both call home without needing an Asian-language rulebook to admit them. Against that, the Academy convened its board this week and promised to 'take criticism into consideration,' not a decision. The board met while the market it built the category to catch up with kept moving, pushing the real deadline to the November 16 nomination lock. Jennie's solo EP 'Fallen Angel' lands August 28, Illit is routing its Seoul tour through Hong Kong, and the fans filling those tour dates are not waiting for a trophy to tell them the music counted.

The Academy has until November 16 to decide whether 'meaningful use of one or more Asian languages' means anything, or whether it was always a marketing category with a grammar requirement stapled on. BTS did not need to boycott to prove the rule was broken. By November 16, the Academy either publishes a language standard Arirang could pass, or it publishes nothing and lets the next act invited into that room find out the hard way.
