  • The Creative Arts Emmys handed out its statues over the weekend of September 5 and 6 at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles, and HBO Max walked out with 122 nominations across the board, more than Netflix's 111 and well ahead of Apple TV+'s 87. 'Hacks,' wrapping its fifth and final season, pulled in 24 nominations for a single comedy series, the most any comedy has ever managed in one year, beating the record 'The Studio' set the year before, which stood at 23. 'The Pitt' outdid even that, leading every show on television with 25 nominations of its own. Somewhere in a Burbank conference room, a marketing deck just got its cover slide. Mariska Hargitay, hosting the Primetime ceremony on September 14, picked up two Creative Arts wins herself for directing and producing 'My Mom Jayne,' a documentary about her late mother Jayne Mansfield, which is either a lovely personal project or the most efficient use of hosting privileges in Emmy history. David Harbour and Linda Cardellini took Supporting Actor and Actress in a Limited Series for 'DTF St. Louis,' and Alan Cumming kept his unbroken streak as reality host for 'The Traitors,' a show whose entire premise is people lying to each other for money, which, considering the week HBO Max is having, feels less like entertainment and more like a tutorial.

  • None of those trophies exist in a vacuum, because the company handing them out is currently being taken apart in a different room. Warner Bros. Discovery announced back in mid-2025 that it would split into two public companies, one holding the streaming and studio assets under the Warner Bros. name, the other left with the cable networks nobody is fighting over. That plan has since turned into a contested sale process, with the final structure, and the final buyer, still unsettled as of this week. An Emmy nomination is not really a message to viewers anymore, and viewers do not check nomination counts before renewing a subscription. Nomination counts are a message to bankers, to potential buyers, and to the talent agencies deciding where their next client's overall deal goes. Read the deal memo, not the trophy case: 122 nominations is a valuation input, the kind of asset you point to in a room full of people trying to figure out what HBO Max is worth when the rest of the balance sheet is debt and cable subscriber decline. Prestige and subscriber economics do not always move together, and this week is a good argument that HBO Max's owners are betting the former can paper over the latter, at least long enough to get a deal signed.

'The Pitt' outdid even that, leading every show on television with 25 nominations of its own. Somewhere in a Burbank conference room, a marketing deck just got its cover slide. Mariska Hargitay, hosting the Primetime ceremony on September 14, picked up two Creative Arts wins herself for directing and producing 'My Mom Jayne,' a documentary about her late mother Jayne Mansfield, which is either a lovely personal project or the most efficient use of hosting privileges in Emmy history. David Harbour and Linda Cardellini took Supporting Actor and Actress in a Limited Series for 'DTF St. Louis,' and Alan Cumming kept his unbroken streak as reality host for 'The Traitors,' a show whose entire premise is people lying to each other for money, which, considering the week HBO Max is having, feels less like entertainment and more like a tutorial.
