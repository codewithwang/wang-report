Grammys Stapled A Language Test To A Trophy

The Recording Academy built a diversity category with a language quota attached, and the artist with the most leverage read the fine print and left.
Joy Lee Entertainment Sunday, August 16, 2026

Read The Fine Print

The Recording Academy spent June unveiling five new Grammy categories as a diversity win, and by August the flagship one had already lost its guest of honor. Best Asian Pop Music Performance arrived with a rule buried in the fine print: winners need 'meaningful use' of an Asian language, which is the Grammys building a VIP room and then checking passports at the door. BTS read the terms and conditions. On July 29, all seven members announced on Instagram they were pulling comeback album 'ARIRANG' from 2027 consideration, saying they wanted the music 'heard and loved for what it is, rather than being divided by region or language.' Translated from press release: the loyalty card only stamps if you sound ethnic enough per verse. Academy CEO Harvey Mason Jr. said he was 'saddened' by the exit but defended the category as honoring 'the depth, diversity and extraordinary growth of pop artistry coming out of Asia,' a lovely sentence for a rule that disqualifies a track the moment the hook drifts into English. The board now has until August 21 to decide whether the category survives with its language quota intact, gets rewritten, or quietly disappears from the ballot altogether.

Stray Kids Stay Quiet

Here is the artist who could actually save the category, and the one staying silent about it. Stray Kids released 'This & That' on August 7 and sold 3.28 million copies in its first week, beating the group's own record and tracking toward a ninth straight Billboard 200 number one. That is the second-biggest first-week album total in the world this year, sales muscle that could hand Best Asian Pop instant commercial legitimacy the way BTS's absence just drained it. JYP Entertainment will not say whether Stray Kids is submitting. Member Seungmin offered the closest thing to a statement: the group has 'never once set out to make an album chasing a particular award,' which is a label betting on the board's August 21 ruling before it commits to anything on the record. HYBE, BTS's label, is now one of the stakeholders the Academy is consulting before its decision, meaning the company that just walked its biggest act out the door also gets a vote on what replaces it. Read the contract clause, not the press release: 'meaningful use' is a language test, and nobody with a number one to protect wants to be the first to take it on the record.

On August 21, the Grammy board rules on a category it built to look like a diversity win and that instead exposed itself as a language test with a trophy attached. Fold the category and the Academy caved to one act's boycott. Keep it and Best Asian Pop fills up with artists too new to say no. Either way, the trophy airs February 7 missing the two names that would have made it mean something, and HYBE gets a seat at the table deciding what fills the space where its own act used to stand.
