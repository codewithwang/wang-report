Iran Hits US Base, Claims Submarine Capture

Tehran escalated from tankers to territory hours after Washington struck its oil fleet, and Beijing's central bank is already moving to absorb the fallout on its own balance sheet.

The Gulf war widened today. Hours after the US destroyed five Iranian oil tankers, Iran fired missiles at an American base in Jordan, and Iranian state media went further, claiming forces captured a US submarine in the Strait of Hormuz, a claim Washington has not confirmed and NDTV reports as Tehran's word alone. NBC News reports a missile strike also killed a father hosting his daughter's wedding in southern Iran. Reuters reports Iran is now hitting US allies in the region too, not just American targets. Brent crude is closing in on $100 a barrel, and Bloomberg reports Asian markets wobbled even as AI-linked chip stocks kept climbing.

Beijing is not waiting to see how this resolves. Vincent Lai's ledger on the Geopolitical desk traces how a sustained move above $90 oil forces the State Administration of Foreign Exchange to choose between defending the yuan and letting import costs pass through to a producer price index that has been negative for three straight quarters, and how the finance ministry is testing whether it can route bank rescue funding through the National Council for Social Security Fund without booking it as new sovereign debt. Rachel Lam's Finance desk fills in the other half: Beijing just put $45 billion into banks and insurers whose capital ratios were already well above the regulatory minimum. That means this was not a solvency patch. It was a directive to lend, dressed as a recapitalization. Read together, the PBOC is trying to defend the currency and subsidize a bank rescue from the same reserve account at the exact moment a war six thousand kilometers away is deciding how expensive that becomes.

Elsewhere, Kai Tanner's Cyber desk has found a rootkit hiding inside F5 BIG-IP devices that never touches disk, so it survives the integrity checks Hong Kong and Singapore banks run under HKMA rules. Cheung Kwok-keung reports the Buildings Department finally laid 259 charges over the Wang Fuk Court fire, nine months after 168 people died. Mei Chen's desk has North Korea timing a new nuclear-capable destroyer to allied military drills, with Beijing and Moscow sending public messages of support the same week.

Washington now has a Jordan base hit and a disputed submarine claim to account for. Beijing is trying to pay its share through a central bank balance sheet stretched between propping up a currency and propping up banks that were never actually broken. That is a bet that the war stays someone else's problem for a little longer than the oil market will allow.
