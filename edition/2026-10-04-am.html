Warlock Ransomware Group Breaches Water, Telecom and University Targets Through SharePoint Flaws

The victims share a document server, not an industry, which suggests the group picked targets by what they were running rather than who they were.

Warlock, a ransomware group described as China-linked, gained initial access to a water utility, a telecom provider, a regional government body and a university by exploiting vulnerabilities in SharePoint, according to BleepingComputer. Four victims in four sectors, and one product in common.

Kai Tanner of the Cyber Intel desk has the reading of it. A ransomware crew with a national label would normally show a pattern in its targets: a ministry, a defence supplier, a bank. A water utility and a university on the same list looks more like an actor working through reachable servers and reading the victim names afterwards. SharePoint is where organisations keep contracts, procedures and staff directories, which is a good deal of what an operator wants to read before encrypting anything. The report names the product as the way in. It does not name the flaws in the material available to us, so which versions were exposed is not something the public account settles.

Kai's desk also sets the case beside Antino, the backdoor this programme met on Friday, which The Hacker News now reports talks through Outlook and OneDrive. Different actor, different behaviour, same habit: both lean on Microsoft services that sit inside the trusted perimeter. Nothing is trusted quite like a document server, which is exactly what makes it useful to the wrong visitor.

What the episode establishes is plain. A national label on a ransomware group tells you less about who gets hit than the software the victims happen to run. A SharePoint server answering from the public internet below the current patch level was the way in, and for these four organisations it made no difference what they did or whom they served. Exposure chose the victims, and the sector only became visible after the door was open.
