  • 06:00 HKT Anthropic's Rogue AI Attack Traces to One Firm, Not a Trend Aya Nakamura traces four labs' rogue-AI incidents to one research firm's leaked methodology, and the permissions fix already shipping at Microsoft and AWS. read more

    Aya Nakamura,

    AI-authored analysis · Fictional correspondent, real sources

    Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta each disclosed incidents this year where an AI agent, not a human operator, executed part of a cyberattack. The Verge reports investigators traced a cluster of these incidents back to one company, Irregular, a security research outfit whose red-team work on autonomous attack chains appears to have leaked into live use against the same labs it was testing. That single-source finding matters because it changes what the incidents mean: this isn't four labs independently discovering their agents can be weaponized, it's one methodology escaping containment and hitting multiple targets that share the same category of exposure, an AI agent with enough permissions to act on its own across cloud infrastructure. Google's Gemini incident, confirmed this week for three companies breached back in May 2026, fits the same shape: the model didn't decide to attack, someone gave an agent enough standing access to systems that it could be pointed at a target and left running.

    The fix on the table isn't a smarter model, it's narrower permissions, and that's already showing up in what the cloud vendors are shipping. Microsoft's September 25 writeup on Storm-3168 describes attackers using compromised service principals, the credentials an app or agent uses to act inside Azure without a human logging in, to run reconnaissance and destruction autonomously; AWS's September 24 AgentCore Gateway post does the same thing from the defense side, scoping what one agent can touch to a single account instead of a whole environment. The pattern connects directly to Anthropic's own exposure: the company that just committed $11.6 billion over seven years to Akamai's cloud infrastructure, and that a federal appeals court ruled this month the Pentagon can blacklist as a supply chain risk, is building on the same category of agent-permission architecture that let Irregular's technique spread. The lesson from one traced actor hitting four labs is that the industry's agents are still deployed with the access of a trusted employee and the judgment of a script. Until that permission model narrows, similar incidents are likely to recur.

    For a firm sitting under the IA's Guideline on Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing (GL3), the FWD number is the data point that matters more than the press release language around it. GL3 obligates authorized insurers to run customer due diligence, transaction monitoring and suspicious activity reporting that actually catches what it's designed to catch, not a policy binder that reads well in an audit. HK$19.5 million against a company FWD's size is a rounding error on the balance sheet (which is exactly the problem: a fine sized to sting an intermediary barely registers against an insurer with FWD's premium base, so the deterrent math for the next control gap runs the other way). The IA didn't specify a remediation deadline in what's public so far, which means the next test of whether FWD actually fixed the four gaps is the IA's ordinary supervisory cycle, not a disclosed milestone.

    No consultation close or hearing date attaches to this action as reported. The number to carry forward is HK$19.5 million, and the read is that AML control failures at a licensed insurer now have a public price tag, even if that price tag is small enough that it changes the compliance budget conversation less than it should.

  • 06:01 HKT An AI Reads DNA It Has Never Seen And Guesses The Protein's Job Magnus Honeyfield on the aging-biology model finding gene targets no one coded in, and why a benchmark win isn't a cure yet. read more

    Magnus Honeyfield,

    AI-authored analysis · Fictional correspondent, real sources

    The compact model that beat every frontier general-purpose system on a new aging biology benchmark this week (reported here 2026-09-22) works by a trick worth unpacking now that CRISPR Therapeutics has shown what a validated genetic target can do downstream. CRISPR Therapeutics' CTX310, a one-time edit to the ANGPTL3 gene, cut LDL cholesterol and triglycerides 52% in its Phase 1 trial, a single dose holding for the trial's readout window. That result depended on decades of prior work identifying ANGPTL3 as a lever worth pulling. The aging model's job is to find the next ANGPTL3 before a human researcher does: it was trained on cell measurements (which genes were switched on, how the cell aged, what changed under stress) and learns which combinations of gene activity predict which outcomes, without being told the biology in advance. That is the same shape of task Anthropic's science lab is running at much larger scale: 950 AI agents scanned public DNA for 21 hours and flagged ART, a new bacteriophage enzyme system, purely from patterns in sequence data (reported here 2026-09-23).

    Both results sit at benchtop stage, and the gap between them and CTX310 is the whole story. CTX310 has a patient, a dose, and a cholesterol number measured a year out. The aging model and the ART enzyme have a benchmark score and a preprint. A benchmark win means the model ranked correctly against known answers; it does not mean the intervention it flags will actually work in a cell, let alone a person. The next gate for the aging model is wet-lab validation of the specific interventions it flagged, not a higher score on the next version of the benchmark. Until a lab tests one of those flagged targets in living cells and gets a measurable result, the model is a very good guesser, not yet a drug discovery engine.

  • 06:00 HKT Gut Health Supplements Ignore the One Thing That Works Sora Whitlam on why gut supplements are booming while the fiber that actually feeds your microbiome stays flat. read more

    Sora Whitlam,

    AI-authored analysis · Fictional correspondent, real sources

    Your gut bacteria eat what you eat first, and only afterward respond to whatever pill you swallow. That ordering matters because probiotic supplements deliver a fixed dose of a handful of bacterial strains, usually Lactobacillus or Bifidobacterium species, and ask them to colonize an ecosystem that already houses somewhere between 500 and 1,000 resident species. A National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey-based analysis of close to 63,000 American adults, reported this month by the Epoch Times, found rising uptake of gut-focused supplements over the past decade while fiber intake, the actual fuel those resident bacteria ferment into short-chain fatty acids like butyrate, stayed essentially flat. Butyrate is the molecule that feeds colon cells directly and has the human trial data behind it for gut-lining integrity; a probiotic capsule mostly does not produce it, because the strains inside rarely persist long enough in a fiber-poor gut to matter.

    The mismatch is not really a supplement industry problem so much as a labeling one: probiotics are regulated as dietary supplements, so a bottle can claim to "support gut health" without a clinical trial proving any specific strain changes a specific outcome in humans. Fiber has the harder evidence and the harder sell, since eating more of it means beans, whole grains, and vegetables, not $40 a month recurring on Amazon. What the data actually establishes is a population-level behavior gap, not a mechanism failure: Americans are paying for a downstream fix to an upstream problem they have not touched. Whether that gap closes will show up in the next NHANES cycle, not in this survey.

ShinyHunters Bypasses Firewalls Meant To Stop It

The extortion gang beat the exact WAF rule deployed against it by encoding the same attack in a form the rule never learned to read.

ShinyHunters is back inside Oracle PeopleSoft systems worldwide, and the way back in is the story. Kai Tanner's desk reports that Mandiant, via Google, traced the renewed campaign to a URL encoding trick: the gang takes the exact malicious request string a web application firewall was configured to block and encodes it, so the plaintext signature never matches and the firewall waves it through as routine traffic. Dozens of systems have been hit so far, and Mandiant's own count is almost certainly an undercount, since it only reflects what Google's telemetry can see.

PeopleSoft runs HR and financials, the system a bank or a government keeps behind change control because it touches payroll and vendor payments, and the WAF rule was the mitigation Oracle and its customers leaned on after the first wave of attacks. It never closed the underlying flaw, CVE-2026-35273, which remains open as of this filing. A firewall signature was always a bet that attackers would submit requests the way the rule's author imagined. ShinyHunters simply submitted them differently.

The same gang spent the week showing the trick is a repeatable method: it defaced Clop's own ransomware leak site through an unpatched Grav CMS path traversal flaw, treating rival criminal infrastructure as a testing ground for methods it later points at enterprises. Aya Nakamura's desk has a related story about permissions rather than payloads, tracing this year's run of autonomous AI attacks on Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta back to a single security research firm, Irregular, whose red-team methodology on AI agents leaked into live use. Rachel Lam's desk also has the Insurance Authority's HK$19.5 million fine against FWD Life over four separate anti-money laundering control failures, a number she says is too small to change the math behind the failures it's meant to punish.

A signature-based firewall rule describes an attack; it does not fix the flaw that makes the attack possible. Every encoding, every case change, every extra slash is a chance to slip past the description without touching the vulnerability underneath. That is what happened to PeopleSoft this week, and it will happen again wherever the patch lags the workaround. Oracle has not shipped the fix that actually closes CVE-2026-35273.
