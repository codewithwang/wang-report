    Kai Tanner,

    AI-authored analysis · Fictional correspondent, real sources

    ShinyHunters says it used an unpatched vulnerability in Oracle PeopleSoft, the human-resources and payroll platform, to get into FBI systems and pull data on bureau employees. The group posted the claim alongside what it says are internal service screenshots and employee records, first reported by BleepingComputer and 404 Media. No CVE number has been assigned, and no PeopleSoft patch is out. The FBI has not confirmed a breach occurred.

    Oracle PeopleSoft runs HR, payroll and benefits administration for organizations that need to keep that data behind something more locked-down than a public website. If ShinyHunters actually has a zero-day, not a stolen credential or a misconfigured instance already public, this is the kind of software normally invisible to the outside. The same week, CISA added a Dassault Systèmes DELMIA Apriso flaw, CVE-2025-6205, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list, a separate enterprise platform now under active exploitation. Two unrelated back-office systems, two live incidents, in the same seven days.

    ShinyHunters has run a string of extortion campaigns this year built on stolen access rather than novel exploits, so an actual zero-day would be a change in tooling, not just targets. The bureau has not published an incident notice, and Oracle has not issued a security alert for PeopleSoft tied to this claim. Until one of those two things happens, what's public is a claim, a screenshot set, and a named platform still running unpatched in whoever else uses it.

  • 17:51 HKT A Cholesterol Drug That Edits The Gene Once And Stops Magnus Honeyfield on a cholesterol drug that edits a gene once and stops working on purpose. read more

    Magnus Honeyfield,

    AI-authored analysis · Fictional correspondent, real sources

    Most cholesterol drugs, statins included, work by chasing a moving target: they block an enzyme or receptor every day, and the moment you stop taking the pill, the target starts working again. CTX310, from CRISPR Therapeutics, does something structurally different. In a Phase 1 trial reported this week, a single infusion cut LDL cholesterol and triglycerides by 52% in patients with treatment-resistant lipid disorders, by editing the ANGPTL3 gene in liver cells so it can no longer make the protein at all. ANGPTL3 normally holds back two enzymes that clear fat from the blood; silence the gene and those enzymes run unopposed, pulling triglyceride-rich particles out of circulation. The edit happens once, in the DNA itself, not in a protein the drug has to keep suppressing.

    The mechanism is a base edit delivered by lipid nanoparticle, the same delivery vehicle as an mRNA vaccine, but here it carries editing machinery into liver cells rather than a vaccine antigen. It lands on a shortlist of gene-silencing cholesterol approaches now in human trials, alongside RNA interference drugs like inclisiran that suppress the same pathway without touching DNA. This is a Phase 1 trial: it establishes that a single edit lowers lipids and, so far, holds, but Phase 1 in the US is built to check safety and dosing in a small group, not to prove it prevents heart attacks. The next gate is whether CRISPR Therapeutics moves CTX310 into a larger Phase 2 trial with a defined cardiovascular endpoint, and how long the reduction lasts before the company needs to report a durability readout beyond the current data.

Second Zero-Day Claim Hits US Agency Same Week

ShinyHunters says it broke into the FBI through unpatched Oracle software. Washington has not confirmed or denied it, hours before Xi lands for his summit with Trump.

ShinyHunters says it used an unpatched flaw in Oracle PeopleSoft, the HR and payroll platform, to pull FBI employee data. It is the second time in five months this masthead has covered the group claiming a breach of a major American institution on the strength of its own screenshots. Kai Tanner's desk has the detail: no CVE number assigned, no PeopleSoft patch out, and the FBI has neither confirmed nor denied a breach occurred. The tooling is what sets this one apart from the usual extortion playbook. The timing does the FBI no favors. Xi Jinping touches down in Washington on September 24, and Mei Chen's desk reports his opening ask of Trump is explicit: halt Taiwan arms sales.
