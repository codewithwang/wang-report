Taiwan Rejects Xi's Sovereignty Claim After Trump Left It Unanswered

Taipei has put a formal answer on the record to a demand Washington heard in person and has not addressed, so the only reply Xi has received comes from the island itself.

Taiwan has answered Xi Jinping's claim of sovereignty over the island, and Washington has not. Taipei rejected the statements Xi made during his US visit, asserted that the Republic of China is a sovereign state, and said it and the People's Republic of China are not subordinate to each other. The formula is old. The timing is what makes it news. Xi's one concrete ask in Washington was that Trump say the US opposes Taiwan independence. Trump did not say it, and he did not refuse it either. The question left the summit unanswered, and Taipei has now declined to wait.

Kai Tanner's Cyber desk has the other headline of the day, the FBI telling ShinyHunters members to turn themselves in after Dutch police arrested an alleged leader. The arrest was Dutch work. The bureau's part, as reported, is the invitation, and nothing in the reporting says stolen data was recovered or any access was cut.

Back to Taiwan, where Beijing's reply came in hardware. On Tuesday Taiwan counted three PLA aircraft and six PLAN vessels around the island, with one aircraft entering the south-west air defence zone, and China's coast guard was reported on patrol east of Taiwan. The numbers are modest, and they are the signal Beijing sends whenever Taipei speaks for itself. As Mei Chen reports from the Geopolitical desk, Taipei also made a second point, this one aimed across the Pacific. An official said Taiwan enjoys bipartisan support in the US Congress and that helping Taiwan strengthen its defences serves US interests. That is a message to the legislature, reminding it that the executive branch has left Xi's demand hanging.

The logic of Taipei's move is simple. A government that hears a foreign capital claim sovereignty over it and says nothing has, in practice, accepted the claim. Taiwan has closed that door on its own account. Until Washington replies, the rejection from Taipei is the only formal answer Xi's demand has received, and it is the island, not the United States, that has put its position on the record.
