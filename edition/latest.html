Morning roundup

Hong Kong ransomware figures, the Giza train collision, Krakow’s investigation, Iris Plaza and the US album chart.

  • Hong KongHong Kong ransomware cases rise

    Hong Kong recorded nearly 18% more ransomware cases during the first three quarters of 2026 despite an overall decline in technology crime, SCMP reports, citing police figures. Police attribute the increase to AI-assisted attacks and say they recorded AI agents being used in cyberattacks as recently as October.

  • WorldDeadly train collision near Giza

    At least seven people died and more than 100 were injured when two trains collided near Giza, Egypt, the BBC reports; prosecutors warned the death toll could rise. President Abdul Fattah al-Sisi ordered an investigation, while services between Giza and Upper Egypt were reported suspended without a restoration timetable.

  • WorldKrakow opens terrorism investigation

    Six people were killed and 15 injured in Saturday’s Krakow shooting, the BBC reports. Prosecutors launched a terrorism investigation, two wounded officers emerged from comas on November 8 and Main Market Square reopened Sunday evening; ministers said the suspect appeared inspired by far-right ideologies.

  • CyberIris Plaza flags potential exposure affecting up to 7.7 million people

    According to BleepingComputer, Iris Plaza’s October 29 disclosure, following October 28 suspensions, concerns potential exposure of 5.8 million current/former members and 1.9 million non-members. Potentially affected data varies by person: contact details, birth dates and active-member passwords encrypted with an unspecified scheme; credit-card data was absent, and no misuse or related loss had been reported to the company by November 5.

  • LifeThriller rises to No. 2

    Billboard reports Thriller rising from No. 13 to No. 2 on the Billboard 200, its highest since 1984, with 55,000 equivalent album units, up 89%, over October 30 to November 5, helped by Halloween and new editions. Luminate figures put Rodrigo’s you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love first with 57,000 units, down 10%; November 14’s chart publishes November 10.
