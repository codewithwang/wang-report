The Evening Briefing

Five developments for this evening.

Evening briefing

  • Cyber · Kai TannerStolen credentials at cloud host Railway ran up about $740,000 in AI charges

    BleepingComputer reports that Railway’s November 10 update on the October breach put unauthorised AI charges from stolen credentials at about $740,000 and potential exposure at up to 100,000 workspaces, with Railway pledging to cover charges providers decline to refund. Railway says it removed the attackers and fixed the flaws they exploited; it had previously advised customers to rotate their secrets.

  • World · Mei ChenUS House stalls impeachment bid against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth

    The BBC reports that the US House voted 211 to 204 on November 10 to send the impeachment resolution against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, blocking immediate action. Congressman Thomas Massie alleges Hegseth waged war without congressional approval and oversaw extrajudicial killings; Hegseth calls the operations lawful and the impeachment bid sabotage.

  • Hong Kong · Cheung Kwok-keungNorthern Link railway estimate falls 28% as construction starts

    The South China Morning Post reports that Hong Kong cut the cost estimate for the Northern Link railway project to HK$86.2 billion after approving the second phase’s financial arrangements. The government and MTR signed an agreement on November 10 to start construction on both the main and spur lines, with opening planned by 2034.

  • World · BBCTrump says FBI is looking into Nvidia chief’s affair

    Trump said the FBI was looking into Jensen Huang’s affair with a woman suspected of spying for China, an allegation Beijing rejects, and the FBI declined to comment to the BBC. Nvidia commissioned an independent inquiry and says it has no reason to believe company secrets or national security were compromised, while a proposed shareholder class action alleges Nvidia and Huang concealed his ties to the woman.

  • Hong Kong · RTHKHong Kong plans up to 12 hours of family-care leave for government staff

    RTHK reports that Hong Kong government staff are expected to get up to 12 hours of family-care leave a year from January 2027, in blocks of one to four hours for needs such as taking a relative to a medical appointment. Unused hours would expire at year-end, and the Civil Service Bureau is still finalising the details and plans to consult staff representatives.
