Binance freezes withdrawals after $6.3 billion hack

A suspected record crypto theft, Tai Po survivors’ demands for accountability, and the human cost of Indonesia’s wildfire smoke.

Evening briefing

  • CyberBinance promises to cover losses as recovery continues

    Binance says $6.3 billion in digital assets were affected by a hack of Binance.com, roughly half have been frozen, and withdrawals are disabled; Binance.US said its platform was unaffected. Its preliminary investigation points to a compromised engineer’s laptop followed by a complex attack, while analytics firms attribute the theft to North Korean hackers: a financial empire can apparently still have a laptop-shaped entrance.

  • Hong KongTai Po survivors question the wait for an apology

    Wang Fuk Court residents told the South China Morning Post that officials’ apology had come too late, with some saying housing chief Winnie Ho’s resignation could not answer for failures across departments. Their demand gives the promised reforms a practical test: officials must enforce the rules, exercise judgment and take responsibility for their decisions.

  • WorldStudy examines Indonesian wildfire smoke

    The Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air estimates that wildfire smoke caused 3,560 deaths in Indonesia in September, twice its August estimate. About 57.8 million people breathed unhealthy to hazardous air that month, a measure of the harm obscured by the mild-sounding word “haze”.

  • Hong KongGuilty plea on disposal charge in Abby Choi trial

    Kwong Kau, Abby Choi’s former father-in-law, pleaded guilty on Friday to illegally disposing of her body, reversing his earlier plea during the fifth week of the High Court trial. He and his two sons remain on trial for murder; the admission concerns the disposal charge.

  • AIMistral releases Large 4 weights for independent deployment

    Mistral has released the weights of its trillion-parameter Large 4 model, allowing organisations to customise it and run it on their own infrastructure, with three reasoning modes to balance performance and cost. Its launch report claims coding and defensive-security gains, but the immediate management opportunity is wonderfully familiar: own the technology, then own the job of keeping it running.
