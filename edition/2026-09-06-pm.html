Ukraine Truce Spares Capitals, Spares Nothing Else

Kyiv and Moscow agreed to stop hitting each other's capitals hours before Witkoff and Kushner arrived, but the sanctions plumbing that actually shapes the war's cost did not move.

Russia and Ukraine have agreed to stop striking each other's capital cities, the Kyiv Independent reported, landing in the same week Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Putin in Moscow and are now headed to Kyiv, the first such visit since the peace push restarted, per the New York Times. Overnight, Bloomberg reports, the two sides traded strikes anyway. A truce on skylines is not a truce on the war. A deal to leave Moscow and Kyiv alone is the kind of thing a president can announce between flights.

What did not move is the machinery that actually sustains this war, and Vincent Lai's desk has the ledger on it. The capital-strikes truce says nothing about the correspondent-banking workaround that has routed Russian trade settlement through banks in Central Asia and the UAE since 2022, and nothing about the frozen reserves sitting at Euroclear that the G7 has argued over for three years without releasing a cent. The Kremlin can decide a missile ceasefire by lunchtime. Unwinding sanctions requires compliance officers to act, and they will not move until the capitals that imposed the sanctions actually lift them. The envoys touring Kyiv this week were not sent to do that.

Mei Chen reports that Beijing resumed air drills around Taiwan, 21 aircraft and 15 ships in Taipei's count, in the same week its Joint Staff chief sat down with the US Indo-Pacific commander for the first time since that channel went quiet. The military track and the diplomatic track are running on separate clocks there too, and neither Beijing nor Washington has said one should be read as a forecast of the other.

Kai Tanner's cyber desk is watching a version of the same split from a different angle: CrowdStrike's own endpoint agent turned out to have a privilege-escalation hole in the trust boundary it was sold to protect. A headline fix and the underlying architecture are not the same thing, and mistaking one for the other is how institutions get surprised. Witkoff and Kushner can extract a quieter skyline in Kyiv this week. They cannot extract a faster reserve-release timeline from Brussels, and that timeline, not the sound of the strikes, is what still determines the cost of holding Russian-linked paper. The war got quieter in exactly the two cities that make for good television. Everywhere the money actually moves, nothing changed at all.

China Resumes Taiwan Air Drills, Meets US General Same Week

Mei Chen on why Beijing's resumed Taiwan drills and its new US general-to-general channel are running on separate clocks.

Continue reading

Beijing paired a renewed air incursion near Taiwan with its military chief's first meeting with the US Indo-Pacific commander since the pause began, a sequencing choice that undercuts the read that either move stands alone.

Taiwan's defense ministry tracked 21 Chinese aircraft and 15 ships around the island in the 24 hours after Beijing resumed the air drills it had paused, according to Taipei's count reported by Taiwan News. The resumption did not happen in isolation. In the same window, China's top military officer sat down with the commanding US officer for the Indo-Pacific, the first such meeting since the drills stopped, per The Japan Times. Liu Zhenli, chief of the Joint Staff Department under the Central Military Commission since 2022, is the officer running that channel; the meeting continued regardless of what Taiwan's airspace looked like that week.

Beijing running the incursion and the general-officer channel on the same calendar is not a contradiction it needs to manage. It is proof the two tracks do not touch. A pause in air drills was never a signal sent to Washington, and its resumption is not one either; the military-to-military channel with the US Indo-Pacific command operates on its own clock, unaffected by how many sorties Taiwan's ministry logs in a day. That decoupling matters more than either data point alone: Washington cannot read troop movements around Taiwan as a barometer of what Beijing will say in the next general-to-general meeting, and Taipei cannot expect a warmer US-China military channel to translate into fewer aircraft over its air defense identification zone. The two tracks will keep moving independently, and the next test is whether Liu's counterpart channel produces any restraint the flight logs don't already show.

Cyclists Are Dying And Police Say It's Your Fault

Cheung Kwok-keung on nine cyclist deaths this year and a police response that blames the riders, not the missing lanes.

Continue reading

Nine cyclists have died on Hong Kong roads this year, double last year's toll, and the government's answer so far is to blame the riders.

Nine people have died cycling in Hong Kong in the first half of 2026. Last year at this point it was four. Police looked at the numbers and said the problem is cyclists running red lights and riding where they shouldn't. Not the roads. Not the drivers. The cyclists.

Look, I ride my bike to the wet market some mornings and I can tell you the actual problem: there is nowhere to ride. You get a painted line for two hundred metres and then it just stops, and you're back in traffic with a minibus doing forty. Doubling the deaths and blaming the guy on two wheels for not stopping at a light is like blaming someone for drowning in a pool with no fence around it. Nine families lost someone this year. Before the government points fingers again, maybe put in a bike lane that actually goes somewhere.

Dev ChatterjeeSports Desk, Senior Correspondent · filed 17:57 HKT

Katie Taylor Retires Undisputed at Croke Park

Dev Chatterjee on Katie Taylor's undisputed retirement at Croke Park, and the pay gap she leaves unsettled for those coming after.

Continue reading

Ireland's greatest boxer walked away a champion in front of her own crowd, closing the ledger on a career that outgrew the sport that underpaid her.

Katie Taylor did not lose her way out of boxing. She left the way almost nobody in the sport gets to, undisputed at 40, WBA, IBF and WBO super-lightweight belts still hers, on a Saturday night in Dublin with Croke Park packed for a fighter instead of a football final. This is her third time unifying a division across a career that started with an Olympic gold in London in 2012 and ends with a homecoming she got to script herself. Most boxers get carried out on a stretcher of diminishing purses and a promoter's indifference. Taylor got a stadium.

Here is the number the trophy ceremony did not mention: male fighters with a fraction of Taylor's résumé have commanded ten times her purse for a single undercard appearance, and it took two decades of headlining her own cards to force Croke Park to sell out for a woman's fight rather than host one as a favor to the men above her. Taylor's retirement closes the account on a career spent proving the sport's economics wrong one sold ticket at a time, not on winning the argument that women's boxing should have paid her that way from the start. She leaves the belts undisputed. The pay gap she fought against remains unresolved for the fighters coming after her.
