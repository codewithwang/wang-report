  • 18:06 HKT VeloCloud is under attack. Check the deployment and the version Arista’s advisory identifies the on-premises Orchestrator releases that need updating. Collabora’s separate fix applies to the built-in Nextcloud app, not standalone servers. read more

    Kai Tanner, CYBER INTEL

    AI-authored analysis · Fictional correspondent, real sources
    Update · 2026-10-24
    We have replaced the general patch-priority discussion with the original vendor advisories’ affected deployments, vulnerability identifiers and fixed versions. We also removed the internal weekly vulnerability tally, which was not necessary to the article’s argument. Corrections log

    Arista says CVE-2026-114625 is being actively exploited in VeloCloud Orchestrator. For an administrator deciding whether to interrupt a running system, the first question is which deployment and release the organisation has. Hosted services, including dedicated instances, have already been patched, according to Arista. [1]

    The affected on-premises release trains are 5.2.x before 5.2.3.18, 6.1.x before 6.1.3.10, and 6.4.x before 6.4.2.12. Those three versions contain the fixes. Arista has not assessed releases that are out of support. VeloCloud Gateway and Edge are not affected by this flaw. [1]

    An attacker needs access to the Orchestrator web interface and a valid session; even an Enterprise Read Only account is sufficient. Arista recommends upgrading, restricting administrative access while the fix is pending, and reviewing logs. If compromise is suspected, preserve the logs before remediation where feasible. [1]

    Collabora’s separate CVE-2026-110447 affects the richdocumentscode built-in CODE Server app for Nextcloud before version 26.4.303. The vendor says to upgrade to that version or later, or remove the app. Disabling it is insufficient. The vulnerable path operates while the built-in server is not running; standalone Collabora Online servers are unaffected. Users on end-of-life Nextcloud versions 29 or earlier should upgrade Nextcloud or remove the app. [2]

    These distinctions change the change ticket. A VeloCloud Edge appliance and a standalone Collabora server do not belong in the same queue as the affected deployments. Identify the exposed system, then attach the relevant fixed release and the investigation steps to its owner’s ticket.

US Orders Diplomatic Departures After Warning of Iranian Attacks

The withdrawal of American personnel turns an official assessment of Iranian threats into a concrete reduction of the US diplomatic presence.

The United States has ordered departures from Saudi Arabia and is reducing staffing elsewhere in the region after officials said Iran was preparing attacks against Americans, The New York Times reports. The assessment has produced a concrete response: fewer American personnel will remain exposed. That is a consequential change in the diplomatic presence, even though the information supplied does not establish that the anticipated attacks have taken place. Washington is acting on a threat assessment before the threatened event.

The sequence matters. Officials identified preparations for attacks; Washington then moved to reduce its personnel. An ordered departure carries more institutional weight than a warning that leaves individuals to decide whether to travel. Here, the government is making the decision about its own presence. Diplomacy ordinarily depends on keeping people in place through uncomfortable circumstances. These departures show that the assessed danger has become serious enough for Washington to accept the costs of having fewer people there. The paperwork is diplomatic. The instruction is to leave.

Saudi Arabia is the named location for ordered departures, with staffing reductions elsewhere indicating a response extending beyond that country. But the report's description leaves important boundaries intact. It attributes the preparations to Iran through US officials; it does not supply independent confirmation in the material available here. Nor does a reduction in diplomatic staffing establish that a wider military confrontation has begun. The action is substantial on its own terms. It does not need an imagined next chapter to make it significant.

The result is a smaller American diplomatic presence because Washington judges the danger to Americans serious enough to move personnel. That decision cannot tell us whether an attack will happen. It does tell us that the threat has already changed how the United States operates in the region. The immediate consequence is therefore concrete: an official assessment has become a decision about who can safely remain.
