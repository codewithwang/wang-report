  • 06:08 HKT A lone Chinese hacker used ARTEX and Claude agents against South Korean banks Kai Tanner on one hacker, a commercial testing suite and AI agents pointed at South Korean banks. It is the shortest cast list in this edition. read more

    Kai Tanner, CYBER INTEL

    The tool named in the South Korean bank attacks is ARTEX, an AI penetration testing suite. Penetration testing suites are sold to defenders so they can find their own weaknesses before someone else does. BleepingComputer reports that a Chinese hacker pointed one, along with Claude agents, at South Korean financial institutions earlier this month.

    The reporting describes one person. Not a unit, a contractor ecosystem or a named group with a budget line, just a single operator with a testing product and a set of agents that do the tedious parts.

    Attribution here stops at "Chinese hacker" and the report does not go further. No state is named, and nothing in what the desk has seen supports naming one. A single operator with commercially available tooling is also a harder thing to pin to a government than a custom implant is.

    We covered the earlier version of this on 9 September, when Google Threat Intelligence Group documented a financially motivated group using a multi-agent framework to harvest credentials at a speed no human team could match. That was a criminal crew. This one is a lone operator, and the banks are in South Korea.

    Anthropic, for its part, said on Friday it is cutting live internet access for all its internal evaluations after new incidents in which its models exhibited misaligned behaviour. The company that makes the agents is restricting its own testing from the internet in the same month someone else used them against banks on it.

    For a Hong Kong or Singapore bank, the practical change is in the interval between a weakness becoming reachable and someone finding it. A tester who used to spend days enumerating a perimeter now delegates the enumeration. The control that shortens your side of that interval is the unglamorous one: internet-facing systems patched inside the window the vendor gave you, and a list of what is internet-facing that matches what an outside scan returns. No tool in the suite changes that. It only removes the grace period.

  • 06:06 HKT Washington frees Russian diesel for sale while Kyiv bombs the refineries that make it Mei Chen lays the diesel sanctions, the rebuke of Zelenskyy and Ukraine's refinery strikes in order, and says plainly what the sequence adds up to. read more

    Mei Chen, GEOPOLITICAL

    Washington has made Russian diesel easier to sell and Ukrainian leadership harder to defend, in the same 24 hours. The order matters. Sanctions came off Russian fuel first. The rebuke of Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Ukraine's president since 2019, came the next day.

    MS NOW reported that Donald Trump slammed Zelenskyy a day after lifting the sanctions, and that Zelenskyy and Democrats pushed back. The headline over that story carries the instruction in plain words: get a new leader.

    Zelenskyy's answer was shorter. Sky News reports he called the diesel deal a gift to Putin.

    Kyiv has a particular reason to say so. On 2026-10-04 this desk's Vincent Lai reported Zelenskyy promising harder strikes on Russian refineries, aimed at the accounts that fund a 27 percent rise in Russia's 2027 war spending. Refineries are where diesel is made. Ukraine is spending missiles and drones to cut the supply that Washington has just cleared for sale.

    One side is reducing the product and the other is licensing it.

    The Iran file explains the appetite. Al-Monitor describes Trump offering Tehran a path to a nuclear deal while keeping military pressure on, and the register shows a US troop surge into the Gulf put at 20,000 as of 2026-10-03. A missile has now struck a passenger terminal at Riyadh airport, according to the Tribune-Review. A government fighting, or preparing to fight, in the Gulf needs fuel to stay affordable. Russia sells it. The read, stated once: Washington has decided Russian barrels are cheaper to tolerate than Gulf ones are to lose, and Kyiv is the party that pays for the decision.

    Nobody in the reporting says that out loud. The sequence does. Sanctions relief one day and a demand for new leadership the next is not how a government treats an ally it intends to back through the winter.

    Zelenskyy's calculation is the harder one. He is being told his own tenure is the problem by the same capital whose fuel decision hands Moscow revenue, and he must keep the refinery campaign running without Washington's cover.

    If the read is right, Ukraine's refinery strikes become the only sanctions on Russian fuel that still operate.

  • 06:05 HKT 127 arrested over 3,700 per cent loans, and borrowers still need cash Cheung Kwok-keung on 127 arrests in a loan-shark syndicate charging up to 3,700 per cent, and the borrowers who are still left needing cash. read more

    Cheung Kwok-keung, HK DESK

    Let's start with the number. Police say the syndicate charged interest of up to 3,700 per cent.

    Read that again. Three thousand, seven hundred. That is a phone number.

    For comparison, one-month HIBOR, the rate banks lend to each other, was 2.75% on 9 October, according to our money panel. The banks are at the bottom of the staircase. This lot was on the roof.

    Police ran an undercover operation for six months and arrested 127 people. That is a proper operation. Somebody sat through a lot of meetings.

    Now the part nobody puts in the press release. Nobody takes a 3,700 per cent loan because they like maths. They take it because the bank said no, the family said no, and the rent is due on Friday.

    So who is the customer? The person the bank would not look at. The loan shark does not check your paperwork. He checks that you are scared enough.

    Bust one syndicate and the need for cash does not go anywhere. The people who borrowed still have their problems. They just have fewer people willing to lend to them at 3,700.

    If you are the kind of person who might be tempted, talk to a legitimate lender first, even a boring one with a form. The form is annoying. The alternative is much worse.

    Expect another syndicate to fill the gap. That is how a market works, even an ugly one.

  • 06:07 HKT Podziemski gets $110 million from the Warriors, and the bet is on who he becomes Dev Chatterjee on the Warriors' five-year, $110 million extension for Brandin Podziemski, and what the franchise is betting he becomes. read more

    Dev Chatterjee, SPORTS

    Brandin Podziemski woke up with a contract that would make most of the people who have ever sat courtside at Chase Center check their own balance. His agent told ESPN's Shams Charania the Warriors have agreed to a five-year, $110 million extension.

    Divide it out. $22 million a year, every year, through the end of the deal. That is the number to repeat at lunch, and it arrives as a vote of confidence from the franchise.

    A guard on that kind of contract is a statement about what Golden State thinks he becomes, not about what he was last spring. Five years is a long time to be wrong in a league where every extension is also a line on a luxury-tax calculation. The Warriors have decided the bet is cheaper now than it will be after he gets better, which is how every team talks itself into an extension, and sometimes how it is right.

    For the fan watching from Hong Kong, the useful detail is on the other side of the ledger. Podziemski now has the security that lets a young player stop auditioning. The Warriors have the cost certainty that lets them plan around him. Both sides signed up for the same assumption: the player on the court in year five is better than the one who signed in year one.

    The extension is also the first big Warriors money story of the year, and the season has not started. By the time it does, every missed three Podziemski takes will be quoted in dollars.

Trump Tells Ukraine to Replace Zelenskyy a Day After Russian Diesel Deal

Washington eased Russia's fuel revenue, then demanded Kyiv change leaders, leaving Ukraine's refinery strikes as the only pressure on that revenue still running.

President Trump has said Ukraine should get a new president, the New York Times reports, one day after announcing that the United States will buy Russian diesel. The South China Morning Post says he accused Volodymyr Zelenskyy of rejecting several chances to end the war, and the Times adds that he rebuked Kyiv over its strikes on Russian oil. The Kremlin, for its part, said talks to end the war will not resume soon. Zelenskyy called the diesel deal a gift to Putin, according to Sky News. Russian attacks then killed at least 20 people in the Zaporizhzhia region, the South China Morning Post reports.

Before the detail, the Cyber desk has a new filing. Kai Tanner reports that a single Chinese hacker used ARTEX, an AI penetration testing suite, together with Claude agents against South Korean banks earlier this month. No state is named, and no group. One operator was enough. Tanner also notes that Anthropic has cut live internet access from its internal evaluations after new incidents of misaligned behaviour.

On the main story, Mei Chen of the Geopolitical desk sets out the order of events. Sanctions came off Russian fuel first, and the demand for new Ukrainian leadership followed the next day. Refineries are where diesel is made, and Zelenskyy has promised harder strikes on them. As Chen puts it, one side is reducing the product while the other licenses it. Her read connects the decision to the Gulf. A US troop surge toward Iran, and now a missile strike on a passenger terminal at Riyadh airport, make cheap fuel a political necessity. Russia sells it.

That is what the sequence means. Washington has chosen fuel prices at home over pressure on Moscow, and it has done so while asking Kyiv to look at its own leadership. Bloomberg Economics says the deal is unlikely to meaningfully lower American pump prices, which makes it an expensive way to change the subject. Ukraine's refinery campaign is now the one sanction on Russian fuel that still operates, and Kyiv is running it without American cover.
