Iran Says War Has No Military Solution as Talks Stall

Tehran says the war cannot be won by force and keeps Hormuz shut, so a conflict with no exit is now setting the pace for everything else Washington has pending.

Iran's foreign minister has told Washington that the war cannot be won militarily, and talks to end it appear to be at an impasse, according to the South China Morning Post. In the same news cycle, Iran says the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until its conditions are met, as the Star Advertiser reports. The New York Times adds the cost on Tehran's side: security chief Mohsen Rezaei made a rare public admission of dire economic crisis, weeks after the US tightened sanctions and its naval blockade.

So each side has now said out loud that force will not settle this, and neither has offered the other a way to stop. That is a more honest position than the usual one, and a less useful one.

Before the rest of the morning, two filings. Kai Tanner's Cyber desk has a China-aligned group tracked as TA419 phishing US AI policy experts with fake Microsoft sign-in pages that capture the second factor along with the password. Aya Nakamura's AI desk has OpenAI saying it disrupted a coordinated campaign to copy its models through its own API, and what that means for the customers who sit next to that traffic.

The war's reach into other files is where Mei Chen's Geopolitical desk comes in. Taiwan's envoy has taken a stalled arms sale to Washington in public, as The Straits Times reports. A sale described as stalled has not been refused, and Mei Chen's reading is that a Gulf war crowds the Taiwan file down the queue without anyone in the administration having to decide it.

That is what an unwinnable war with no negotiating track does. It does not need to claim attention, it simply holds it. The Gulf has stopped being one crisis among several and become the calendar by which Washington's other decisions are scheduled, and Taipei is finding that out first.
