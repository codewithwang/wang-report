Hong Kong

Public housing's recorded wait falls to 4.4 years; rider injury cover remains a draft

The release points to faster access for recently housed applicants, while couriers still await legally enforceable compensation.

The Housing Bureau's November 20 release measures general applicants housed during the 12 months ending September 2026. The comparable composite average at June's end was 4.8 years. Conventional public rental housing and light public housing contributed different averages: 5.3 years and 2.8 years respectively. This retrospective measure does not predict the remaining wait for every applicant still in the queue, according to the definitions in [RTHK's report](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/en/component/k2/1878604-20261120.htm).

About 11,200 general applicants were housed during the third quarter, including approximately 5,450 in light public housing, more than four times the preceding quarter's light housing figure. About 96,100 general applications remained at September's end. These figures support an improvement in access through the measured period. The bureau's projected average supply exceeding 35,000 units annually from 2027/28 through 2031/32 remains a forecast.

Private affordability has a separate measurement. The supplied Rating and Valuation Department observations put the territory wide private domestic rent index, covering all classes, at 211.2 in August and 212.2 in September, an increase of approximately 0.47 percent. Those older monthly observations cannot establish a November weekly rent trend or show that shorter public housing waits have reduced private rents.

For delivery workers, the November 22 development was completion of a draft compensation bill intended for Legislative Council submission in December. [Dimsum Daily's account](https://www.dimsumdaily.hk/hong-kong-government-plans-three-tier-paid-sick-leave-for-food-delivery-riders-and-couriers/) describes proposed injury leave at four fifths of wages. Maximum leave would be three months for workers averaging at most three daily hours, six months for those averaging more than three but below five, and 12 months for those averaging at least five. The calculation uses a six day working week over the preceding 90 days. Coverage would run from accepting an order through collection and delivery until the worker returns to sit on their vehicle. Platforms would have to buy designated compensation insurance when the law takes effect.

Subsequent allocations, separated by housing type, and newer rent observations will test whether access and affordability improve together. For riders, the next evidence is the submitted bill, its final coverage and an effective date.

Cyber

Microsoft fixes Entra monitoring flaw after attackers read tenant metadata

Affected organisations need to review privileged access and logs while the extent of historical disclosure remains unknown.

Entra Connect Health monitors organisations' local identity infrastructure through Microsoft's cloud administration service. According to Microsoft's notification reproduced by [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-entra-connect-health-flaw-exploited-to-steal-tenant-data/), the company discovered its access weakness on November 9, deployed a server fix on November 19 and concluded its investigation on November 20, when it began notifying affected organisations. Exposed information included administrator and technical contact email addresses, organisation identifiers, licence tiers and directory synchronisation status.

Microsoft says it identified no unauthorised configuration changes and that passwords, password hashes, authentication tokens and valid cryptographic keys were not exposed. It says no customer software update is required. Its advice nevertheless includes phishing resistant authentication for privileged accounts, reviewing Connect Health configurations, and examining Entra audit and sign in logs. Administrator contacts and organisation details could help an attacker make a phishing approach more convincing.

The accompanying inspection script displays current metadata values. It cannot reconstruct what an attacker read historically. Microsoft has not disclosed the affected tenant count or when exploitation began, so neither the scale nor the duration can be inferred from the notification dates. Further disclosure and historical access records would be needed to establish those boundaries.

Separately, the supplied [CISA catalogue observations](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cisagov/kev-data/develop/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json) contain nine additions dated November 16 through 22, all entered from November 17 through 20, compared with 12 dated November 9 through 15. The later entries cover Puppet Enterprise, Synology DSM, LiteLLM, REDCap, Apache Axis and Roller, VMware vCenter, SAP NetWeaver and Cisco Expressway. This is the coverage of the supplied observations, not a verified final weekly census. Catalogue dates identify additions, not the start of exploitation; fewer additions do not demonstrate fewer attacks. Matching these entries to deployed products and obtaining the relevant advisories would establish the operational work required.

US contractor disclosures face new restrictions as Russian strikes disrupt Ukrainian services

The reported order threatens suppliers' contracts, while attacks on Ukraine interrupt essential services and raise questions for NATO.

The accessible [New York Times preview](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/21/us/politics/trump-congressional-oversight-contractors.html) reports that a November 20 executive order directs federal contractors and subcontractors to seek White House permission before complying with congressional information requests. Firms must coordinate responses and withhold material the administration considers confidential or protected by executive privilege. Contracts could be suspended or terminated for noncompliance. This places a commercial sanction behind the administration's instructions to potential witnesses.

Democrats intend to investigate contracts awarded without competitive bidding and alleged conflicts of interest, but their election victory has not transferred congressional control during this week. [Al Jazeera's November 21 account](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/21/us-midterms-2026-what-happens-before-the-new-congress-takes-office) says Republicans retain both chambers during the outgoing session. The incoming term begins January 3, 2027, on the source's US calendar. December 11 is the reported government funding deadline; an extension into February remains speculation. Representative Jamie Raskin's description of the contractor order as unlawful is an objection, not a judicial finding. The accessible material establishes neither its legal validity nor subsequent enforcement. Actual information requests, contractor responses and any court decisions will show how far the restriction operates.

In Ukraine, the November 20 attacks produced documented disruption to electricity, water and transport. [The New Voice of Ukraine](https://english.nv.ua/amp/zelenskyy-calls-nov-20-russian-attack-on-ukraine-one-of-the-largest-50661048.html) reports damage to three Kyiv bridges, water and power outages in the capital, a strike on Sknyliv railway station and emergency electricity outages in Lviv, Zakarpattia and Ivano Frankivsk. Zelenskyy said attacks hit power facilities serving nuclear plants and energy links with Europe. He also described missile groups arriving at intervals of 20 to 30 minutes, obstructing rescue and repair work. Russia said its targets were military industrial sites and transport infrastructure. These statements about targeting remain attributed accounts.

The Polish incident requires separate treatment. [SBS and AFP](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/russia-pummels-ukraine-in-deadly-strikes-after-renewed-international-criticism-of-war/kd5qp5i1n) report that Polish authorities confirmed one missile had landed at Nowy Lipowiec, with another missile and a drone suspected at other locations; nobody was hurt. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it deliberate provocation and said he had informed NATO. That assessment does not independently establish intent. The Ukraine reports also contain differing launch and casualty counts that cannot be reconciled from the available material. Restoration records, subsequent official assessments of the attacks and landed objects, and NATO responses are the next consequential evidence.

Hong Kong election reporting and Taiwan military observations remain gaps in this review: the linked Election Committee article was unavailable, as were the dated aircraft and naval observations. Those gaps support no conclusion about the importance or direction of either story.

AI

Matched AI workload costs fall 3.95 percent as disclosures expose limits in data protection

Buyers can compare a cheaper fixed token workload, but must separately establish who receives and retains their prompts.

The supplied [OpenRouter observations](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models) match 28 model identifiers present on both November 15 and November 22. For one million input tokens and 250,000 output tokens, the mean listed cost fell from approximately US$3.8904 to US$3.7368, excluding cache discounts. The later snapshot lists 36 available models. The comparison therefore covers the matched sample and specified workload, not the price movement of every available model.

[Dragos's November 19 disclosure](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens) concerns approximately two million test prompts submitted from July through November. Unique links and bait credentials let researchers connect later activity to particular requests. Dragos reported evidence of compromises at three providers, reseller access to prompts through logging, and apparent failures to honour privacy settings. Attempts to use planted credentials were distinct from automated retrieval of seeded URLs, which supplied evidence about data handling and onward access. A triggered canary alone cannot identify which intermediary disclosed the prompt. These findings concern selected providers and routes, not an estimate of prevalence across the industry, and the incidents did not all begin this week.

[Signal's November 20 announcement](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/) addresses a separate route to information: operating system assistants accessing messages displayed on a device. It describes screenshot restrictions for iOS 27 and automatic activation of Android's Screen security setting when assistant access is detectable. Coverage varies across Android devices. Signal says screenshot restrictions cannot prevent every other route, including access through files, and it cannot reliably block screenshots on macOS. Its recommendation for macOS 27 is to add Signal to Excluded Apps in System Settings > Siri. Disabling the available protection can expose other conversation participants' content as well as the device owner's.

The connection for buyers is practical. A lower quoted workload cost leaves routing, retention and assistant permissions to be assessed separately. Neither disclosure establishes that the observed price reduction caused an exposure. Provider investigations and repeatable tests of request routing, retention settings and device controls could establish whether the specific weaknesses have been addressed.
