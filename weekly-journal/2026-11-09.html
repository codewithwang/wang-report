Hong Kong

The investigation at Tung Chung West station widened substantially during the week. The Buildings Department had identified two substandard concrete columns during its 27 October inspection. Further findings were reported on 3 November, and a department statement on 7 November, reported by RTHK on 8 November, put the number identified by the project's registered structural engineers at around 30. MTR suspended the relevant construction activities. The department said temporary supports beneath the floor slab remained in place and that the defects therefore posed no risk to the station's structural integrity. Those supports could be removed only after the engineers were satisfied with the works. An investigation report and proposed repairs were due by mid November. The department's preliminary review found no evidence of intentional departures from approved plans or breaches of relevant requirements in the follow up actions. [1]

Building renovation procurement brought a separate enforcement development. On 6 November, authorities announced that 28 people had been arrested in an operation against a suspected bid-rigging syndicate targeting building maintenance and renovation. RTHK reported suspected offences including deception, forgery, conspiracy to defraud and conspiracy to offer an advantage to an agent. It was the first operation by the joint police, ICAC and Competition Commission task force established after the previous year's Wang Fuk Court fire. Security Secretary Chris Tang alleged that a suspected syndicate pressured homeowners to sign quickly by citing changing material prices and urgent works. At least 20 buildings or estates were involved: 12 had awarded contracts worth about HK$100 million, while eight had prospective contracts worth approximately the same amount. Officials promised to contact the affected owners' corporations. The report established arrests and allegations, without establishing guilt or confirming delivery of that assistance. [3]

RTHK also issued a public denial on 6 November after the crowdfunding website FundHK used its abbreviation and described it as a media and institutional partner. The broadcaster said it had authorised no cooperation, partnership or endorsement. Its statement establishes RTHK's position; it supplies neither FundHK's response nor evidence of a police investigation. [2]

Cyber

The week's operational security reporting included attacks that could change physical storage conditions. BleepingComputer reported on 5 November that a further wave of refrigeration attacks, reported during November, affected more than 115 stores across six European countries. It also reported that the French Blood Establishment confirmed on 3 November that an incident involving a refrigeration equipment provider had altered temperature and defrost settings at three blood storage facilities. These were reported incidents; the article did not establish that patients had received damaged blood products. [5]

A joint advisory issued on 5 November by CISA, the FBI and three US departments described malicious activity targeting refrigeration and cold chain monitoring systems globally. Attackers could change settings, force continuous heating or defrost cycles, and obscure the temperature readings operators depend on. Inventory losses, equipment damage, fire hazards and harm from unsafe storage were identified as possible consequences. The advisory named no particular vendor, model or fixed firmware. Its recommendations covered removing public internet exposure, protecting necessary remote access, separating operational technology from business networks, updating equipment and enforcing safe temperature limits downstream of controllers. The reported ability to hide readings made access controls and physical operating limits relevant alongside monitoring. [6]

For Zyxel switch owners, the new information concerned an earlier repair for CVE-2026-7273 that remained inadequate. The subsequent vulnerability has a separate identifier, CVE-2026-107441. Zyxel's 3 November advisory listed Patch 2 and earlier firmware on ten GS1900 models as vulnerable to CVE-2026-107441, which could allow an unauthenticated attacker with the required network access to execute operating system commands. Each affected model required its corresponding Patch 3 build. The Hacker News reported on 5 November that those replacements had been posted on 23 October. According to its account of Binarly's testing, the earlier patch checked a parameter counter only after an oversized input could overwrite it; Patch 3 checked before copying and limited the copy's size. Previously reported data theft from 996 switches around 17 August did not establish which firmware those devices ran. Neither the vendor advisory nor that attack history demonstrated that installing the replacement removed access already obtained by an intruder. [7] [8]

The affected Zyxel firmware and replacement builds are: GS1900-8, 2.90(AAHH.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(AAHH.3)C0; GS1900-8HP, 2.90(AAHI.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(AAHI.3)C0; GS1900-10HP, 2.90(AAZI.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(AAZI.3)C0; GS1900-16, 2.90(AAHJ.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(AAHJ.3)C0; GS1900-24, 2.90(AAHL.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(AAHL.3)C0; GS1900-24E, 2.90(AAHK.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(AAHK.3)C0; GS1900-24EP, 2.90(ABTO.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(ABTO.3)C0; GS1900-24HPv2, 2.90(ABTP.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(ABTP.3)C0; GS1900-48, 2.90(AAHN.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(AAHN.3)C0; GS1900-48HPv2, 2.90(ABTQ.2)C0 and earlier, fixed in 2.90(ABTQ.3)C0. [8]

QNAP made the distinction between patching and recovery explicit in its 6 November Plex2Shell notice. It reported suspected compromises among customers and relayed Plex's account of widespread exploitation following publication of a proof of concept on 16 October. The fixed Plex Media Server version was 1.43.3.10861 or later. QNAP said a NAS running version 1.43.2 or earlier while reachable from the internet should be treated as potentially compromised, because updating would not remove an attacker's installed software. The reported recovery guidance included checking indicators, performing a clean reinstall of the NAS operating system, QTS or QuTS hero, and resetting Plex and NAS credentials. Customers seeking help confirming compromise were told to preserve the Plex Logs folder before removing the application. QTS and QuTS hero were not themselves affected by these vulnerabilities, although a compromise through Plex could still require recovery of the device. [24] [4]

The four Plex flaws are CVE-2026-96652, CVE-2026-96654, CVE-2026-96655 and CVE-2026-96656. QNAP says users who do not need remote access should disable Plex Remote Access and stop forwarding port 32400. It recommends a VPN where remote access is needed. A port opened through UPnP or automatic router configuration counts as internet exposure when assessing whether an unpatched NAS may have been compromised. [24]

Finance

HKEX's third quarter result fell short of the analyst forecast. The retrieved South China Morning Post report, updated on 6 November, puts net profit at HK$4.859 billion against an analyst estimate of HK$5 billion. That was a miss. Profit fell 1 per cent from a year earlier and about 10 per cent from the second quarter's HK$5.4 billion. SCMP described it as the first annual decline in quarterly profit in ten quarters and linked the result to slower turnover, without quantifying that contribution in the available excerpt. [14]

Technology shares fell sharply across successive trading sessions. The Nasdaq lost 5.5 per cent in the US session on 5 November. On 6 November, Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each closed down 6 per cent; the Kospi had fallen more than 8 per cent during trading, triggering a temporary halt. The BBC reported concerns about returns on AI spending and possible restrictions following the Democratic election victories. Its account documents those concerns, without isolating their contribution to the declines or identifying an enacted restriction responsible for them. Despite the fall, the Nasdaq remained approximately 4.6 per cent above its level at the start of the year. [13]

Fox announced completion of its Roku acquisition on 6 November. Under the terms in its SEC filed release, holders were entitled to $95.30 in cash plus 0.9799 Fox Class A shares for each Roku Class A or Class B share. Former Roku shareholders consequently owned approximately 26 per cent of Fox's outstanding common shares, with existing Fox shareholders owning approximately 74 per cent. The purchase brought Roku together with Tubi and FOX One. Fox's expectation of approximately $400 million in annualised cost savings within two years remained a forecast, as did its expectation that the transaction would increase free cash flow per share by the second full year after closing. Integration and delivery of those benefits remained ahead. [12]

Life

Elvis Presley Enterprises filed suit against Suno on 6 November, bringing allegations about training material and vocal identity into the same case. According to Billboard, the complaint alleges unauthorised training on copyrighted songs, including Can't Help Falling in Love and Don't Be Cruel, and unlawful imitation of Presley's voice. The voice claims invoke Tennessee's ELVIS Act, enacted in 2024, which allows claims against providers of tools for unauthorised voice and image cloning. A separate suit filed that day by Eight Mile Style, a publisher owning much of Eminem's catalogue, also made copyright allegations concerning Suno's training. These were new legal claims, with no adjudicated outcome established by the report. [20]

Suno's response on Saturday, 7 November, said it prohibited unauthorised use of artists' work, blocked prompts naming specific artists and songs, and used outside providers to screen uploaded audio and lyrics. The Presley complaint disputed the effectiveness of those safeguards, alleging that lyrics containing his name had produced a voice imitation as recently as October. Billboard reported links to examples supplied by the plaintiff; its account did not independently authenticate them. The unresolved questions include whether the training uses were lawful and whether the generated performances infringed protected identity rights. [20]

Fox's completed Roku acquisition also carried a consumer question beyond the transaction terms recorded by Finance. Fox said Roku's platform reached more than 100 million households and promised to preserve its openness to other content partners. That is a management commitment about the platform's future operation. The retrieved corporate announcement documents no observed change to viewers' search results, recommendations or access. [12]

Astronomical imaging supplied a completed research result. A study available from 4 November and recorded in Caltech's repository on 5 November demonstrated a method for modelling and subtracting stellar glare from James Webb Space Telescope observations. Rodrigo Ferrer-Chavez and colleagues reported sensitivity improvements of two to fifteen times over standard techniques in the comparisons tested, including the first detection of the already known planet 51 Eridani b at a wavelength of 4.3 micrometres. The method compensates for instrument changes that make reference images imperfect guides to the glare. The improvement applies to the tested observations; it establishes neither a universal gain across Webb imagery nor the discovery of an Earth analogue. Applications to future observatories remained prospective. [23] [26]

Voyager 1's anticipated distance milestone remained ahead of the week. NASA's mission page forecasts that it will reach one light day from Earth on 18 November 2026, approximately 26 billion kilometres away. At that distance, radio propagation alone would require roughly 24 hours each way, or 48 hours for a command and an immediate reply. That calculation does not predict when an operational response would actually be transmitted. NASA's 30 October outreach article describes the same dated milestone as a distance from the Sun, whereas its mission page specifies Earth. The retrieved pages leave that discrepancy unexplained.
