Mainland Money Is Betting $30 Billion to $35 Billion on Shein

Shein's Hong Kong cornerstones are bargaining the IPO down to protect an aftermarket that mainland Stock Connect money, not returning Western capital, will be left holding.
Rachel Lam Finance & Risk Desk, Senior Correspondent Sunday, August 16, 2026

Three Valuations, One Stake

Shein Global Holdings, the Singapore-headquartered parent, sold stakes to private investors in 2022 at a valuation near $100 billion. By 2023-2024 that same equity was trading in secondary rounds at roughly $64 billion, per SCMP and CNBC reporting. Now, ahead of a Hong Kong listing that opens order books around August 20, sources tell SCMP the company is targeting $35 billion and 'won't be less than' $30 billion. $100 billion, then $64 billion, then $30 billion to $35 billion, each one a fresh mark on the same asset, none of them set by a public market until this week (a private-round mark is an opinion; an order book is a vote). The 2022 buyers are the ones who should be doing this arithmetic hardest. They are about to get the first tradeable, public price on a position they have carried at a private mark for four years, and it prices their stake at less than a third of what they paid for it. That is the entire private-market growth thesis on Shein, from 2022 to now, getting marked down in public for the first time.

Who Holds The Price

Look, the popular explanation for Hong Kong's 2026 listing boom is that Western capital is coming back. The flow data out of HKEX's Stock Connect desk says otherwise. Southbound net inflows hit a record HK$35.876 billion in a single session on August 15, accounting for 57 percent of Hang Seng turnover that day, and year-to-date southbound inflows have already passed HK$938.921 billion, more than all of 2025's HK$807.869 billion combined. HKEX's own tally puts 2026 IPO fundraising at HK$328.2 billion, about $41.8 billion, through July, up 154 percent year-on-year. That is the pool cornerstones are negotiating around when they push Shein toward the low end of its range: not BlackRock or Temasek this time, but mainland accounts buying Hong Kong-listed shares through southbound tickets. Shein swung to a $99 million net loss in the first quarter of 2026 from a $395 million profit a year earlier, and full-year 2025 net income fell 38.7 percent to $2.06 billion even as revenue grew 8 percent, after Washington's May 2025 removal of the de minimis exemption pushed Shein's effective US duty rates from a 0 percent to 62.5 percent range up to a 10 percent to 87.5 percent range. Those numbers explain why cornerstones want a floor, not a ceiling, under this listing.

Shein's pricing range was set at $30 billion to $40 billion, not the discounted conversion floor. Cornerstones bargaining toward $30 billion to $32 billion still sits inside that band, but it is a near miss, not a clean call, and the order book opening August 20 will decide it. If the mainland money holding this listing up decides Shein's US tariff exposure isn't worth defending, the aftermarket on August 28 sells off.
