Three Robots Argue, HKO Won't Pick A Winner

Hong Kong's own weather site is showing three AI models split on whether a cyclone brushes the city Saturday, and the Observatory is letting the public referee it themselves.
張 國 強 · Cheung Kwok-keung HK Desk, Senior Correspondent Monday, September 7, 2026

The Site Disagrees With Itself

Open the Hong Kong Observatory's Earth Weather platform this week and you get three separate opinions on your Saturday. FengWu, one of the AI forecast models HKO runs on its own site, has a low-pressure system forming in the Bashi Channel by Friday and possibly spinning into a tropical cyclone in the South China Sea by Saturday, passing within roughly 400km of Hong Kong. Fuxi and Pangu, two rival models sitting right next to it on the same page, show the system sliding west toward Hainan instead. Same data window, same government website, two different weekends.

HKO itself is not picking a side. No tropical cyclone warning as of Monday, just a forecast for windier, showery conditions as the low-pressure area teams up with the northeast monsoon. That is the entire official position: it might get breezy, bring a jacket. Nobody at HKO is required to reconcile FengWu with Fuxi for you, so the models just sit there on the public dashboard, showing different tracks, and you are the one who has to decide whether to reschedule the barbecue.

Everyone Planned Outdoors Anyway

The timing is the joke. This is the first weekend of the new horse-racing season, HKJC opened at Sha Tin on Saturday with John Lee officiating and Aaron Kwok performing, and last season the two tracks combined pulled over 400,000 mainland and overseas visitors, double the year before. The city's tourism pitch this cycle is explicitly outdoors and event-led: a racing calendar built for crowds, a 130-plus event schedule, and district markets like Discovery Bay's weekend stalls and PMQ's Flockfest running Friday through Sunday. None of that was built with a hedge for 'the AI models can't agree if there's a storm.'

The government has also been busy protecting the tourist experience on the ground, the Travel Industry Authority pulled the licences of two agencies and a tour guide on Monday over coerced shopping trips earlier this year, the third such action in 2026. Fair enough, that is a real problem worth stopping. But you can police the tour guides and still leave the weather itself as a coin flip nobody in government will call. One risk got a regulator. The other got a shrug and an umbrella suggestion.

What Actually Holds

Strip out the drama and the honest answer is nobody knows yet, not FengWu, not HKO, not you. This far out a track can still move 400km either way. What is real: winds and rain pick up this weekend regardless of whether anything organizes into a named storm, so outdoor plans get wetter and windier no matter which model wins the argument. If you are heading to Discovery Bay or Flockfest, go Friday if you can. If you are racing, check the signal Saturday morning, not Thursday night.

By Monday the models will have sorted themselves out one way or the other, and whichever one was right will get quietly forgotten while HKO's careful 'bring an umbrella maybe' looks either cautious or lucky. Either way, the city just ran its biggest outdoor weekend of the new season on a forecast three robots couldn't agree on, and HKO's next scheduled update lands Saturday morning, the same window racegoers are told to check before heading to Sha Tin.
