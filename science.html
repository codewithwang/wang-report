China's EAST Reactor Sets Plasma Confinement Record With New Magnet

China's Experimental Advanced Superconducting Tokamak, EAST, in Hefei has pushed plasma confinement to a new record, run with the help of a newly installed superconducting magnet weighing 582 tonnes. The magnet's job is to hold a cloud of hydrogen isotopes at tens of millions of degrees inside a magnetic bottle without letting it touch the walls, since no physical container survives contact with plasma that hot. The reported gain comes from a "density-free" operating regime, meaning the plasma stays confined without needing to be packed as tightly with fuel, which matters because the tighter you pack a plasma the more ways it finds to slosh, kink, and leak heat before fusion reactions can pay back the energy spent containing it.

This is still a confinement demonstration, not a power run: EAST is a benchtop-scale research reactor that has never produced net energy, and a bigger magnet plus a new operating mode is an engineering result, not a fusion ignition. The number that would actually move the field is how long the record confinement holds and at what temperature, figures that determine whether the technique scales to ITER, the international reactor under construction in France that China is a partner on and the intended proving ground for exactly this kind of magnet and plasma regime.
