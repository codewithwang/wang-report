The Ministry of National Defense's decision to run reservist mobilization alongside the Abrams tanks this week, rather than confine the new hardware to live-fire ranges, is a signal read in Taipei as inventory management for a contingency now treated as near-term. The People's Bank of China's open-market desk reads a different instrument entirely. Through the second week of the Han Kuang exercises, the PBOC's seven-day reverse repo rate has stayed anchored near 1.4 percent, and the offshore yuan has traded inside its recent band against the dollar, a signal that the mainland's liquidity desks are not pricing in a near-term cross-strait shock, or, more precisely, are not being instructed by Beijing's economic planners to build a contingency premium into the rate corridor the way they did during the August 2022 Pelosi-visit window, when overnight funding costs spiked and the central parity rate moved by more than the daily band typically allows.

That gap between Taipei's calendar and the PBOC's calendar is the trade. Han Kuang runs through this week on a schedule set by Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense; the PBOC's Monetary Policy Committee does not meet again until its routine quarterly session, and the People's Liberation Army's warplane and warship presence around the island, tallied daily by Taiwan's defense ministry at 21 aircraft and 14 vessels as of this week, has not moved the mainland's funding desks to reprice risk the way a genuine mobilization signal would. Premier Li Qiang's economic working group, which has used liquidity tools rather than statements to signal policy intent all year, would be the first mover if Beijing's assessment of Han Kuang shifted from routine to provocative. Until the reverse repo rate or the central parity band moves outside its recent range, the PBOC's own ledger says this week's drills are read in Beijing as exercise, not preface.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 6 Taipei Runs Its Largest War Drill With Abrams Tanks

Taiwan's Han Kuang exercises this week deploy US-made Abrams tanks for the first time in the drill's history, alongside reserve mobilization and urban resilience testing across the island. The exercise is the largest Han Kuang since the drill's creation, and it runs concurrent with a Taiwan defense ministry tally of 21 Chinese warplanes and 14 warships operating near the island, a presence count the ministry now publishes daily. The Abrams arrived under a 2019 sale approved in President Tsai Ing-wen's first term; their appearance in reserve-mobilization drills, not just live-fire ranges, is the tell. Tanks in a reservist call-up scenario are not a deterrence photo op. They are an inventory check for a mobilization Taipei's Ministry of National Defense now rehearses as a near-term contingency, not a hypothetical one.

Beijing's answer sits in a different weapons class. The DF-17, a hypersonic glide vehicle mated to a ballistic booster, is nearing operational readiness, according to reporting this week, and it renders the Abrams' battlefield utility beside the point. A tank division does not intercept a glide vehicle that separates from its booster mid-flight and maneuvers through the atmosphere. Taipei is drilling for the invasion that follows a landing; Beijing is fielding the weapon that would decide whether a landing force ever needs cover. The mismatch is the story Han Kuang's own footage does not show, and it is the reason the exercise reads less as deterrence than as triage: hardening what a ground force can reach, because the missile force is not Taiwan's to counter.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 5 NPM Worm Plants Claude Code Hooks Across Hundreds Of Packages

The worm that started in keyv@6. 0.

Continue Reading →

The worm that started in keyv@6.0.0 on August 4 did not stop at credential theft. SafeDep researchers tracked it spreading beyond the Keyv and Cacheable namespaces into hundreds of downstream packages, and among the payloads it drops are hooks targeting Claude Code, the kind of local developer tooling that runs with the same file and shell access as the engineer who installed it. A worm that plants agent hooks is not stealing tokens anymore. It is trying to recruit the build machine.

The mechanism matters more than the package count. npm's dependency graph gives a single compromised maintainer credential a blast radius measured in transitive installs, and a hook aimed at an AI coding agent inherits whatever permissions that agent already has on the host, the same over-permissioning gap Anthropic's own incident disclosures flagged last month. The control that would have changed this outcome is dependency pinning with lockfile integrity checks enforced at CI, not after. Engineering teams running Claude Code or any agent with local execution rights should audit hook configurations against known-good hashes before the next `npm install`, not after the next disclosure.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 6 China Reports Second Death in Gene Editing Trial

A patient has died in a second undisclosed case from an investigator-led gene editing trial in China, according to reporting by STAT News on August 5. Investigator-led trials, sometimes called IIT in China, sit outside the standard regulatory pathway used…

Continue Reading →

A patient has died in a second undisclosed case from an investigator-led gene editing trial in China, according to reporting by STAT News on August 5. Investigator-led trials, sometimes called IIT in China, sit outside the standard regulatory pathway used for company-sponsored studies: a hospital or researcher designs and runs the trial under looser reporting rules, with no requirement to post results on a public registry before or during enrollment. That gap is why the death surfaced only after reporters went looking, not through a disclosure filed at the time it happened.

CRISPR-based editing works by cutting DNA at a targeted sequence and letting the cell's repair machinery paste in or disable a new instruction, which means an adverse event can come from the edit itself, from the viral or lipid vehicle that delivers it, or from the patient's underlying disease, and distinguishing between those causes requires exactly the kind of trial-level detail an investigator-led study is not obligated to publish. The next gate is procedural, not scientific: whether China's regulators or the hospitals running these trials move investigator-led gene editing studies onto the same public-reporting standard as sponsored trials, the mechanism that would have surfaced this death months earlier.

Jerry Jones told reporters this week he would be open to selling his stake in the Dallas Cowboys, the first time in thirty-seven years of ownership he has said the sentence out loud. Jones bought the team from Bum Bright on February 25, 1989, for $140 million. Forbes now values the Cowboys at $13 billion, the highest figure in the NFL, which means the man who once mortgaged nearly everything he owned to buy a football team is sitting on a return of roughly 9,185 percent. Mark Cuban's name is already attached to the speculation about who writes the next check.

Here is the arithmetic nobody needs a calculator for: that $13 billion valuation was built almost entirely without a Super Bowl since January 1996, which tells you the Cowboys stopped being a football team and became a media company that occasionally plays football on the side. Jones did that on purpose, selling out AT&T Stadium suites and Cowboys Network rights while the roster went three decades between championships. A sale, if it happens, values the brand recognition over the banner count, and the next owner inherits a fan base that has made peace with exactly that trade.
