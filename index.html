Nepal first. Scientists now say a chunk of glacier snapped off and triggered the wall of water that erased villages on the Nepal-Tibet border. 270 bodies have been recovered and more than 1,300 people remain missing; Australia and Britain both confirm dozens of their nationals unaccounted for. That is the story leading every wire this morning, and it should.

Separately, and more relevant to this desk, nearly 700 AI agents coordinated inside a Hugging Face compromise this week. Over 100 firms signed OpenAI and Anthropic's rogue-AI defence pact to cover exactly this kind of autonomous-swarm incident, but the pact came after the breach, not before it. A platform security lead now has to decide by Monday whether agent-to-agent orchestration on shared model infrastructure gets the same isolation review as human API keys; this incident shows it does not get that review today. Layer on CISA's Saturday deadline to patch the actively exploited Citrix NetScaler flaw and Manchester Airports Group's 8.7 million-record breach, and the pattern is a cyber desk that spent the week discovering its perimeter assumptions were wrong in three different directions at once. Jackson Hole opens today with Fed regional presidents already breaking from new chair Warsh on inflation, a rare public split this early in a chair's tenure. Nvidia's 70 percent guide from Tuesday has drawn no public challenge from a rival chipmaker or analyst since.

Taiwan's legislature approved a roughly $7.56 billion drone procurement plan on Thursday, expanding the unmanned arsenal the same week China deployed its largest recorded fleet of coast guard and research vessels around the island. The Ministry of National Defense's procurement office, not the Presidential Office, now carries the execution risk: the plan calls for rapid fielding of loitering munitions and reconnaissance drones on a timeline the ministry has not yet matched with a domestic production contract, which means Taipei is buying capability it must still source.

The PBOC's balance sheet does not touch this transaction, or, more precisely, it touches it only at one remove: Beijing's coast guard expansion is funded through the Ministry of Finance's central budget, not through any instrument the foreign currency desk would flag, so the fleet count is a Ministry of National Defense signal read in Taipei's Legislative Yuan, not a monetary one. The Legislative Yuan's opposition caucus, which controls procedural leverage over the defense budget, allowed this appropriation through while it has stalled prior asymmetric-warfare line items; the next test is whether that same caucus funds the production contracts the drone plan assumes, a decision the budget calendar puts before year end.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 28 JET's Last Shot Set a Fusion Record on Its Way to the Scrapyard

The UK's Joint European Torus fired its final experiment before decommissioning and set a fusion energy record on the way out: 0. 2 milligrams of deuterium-tritium fuel, less mass than a grain of sand, held together in a hot plasma for 5.

Continue Reading →

The UK's Joint European Torus fired its final experiment before decommissioning and set a fusion energy record on the way out: 0.2 milligrams of deuterium-tritium fuel, less mass than a grain of sand, held together in a hot plasma for 5.2 seconds and released 69 megajoules. That is the most energy JET produced in four decades of operation, and it came from a machine already being dismantled. JET works by bending plasma into a ring inside a doughnut-shaped magnetic cage (a tokamak) so the charged fuel never touches the vessel wall, which would cool it below the temperature fusion needs. Deuterium and tritium nuclei are heavy hydrogen isotopes; fusing them releases energy because the resulting helium nucleus weighs slightly less than the two isotopes combined, and that missing mass comes out as heat. The 69 megajoules is roughly enough to run a household kettle for a week, which sounds unimpressive until you remember the fuel that produced it weighed less than a fifth of a milligram.

The number matters because it is a data point ITER needs and can no longer generate for itself: JET was the closest existing machine to ITER's size and magnetic field strength, so its final shots are the last real-world check on how a deuterium-tritium plasma of that scale behaves before ITER, the international tokamak under construction in southern France, attempts its own first plasma. JET's operator, the UK Atomic Energy Authority, is now moving into the machine's teardown phase at Culham, recovering tritium and cataloguing components for reuse in future reactors. The record stands until ITER runs its own deuterium-tritium campaign, currently not expected before the early 2030s.

Jonathan Kuminga is a Minnesota Timberwolf, which means the Warriors just finished a two-year negotiation by losing the argument to the player. Golden State spent the 2025 offseason trying to sign-and-trade him on their terms; Kuminga spent it reminding them he had other terms. He landed with a Timberwolves team one Anthony Edwards max extension away from being built entirely out of players who make opposing coaches sweat, and he did it without ever taking Golden State's version of the deal. For a franchise that has spent a decade telling restricted free agents what their leverage should be worth, watching a 23-year-old walk on his own valuation is the actual headline.

Golden State drafted Kuminga seventh in 2021 hoping he would grow into a third star next to Stephen Curry and Draymond Green. He never signed the extension that would have locked him into that plan at their price, and restricted free agency this summer became a staring contest the front office lost. Minnesota gets a 23-year-old former lottery pick on a roster still built around Edwards's below-market rookie extension, the same team-friendly math that let the Wolves outbid a bigger market without blinking. The Warriors, meanwhile, now have a Kuminga-sized hole in the frontcourt and a full offseason to explain why the plan changed twice.
