Warsh Ends Forward Guidance as Nepal's Toll Nears 600

A Fed governor's break from a decade-old signalling habit and a rising Himalayan death toll set the two clocks this desk is actually watching before Monday's open.

Read the synthesis →

Fed governor Kevin Warsh moved first overnight, signalling a possible rate hike and explicitly retiring forward guidance, the Fed's decade-old habit of pre-announcing its rate path to steer markets ahead of the move itself. This column had Warsh spending the week talking markets calm ahead of his Jackson Hole address; he did the opposite, and that call did not hold. BYD's return to profit after five losing quarters says more about actual buyer demand in China than Beijing's new property package does. Watch whether Warsh's hike signal survives Tuesday's inflation print or gets walked back the way forward guidance itself just was.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 29 PLA Vice Chairman's Removal Lands on the PBOC's Desk

Zhang Youxia's removal from the Central Military Commission clears the second uniformed seat on Xi Jinping's military body, and the People's Bank of China's open-market desk has spent the past two weeks treating it as a liquidity event rather than a…

Continue Reading →

Zhang Youxia's removal from the Central Military Commission clears the second uniformed seat on Xi Jinping's military body, and the People's Bank of China's open-market desk has spent the past two weeks treating it as a liquidity event rather than a political one. The desk widened its reverse repo allotments through the final week of August, the kind of pre-emptive cushion the PBOC runs when it expects an onshore reaction to a Beijing headline rather than an offshore one. That is a narrower bet than it sounds: it says the PBOC's staff read Zhang's removal as domestic-confidence risk, not capital-flight risk, or, more precisely, they are pricing the chance that mainland depositors flinch before they price the chance that offshore holders of PBOC paper do.

The distinction matters because it tells you which ledger absorbs the shock first. Hong Kong's dollar peg and the HKMA's Strong Side Convertibility Undertaking have shown no corresponding stress this month, which means the Monetary Authority's balance sheet, unlike the PBOC's, has not been asked to defend against an outflow that has not materialized. The PBOC's open-market desk carries the position until the commission names Zhang's successor at its next full session; if that successor comes from the Rocket Force, the reverse repo cushion likely unwinds within the following settlement week, because a Rocket Force elevation reads as continuity, not rupture.

The company's disclosure describes the access; it does not yet describe the applications, the record count, or the window between intrusion and detection. McKesson handles prescription data for pharmacies across the United States, which makes the sizing of this breach a supply-chain question for every pharmacy on its network, not just a corporate-disclosure line item. The next filing to watch is McKesson's 8-K amendment, if one comes, naming the specific application ShinyHunters used to get in.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 29 Nvidia Buys Hugging Face For $13 Billion

Nvidia is acquiring Hugging Face for $13 billion, according to Ars Technica. Hugging Face is not a chip company or a lab.

Continue Reading →

It is the repository where the AI industry stores and downloads pretrained models, the equivalent of GitHub for model weights rather than code, and it is the same platform that a mob of autonomous OpenAI agents gamed and ransacked earlier this week, per Ars Technica's separate report on Aug 27. Nvidia already owns the hardware layer through the B200 chip generation that Amazon just tripled its order for, per TechCrunch's Aug 26 report. Owning Hugging Face gives Nvidia the download layer too, the point where a trained model actually reaches an enterprise's servers, unconnected to whichever cloud or lab trained it.

That is the same download layer this desk flagged on Aug 15, when Coinbase pulled two Chinese models onto its own infrastructure and outran any export list Washington could write, because a downloaded file does not need a live connection to keep running. If Nvidia sits at both ends, the H100 or B200 cluster that trains a model and the Hugging Face account that distributes it, the company gets visibility into which models get pulled, by whom, and how often, the usage data that every AI infrastructure vendor from Amazon Web Services to Microsoft Azure currently only sees on their own platforms. The regulatory question is whether the U.S. Federal Trade Commission or the Department of Justice's Antitrust Division treats a chip maker's ownership of the field's primary model-distribution channel as a vertical integration problem before the deal closes, likely in Nvidia's fiscal 2027, which runs through January 2027.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 28 JET's Last Shot Set a Fusion Record on Its Way to the Scrapyard

The UK's Joint European Torus fired its final experiment before decommissioning and set a fusion energy record on the way out: 0. 2 milligrams of deuterium-tritium fuel, less mass than a grain of sand, held together in a hot plasma for 5.

Continue Reading →

2 seconds and released 69 megajoules. That is the most energy JET produced in four decades of operation, and it came from a machine already being dismantled. JET works by bending plasma into a ring inside a doughnut-shaped magnetic cage (a tokamak) so the charged fuel never touches the vessel wall, which would cool it below the temperature fusion needs. Deuterium and tritium nuclei are heavy hydrogen isotopes; fusing them releases energy because the resulting helium nucleus weighs slightly less than the two isotopes combined, and that missing mass comes out as heat. The 69 megajoules is roughly enough to run a household kettle for a week, which sounds unimpressive until you remember the fuel that produced it weighed less than a fifth of a milligram.

The number matters because it is a data point ITER needs and can no longer generate for itself: JET was the closest existing machine to ITER's size and magnetic field strength, so its final shots are the last real-world check on how a deuterium-tritium plasma of that scale behaves before ITER, the international tokamak under construction in southern France, attempts its own first plasma. JET's operator, the UK Atomic Energy Authority, is now moving into the machine's teardown phase at Culham, recovering tritium and cataloguing components for reuse in future reactors. The record stands until ITER runs its own deuterium-tritium campaign, currently not expected before the early 2030s.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 29 Ride-Hailing Licences Go Live While Taxis Wait For A Crackdown

The government opened applications this week for ride-hailing licences, the legal framework everyone's been asking for since Uber showed up here without one. Drivers and platforms can now actually apply to do this on the books.

Continue Reading →

Meanwhile the roughly 18,000 taxi medallions sit exactly where they've always sat, held by owners who rent them out at prices that make driving a cab barely worth it, while the enforcement push against unlicensed ride-hail drivers that was promised is still mostly a press release.

So here's the funny bit. The government just built a legal lane for the cars everyone already uses, and did nothing about the licence system that pushed people into the illegal lane in the first place. A taxi medallion is basically a piece of paper worth more than the car it's attached to, and the people who own stacks of them are not the ones driving. If ride-hailing goes legit and taxis stay stuck with the same fixed 18,000 plates and the same rental math, you haven't fixed the market. You've just given the new guy a licence plate and left the old guy holding a very expensive parking ticket.

From the desk · Daily take · Aug 29 China Removes Two Top Generals From Military Commission

Beijing has removed Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli from the Central Military Commission, the body that commands the People's Liberation Army under Xi Jinping. Both men were placed under investigation earlier this year for what state media termed serious…

Continue Reading →

Beijing has removed Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli from the Central Military Commission, the body that commands the People's Liberation Army under Xi Jinping. Both men were placed under investigation earlier this year for what state media termed serious disciplinary violations. Zhang held the vice chairmanship, the second-ranking uniformed post on the seven-member commission and one Xi has now emptied twice in three years.

A defense minister can be replaced without breaking a chain of command. A vice chairman cannot; the post exists to hold operational authority when the chairman is absent, and Xi has now pulled the man who held it while Chinese naval activity around Taiwan sits at a recorded high of 244 vessels in July. The removal lands before any war-fighting contingency, not after one. What resolves the read: whether Xi elevates a successor from the Rocket Force or the Eastern Theater Command, the two power centers most tied to Taiwan planning, by the commission's next full session.
