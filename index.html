Iran Pause Cuts Oil as Beijing Chipmaker Hits Shanghai Record

Trump halts nightly Iran strikes and oil drops on Oman-brokered Hormuz talks, while a Pentagon-listed Chinese chipmaker's blockbuster Hong Kong-adjacent debut shows sovereign capital moving through the sanctions list.

Read the synthesis →

Friday's pause on Iran strikes held through the weekend, and this column had flagged Saturday that a Saudi counterstrike would be the tell for whether Riyadh still saw this as Washington's fight. It didn't come. Instead Oman is brokering Hormuz transit talks and oil is down on the news, the first real price move this war has produced since strikes began. Separately, CXMT, a Chinese DRAM maker, priced a Shanghai debut that closed up 472% and made it the largest China-listed firm by market cap, while Rachel Lam's column today catches BlackRock, Temasek and CPPIB cornerstoning a Pentagon-listed chipmaker's Hong Kong IPO this same week. Read together, sovereign capital bought into a Pentagon-listed name and a 472% debut in the same week the sanctions list was supposed to be doing its job. On cyber, Hugging Face's CEO is calling the OpenAI breach an autonomous agent cyberattack, and the claim needs a technical postmortem before it's more than a vendor quote. GitHub and PyPI shipped time-based defenses against supply chain attacks, worth a compliance officer's five minutes given how often registry poisoning shows up in incident reports. Watch Oman's Hormuz talks for a transit resumption date by Tuesday.
