Trump Sent Troops And Offered Russia Fuel Business

Washington committed more forces to the Middle East and offered Russia renewed energy business as Taiwan awaited weapons, Ukraine sought European funding and Hong Kong shares fell.

The Aircraft Arrive

Taiwan received its first new F16V fighters on October 2, seven years after Washington approved the sale of 66 aircraft. The aircraft came from the 2019 order; Washington still had another weapons decision to make. That same day, a US official said roughly 9,000 American troops were being sent aboard ships to the Middle East. By October 3, reporting described a third aircraft carrier joining the deployment.

On October 1, US officials had judged a Chinese invasion of Taiwan before 2028 increasingly unlikely. Taiwan’s defence ministry counted 23 Chinese military aircraft, seven naval vessels and seven official ships around the island that day.

Vincent Lai reported on October 1 that sources described a slowdown in American exports of aircraft parts to China while trade negotiations continued. Washington had announced no measure governing the slowdown. Chinese carriers awaiting components for scheduled maintenance faced reported paperwork delays, with no published rule to establish whether a particular shipment would clear.

Washington had still to decide on the interceptor package when Taiwan’s envoy publicly pressed for it on October 4. On October 5, SCMP reported that American defence experts were working inside Taiwan’s military headquarters under a pact.

Vincent’s October 2 report put roughly 9,000 troops aboard ships but supplied no cost for the deployment. The Navy would have to sustain the vessels and everyone and everything aboard for as long as the mission continued. No corresponding account established which other expenditure would be deferred or increased. Washington had committed the troops while leaving the interceptor decision outstanding, though no documented transfer of Taiwan’s weapons allocation to the Middle East linked the two.

Reuters reported on October 3 that satellite imagery showed China accelerating runway work on Antelope Reef in the Paracel Islands, which Vietnam and Taiwan also claim.

On October 8, a patrol reported finding a Chinese maritime militia vessel off Taiwan’s east coast for the first time. It was on the Pacific side of the island, facing possible American and Japanese reinforcement routes. A crew assessing the approaching vessel had to weigh its civilian registration against its reported militia role. The vessel was in waters through which outside forces might arrive, although the sighting alone did not establish a mission to obstruct them.

By October 12, Taiwan was monitoring Chinese live fire drills, and reporting described a military manpower shortage undermining its ability to fight. Its navy was preparing responses to maritime militia while serving crews assessed vessels and exercises. Taiwan said on October 13 that commercial shipping remained safe.

Taiwan separately counted nine naval vessels and seven official ships on October 17.

Oil Beyond Hormuz

Gulf exporters were moving cargoes by routes that avoided the closed strait. On October 3, Vincent reported that oil exports had recovered close to their volumes before the Iran war through shuttle tankers and bypass pipelines, with American military assistance. His account supplied neither a daily barrel count nor the names of operators.

Iran said on October 5 that the war had no military solution. Talks were stalled and Hormuz remained shut.

Maarten Bakker reported on October 5 that the US Air Force had pulled bombers from RAF Fairford over an Iran plot threat. On October 6, the Pentagon confirmed that B1 bombers had left because of an unspecified threat.

The New York Times reported that evening that Iran’s Revolutionary Guard had ordered a possible drone attack on the bombers at Fairford. British police arrested a seventh man in connection with the security incident on October 7. The arrests did not establish that anyone had carried out the reported attack plan; the aircraft had left before the seventh arrest.

The Pentagon left the threat unspecified; the New York Times described a possible drone attack ordered by the Revolutionary Guard. Neither account reported a completed attack on the base.

On October 11, a missile struck the terminal at Riyadh airport, killing 12 people and wounding more than 300. Maarten’s evening report described the deadliest attack on a Gulf state since the Iran war began. Washington was offering Tehran talks while sending troops.

Three days later, Charmaine reported that a Houthi missile had hit Saudi Aramco headquarters. Neither report supplied a new daily export total.

The United States released its September producer price figures on October 16. Prices had recorded their largest monthly rise since 2022, with rising energy costs spreading through the economy even as oil continued moving. The figures measured prices before the October strikes on Riyadh airport and Saudi Aramco headquarters. Those September costs were part of the economic pressure facing Washington when Trump announced the Russian diesel imports on October 10. The figures documenting the increase came out six days after his announcement.

Iran reported on October 23 that seven tankers had been hit in Hormuz. Charmaine’s morning edition recorded higher energy prices and falling technology shares alongside renewed fears of conflict.

Washington ordered diplomatic departures on October 24 following warnings of Iranian attacks. Maarten reported the reduction in the American diplomatic presence three weeks after Washington committed roughly 9,000 troops.

Russian Fuel And Kyiv

Kyiv had one open bridge out of six on October 3, after Russian strikes and city closures. People crossing the Dnipro had to share that remaining route with the city’s other traffic. Zelenskyy promised harder attacks on Russian oil refineries.

A day earlier, Ukraine’s military had estimated that 46,230 Russian soldiers were killed or wounded in September, its highest monthly count of 2026. Separate media reports put the planned increase in Russia’s 2027 military spending at 27 percent.

Trump announced on October 10 that the United States would import Russian diesel before the midterm elections. Fuel prices were high, and Americans would vote the following month. Maarten reported the proposed American purchase, with no completed shipment or import volume accompanying the announcement.

On October 11, Trump also called for Ukraine to replace Zelenskyy. Charmaine reported the demand the morning after the diesel announcement. Zelenskyy had promised harder attacks on Russian refineries. Now the American president was calling for his replacement while offering a new destination for Russian fuel. No change of Ukrainian leadership was reported following the demand.

Ukraine continued striking Russian infrastructure. On October 12, Charmaine reported that it had shut down a third Yandex data centre in four days. Ukraine was expanding its attacks into Russian computing infrastructure while the proposed American diesel purchase remained a prospective commercial arrangement. Trump’s announcement had brought neither a reported ceasefire nor an end to Ukraine’s attacks.

Zelenskyy sought another $79 billion from Europe on October 17, amid deteriorating relations with Washington and uncertainty over continuing American operational support. He was asking for money; no approval or transfer was reported. European governments faced a request for a larger financial commitment while Ukraine still depended on forms of American assistance that a larger European appropriation could not itself supply.

That evening, Maarten reported that Trump had temporarily waived sanctions on Russia. The waiver came a week after the October 10 diesel announcement, on the same day as Zelenskyy’s European appeal. Trump had granted temporary relief, leaving permanent sanctions removal unresolved. Neither report established the value of completed Russian sales.

Washington proposed increased Russian gas sales to Europe on October 18 as part of a proposed Ukraine peace agreement. Charmaine described skeptical European governments being asked to accept the expansion. Those governments had received Ukraine’s request for another $79 billion the previous day.

The available reports established neither European agreement to the additional purchases nor delivery of the full Ukrainian funding request.

Hong Kong Buyers

Rachel Lam examined reports on October 4 of US$47.5 billion in third quarter share sales, HK$388 billion in nine months of IPO proceeds and US$92 billion in IPO fundraising. The reports did not establish a common basis for comparing those totals.

The Securities and Futures Commission suspended Baicha trading during action against suspected IPO rigging syndicates. An existing shareholder could no longer sell through the exchange, whether or not that person had any connection to the suspected activity. Rachel followed the suspension alongside the SFC’s separate circular to sponsors and the firms handling bookbuilding and placements. Those announcements established no finding against Baicha’s sponsors or placing firms. Her October reporting gave shareholders no date for trading to restart.

Jiangsu Slender West Lake Culture and Tourism announced on October 21 that it was postponing its planned October 23 listing, citing factors including market conditions. Investors considering the offering received the postponement two days before the intended debut.

One month HIBOR fixed at 2.68 percent on October 21, down 21 basis points since September 8. The aggregate balance had moved from HK$54.0 billion to HK$54.1 billion. Rachel also followed renminbi sovereign bonds issued through Hong Kong’s Central Moneymarkets Unit on October 20.

On October 22, one month HIBOR stood at 2.75 percent, and the Hang Seng fell 3 percent to 22,935.

Maarten’s October 22 evening report described Chinese regulators summoning ecommerce operators, deepening the technology selloff. Weak Chinese consumption and doubts about returns on artificial intelligence had already weakened confidence. Alibaba shares fell amid the regulatory pressure. The Hang Seng’s 3 percent decline preceded Iran’s October 23 report of attacks on seven tankers.

On October 23, energy prices rose and technology shares fell again alongside the reported tanker attacks.

State investors helped Chinese stocks reverse losses later on October 23. Maarten reported the recovery amid a wider Asian technology selloff.

Rachel reported on October 27 that dedicated repo capacity had risen 10 percent to RMB22 billion across twelve designated banks. The expansion followed the HKMA’s designation of primary liquidity providers and announcement of broader support on October 23.

The Ministry of Finance received applications of RMB13.684 billion for RMB4 billion issued when it reopened a two year sovereign renminbi bond on October 23. Investors were applying for more than the amount on offer.

Rachel’s October 28 filing counted eight of fourteen Hong Kong listings since September that had fallen below their offer prices on debut. They included Medcaptain Medical Technology, whose shares fell nearly 43 percent. She also returned to Slender West Lake’s postponed offering: market conditions had entered the issuer’s decision before investors could buy or sell its shares on the exchange.

Medcaptain’s offering still counted towards the capital raised. A buyer who paid the offer price held shares that fell nearly 43 percent on debut.

The Desks, In Their Own WordsWhat each correspondent noticed this month, and what stayed quiet

Mei Chen, Geopolitical Desk. On October 17, Taiwan counted nine naval vessels and seven official ships separately. The second column established presence without establishing coordination, leaving the receiving staff to determine what those seven ships added to the deployment. Taiwan said commercial shipping remained safe on October 13 while its military continued monitoring Chinese live fire drills.

Vincent Lai, Geopolitical Desk. Manila's word 'unsafe' and Beijing's word 'airspace' put the same intercept before different institutional tests. Neither ministry had to answer the other's complaint for its own account to remain useful. The approaching conclusion of ASEAN talks produced no recorded agreement in my filings that settled how Philippine and Chinese crews would handle their next encounter.

Rachel Lam, Finance & Risk Desk. The extra RMB2 billion in the repo announcement belonged to the whole twelve bank scheme, not to each designated bank. The capacity increase also supplied something the falling HIBOR figures could not: funding capacity denominated in renminbi. No Baicha trading restart date appeared in my October filings.

Kai Tanner, Cyber Intel Desk. AOMEI's malicious update could occupy the correct vendor address for only five to ten minutes, then disappear before an administrator checked it. A later clean file was evidence about that later moment, not the download already installed. The feared exploitation of every disclosed flaw did not materialise in the filings: PHP had no established exploitation, and Brotli had no reported exploitation outside testing.

Aya Nakamura, AI Desk. The Kimi task bill and Anthropic margin exposed the same accounting trap at different scales. More generated output could absorb a token price cut, while marketplace fees could remain an expense without reducing reported gross margin. Lower token prices did not produce a lower measured Kimi task bill in the cited Intelligence Index evaluation.

Cheung Kwok-keung, HK Desk. The three Blockchain Development Company employees had passed two payment deadlines: the original wage deadline and the deadline for paying their tribunal award. Winning had given them another amount owed before it gave them money. The Wang Fuk Court findings did not bring completed individual disciplinary cases by the end of October.

Magnus Honeyfield, Science and Health Desk. IQM's more reliable operation could still damage the calculation by leaving other qubits waiting long enough to lose their state. The relevant error budget included the time spent doing nothing. The month ended without IQM's entanglement of all 54 Emerald qubits establishing a computing advantage.

Sora Whitlam, Science and Health Desk. The two muscle stories failed at different steps: myostatin inhibitors added or preserved tissue without corresponding strength, while tadalafil kept precursor cells alive but slowed their development into mature muscle. Neither result established more force from the finished muscle. Neither muscle filing established a strength benefit in aging people.

Dev Chatterjee, Sports Desk. Contreras overturned ten calls in 13 World Series challenges while batting .381. The catcher could now help his club by getting an umpire's recorded decision changed, as well as by hitting or catching the pitch. The surgery suggested by concern over Shohei Ohtani's knee and biceps did not follow: the October 22 filing reported that he needed none.

Joy Lee, Entertainment. Bleecker Street could book nearly 500 screens without being able to buy TikTok invitations to them. Cliff Booth ended the month with the reverse problem: famous names and a release announcement could not make Regal and Cinemark supply the room. The October filings never supplied the later ticket sales that would establish whether the Holmes documentary kept its enlarged estate of 2,600 theaters full.
