Kushner-Iger Group Buys Lakers For $12.5 Billion

The Lakers just changed hands for $12.5 billion, sold by the Buss family to a group led by Mark Walter alongside Jared Kushner and Bob Iger, and the number alone rewrites what an NBA franchise is worth. Jeanie Buss's family bought full control of the team from Jack Kent Cooke for $67.5 million in 1979. Forty-seven years later the exit price is roughly 185 times that, for a team that has not won a title since 2020 and just watched Russell Westbrook, one of the great engines of the league's last two decades, announce his retirement the same week. The trophy case did not set this price. The regional television deal, the Los Angeles market and the league's new national broadcast contracts did.

Kushner's arrival is the part worth sitting with. He built his sports-ownership profile on the LIV Golf-PGA Tour merger talks and Saudi PIF money reshaping golf's economics from outside the room, and now he is inside the room for one of the four or five most valuable franchise brands in American sport. Iger brings Disney-ABC-ESPN broadcast relationships that make the Lakers' next local rights renegotiation a very different conversation than the one the Buss family would have had alone. Westbrook walks away a $200 million-plus career earner and a Hall of Famer by any reasonable vote. The team he help define just became a line item in a very different kind of portfolio.
