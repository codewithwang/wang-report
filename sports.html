Norris Takes Pole in Belgium as Hamilton Struggles

Lando Norris grabbed pole position for the Belgian Grand Prix on Saturday, out-qualifying teammate Oscar Piastri and putting McLaren back on top of the timesheet at Spa-Francorchamps, a circuit that has decided more championships by attrition and weather than any other stop on the calendar. Lewis Hamilton, seven years removed from his last win at Spa, could not get his Ferrari higher than the midfield, the kind of Saturday that turns a Sunday into an audit of what a nine-time Belgian Grand Prix winner is still doing in a car that will not cooperate.

Spa does not reward the fastest lap so much as the driver who survives it, four kilometers of elevation change and weather microclimates that can turn a comfortable pole into a wasted Saturday by lap one on Sunday. Norris starts up front in a title fight that is his to lose against his own teammate as much as anyone else's. Hamilton starts from the pack in a Ferrari seat that costs the team a rumored nine figures a year to keep him in, and at some point the invoice and the results are supposed to match. Sunday's race is the next chance for that math to work.
