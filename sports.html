LeBron's Sixers Deal Puts Klay Trade Chatter on Ice

LeBron James is going to Philadelphia, and the league's Tuesday afternoon was too busy processing that to finish anything else. The Klay Thompson rumors that had circulated out of Dallas all week (the Mavericks reportedly floating the future Hall of Famer in three different shapes of trade) stalled the moment the Sixers news broke, because no front office wants to price a role-player deal into a market that just moved. Vita Vea telling the Buccaneers to trade him landed the same day and got maybe four minutes of oxygen. That is what a superstar signing does to a Tuesday. It does not just make news, it evicts it.

Here is the part nobody puts on the jersey: the 76ers now owe LeBron's cap number and Joel Embiid's supermax in the same room, on a roster that has not escaped the second round since 2001. Philadelphia is betting a year of James's dwindling shot creation buys enough noise to keep season-ticket renewals moving while a real rebuild waits its turn. Dallas, meanwhile, is stuck holding Klay at ticket-department value with no trade partner willing to negotiate this week. The Mavericks front office picked a bad Tuesday to shop a 36-year-old shooter. The market did not close. It just went quiet on them specifically.
