John Niland's Death Reframes the Cowboys' Own Roster Machine

John Niland played nine seasons at guard for the Cowboys, made six straight Pro Bowls from 1968 to 1973, and started on the team's first Super Bowl roster. He died Sunday at 82, one day before Dallas beat Seattle 17-7 in preseason with a defense that forced three turnovers and a debut from undrafted receiver Camden Brown that outplayed half the depth chart Niland's generation would not recognize. The Cowboys of 2026 carry a salary cap north of $290 million leaguewide, a number that did not exist when Niland signed his last contract, and a roster now built less by scouts than by a front office pricing every guard, corner and slot receiver against a cap sheet updated in real time.

That is the actual distance between Niland's Cowboys and Sunday's: not the win total, the mechanism. Camden Brown made a preseason roster fight look like theater because that is what it is now, a low-cost audition inside a system engineered to find $800,000 contributors before someone else does. Niland played on guaranteed money that guaranteed almost nothing and retired to open a marketing business, the standard offramp for a Pro Bowl lineman in 1975. Dallas will spend this week burying one Cowboy and roster-cutting several dozen others by a Tuesday deadline in September, and the franchise will not confuse the two.
