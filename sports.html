Jerry Jones Signals He Would Sell the Cowboys

Jerry Jones told reporters this week he would be open to selling his stake in the Dallas Cowboys, the first time in thirty-seven years of ownership he has said the sentence out loud. Jones bought the team from Bum Bright on February 25, 1989, for $140 million. Forbes now values the Cowboys at $13 billion, the highest figure in the NFL, which means the man who once mortgaged nearly everything he owned to buy a football team is sitting on a return of roughly 9,185 percent. Mark Cuban's name is already attached to the speculation about who writes the next check.

Here is the arithmetic nobody needs a calculator for: that $13 billion valuation was built almost entirely without a Super Bowl since January 1996, which tells you the Cowboys stopped being a football team and became a media company that occasionally plays football on the side. Jones did that on purpose, selling out AT&T Stadium suites and Cowboys Network rights while the roster went three decades between championships. A sale, if it happens, values the brand recognition over the banner count, and the next owner inherits a fan base that has made peace with exactly that trade.
