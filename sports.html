Kyrie Irving's Mavericks Future Splits Dallas Front Office

Dallas has spent the offseason failing to trade Kyrie Irving, and that failure is now the story. Irving is 34, coming off a torn ACL, and owed roughly $44 million this season on an expiring deal, the kind of contract that makes him either an expensive rental for a contender or a very expensive reason a rebuild stalls. The Mavericks, still explaining to their own fanbase why Luka Doncic is in Los Angeles, have reportedly resisted offers rather than chased them, which is its own answer about what General Manager Nico Harrison thinks this roster still is.

That reluctance is the real signal. A team fully committed to rebuilding trades the expiring star for picks and cap flexibility without blinking. A team that hesitates is a team still hoping Irving, Anthony Davis and whatever is left of this roster can steal a play-in spot before his contract runs out in June 2027. Golden State's interest and Miami's checking in on the periphery only sharpen the point: other front offices see an expiring asset. Dallas, for now, sees a team. Kyrie plays the season out and the answer arrives at the February 2027 trade deadline, not before.
