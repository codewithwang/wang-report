Buss Family Sells Los Angeles Lakers, Ending 46-Year Dynasty

The Buss family is selling the Los Angeles Lakers, and Jeanie Buss is out as governor, closing the book on 46 years since Jerry Buss bought the franchise for $67.5 million in 1979 and turned it into the most valuable property in American sports. The deal, reported by ESPN and CBS Sports, ends a run that included eleven championships and a Magic-to-Kobe-to-LeBron pipeline no other franchise has matched, and it comes at a valuation that makes the elder Buss's original purchase price look like he found the team in a couch cushion. Sports team ownership has quietly become the best-performing asset class of the last decade, and the Lakers just proved it in the most literal way possible: a family sold the thing that made them famous because the number on the table was too large to argue with.

Every dynasty ends with somebody doing math, and the Busses did theirs. What happens next is the part worth watching: whoever signs the check inherits a roster mid-rebuild, a regional television deal in flux across the league, and a fan base in Los Angeles that has never once accepted "patience" as a plan. The NBA's board of governors still has to approve the sale, and that vote, not the trophy case, is where the next Lakers era actually begins.
