Kyrie Irving Trade Talk Reshapes Three NBA Rosters

Dallas floated Kyrie Irving into a three-team framework this week that would send him to the Knicks, and the mechanics matter more than the name. Irving is 34, coming off the second ACL tear of his career, and Dallas is the team offering him up rather than fielding calls, which tells you where his value sits after Klay Thompson's buyout left the Mavericks scrambling for anyone who can shoot from twenty-three feet. New York, still hunting the perimeter shooting that has haunted its last two playoff runs, is willing to bet on the rehab. Miami didn't wait on a hypothetical: the Heat beat the Lakers outright for Klay, guaranteeing $298 million in the four-year deal, which means two different franchises just wrote two very different prices for what "Klay Thompson in 2026" is worth.

Follow that split screen to the number underneath it. The Warriors let their own championship banner-hanger walk to Miami rather than match, the clearest signal yet that Golden State's roster math is now built entirely around Steph Curry's remaining window and nobody else's sentimental value. Dallas, meanwhile, is auditioning Tarik Biberovic as the emergency three-point answer while trying to offload Irving before his body answers the question for them. The Knicks' front office has final say on whether the third team's centerpiece even clears a physical, and that call, not the Vegas odds on the trade, is the one due first.
