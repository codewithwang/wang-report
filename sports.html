Josh Kushner Buys Into Lakers Ownership Group

Josh Kushner, the Thrive Capital founder who has spent a decade making the Lakers logo look like the safest bet in his portfolio (Peloton and Instacart notwithstanding), is now an actual owner of the team, joining the ownership group that valued the franchise at $10 billion when Mark Walter's group took control last year. The Lakers were a Buss family operation for 46 years, run on Jerry Buss's original 1979 gamble and Jeanie Buss's refusal to sell to the highest bidder until the number got genuinely absurd. Now the cap table reads like a Thrive portfolio review, and the purple-and-gold has become the league's most liquid trophy asset.

Here's the part that actually matters for the other 29 teams watching: private equity is no longer knocking on the NBA's door, it's sitting at the table making decisions, and the league's 2021 rule change letting institutional capital own up to 20 percent of a franchise looks less like a safety valve and more like the on-ramp everyone is now driving down. Kushner didn't buy a team. He bought a seat at the table that sets the salary cap math for the other 29 owners. The Lakers used to be a family business. Now they're a line item.
