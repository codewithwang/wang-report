Iran Pause Holds a Week, Houthis Now Run the Escalation

The ceasefire this column doubted last Friday is still standing, but the Red Sea has become the live front while Washington and Beijing quietly widened the AI credit story overnight.
Walter Wang · WR Editor-in-Chief · Hong Kong

The Iran pause held through a full week, and Tehran's own statement that it will keep halting strikes as long as Washington does is the strongest evidence yet that this column's Friday skepticism was too flat. But the escalation moved sideways again. Iran is now blaming Ukraine for a Caspian Sea vessel strike that killed a sailor, folding two separate wars into one narrative, and a supertanker rerouted through Suez rather than risk the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the Red Sea chokepoint the Houthis have threatened since their blockade claim. One ship's routing decision says more about shipowner confidence than any statement out of Sanaa. Away from the Gulf, the FT's read on Big Tech's AI-funded borrowing spree deserves the lead column today: credit risk is stacking up faster than the capex it funds, sharpened by Nvidia's five billion dollar bet on Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence overnight. A Hong Kong-listed chipmaker's 470 percent debut shows investors demanding no premium for that risk. Watch OpenAI's Hugging Face breach and the Ernst & Young ShinyHunters claim: supply chains are now the shared attack surface for both AI labs and Big Four firms alike.
