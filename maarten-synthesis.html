A Property Tycoon Gets Life While Whales Move Into Greenland

China closed the book on its biggest real estate collapse today, the Gulf cut trade with Iran, and somewhere north of everything a cancer vaccine actually worked.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Hui Ka Yan built Evergrande into an empire large enough to take an economy down with it. He was sentenced to life in prison today. The UAE suspended trade with Iran after renewed missile fire, a sharp escalation in Gulf-Iran tensions. Pyongyang answered Washington's talk of a Kim summit with short-range missiles. Away from the standoffs, Moderna's experimental cancer vaccine passed a real trial, extending remission on a day crowded with tanks and tycoons. Off Greenland, giant whales moved into waters the ice used to hold, which is either a migration story or a slow obituary for the Arctic. Hui's sentence closes China's biggest corporate collapse of the decade. The missiles and the trade freeze close nothing. Watch whether Abu Dhabi's move hardens into a wider Gulf pullback, and whether Pyongyang's answer to an olive branch was the whole message or just the opening line.
