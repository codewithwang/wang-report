Iran Gets an Economic D-Day While a Robot Breaks a Human Record Twice

Washington rolled out sanctions dressed as military history, Ottawa learned what fifty percent tariffs actually cost, and a Chinese robot company quietly kept winning the race nobody in government seems to be running.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Washington named its Iran sanctions package Operation Economic Outcast, which is the kind of branding exercise that tells you the policy itself needed help. Sanctions have worked, when they work, without needing a marketing department, and the ones that need one usually do not. Ottawa got the more honest treatment: no cinematic title, just Trump threatening fifty percent tariffs on every Canadian car and truck, which Mark Carney's own businesses are already calling a downward pressure vortex, a phrase economists reach for when the numbers are too ugly for adjectives. Meanwhile TikTok closed out a four hundred million dollar settlement over child privacy, the sort of number that sounds punitive until you remember what the platform earns before lunch. Indonesia, more usefully, promised to go after the corporations actually burning the land behind its haze crisis rather than the haze itself. And Oura, the ring company, is reportedly eyeing a September IPO above sixteen billion dollars, proof that sleep tracking remains a more reliable investment thesis than most governments' trade policy. Watch whether Ottawa's tariff response survives contact with November, and whether Washington's sanctions outlast their own press release.
