Tehran Hits Back, a Botnet Falls, and Apple Renames a Lake

Iran answered American strikes with attacks on Gulf shipping and bases, Hong Kong's Sality botnet takedown was the day's quiet win, and a map company solved a border dispute by pressing a button.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

A monthlong lull was never the same thing as peace. Iran struck a Saudi tanker in the Strait of Hormuz and hit bases across the Gulf, while Washington announced it is prepared to strike Iran "anytime we want," a mood set to public rather than a policy. Two Filipino crew members died on that tanker. Away from the missiles, a joint US-European operation dismantled the Sality botnet, malware old enough in internet years to have grandchildren. Google avoided being broken up over its advertising business but must change how it runs it, which regulators will call accountability and Google will quietly call a Tuesday. Vietnam arrested a deputy health minister over bribery and medical smuggling. And Apple, under a presidential order to rename Lake Ontario for American users, simply flipped a switch and called it Lake America, an honest description of how borders work in software. Watch whether Hormuz war-risk insurance premiums rise by morning on the tanker strike, and whether Uber's Nigeria and Uganda exits draw the same regulatory attention its ad rivals just got.
