Iran War Widens, Nobody Calls It That, and the Earth Shakes Anyway

Saudi Arabia joined a war Washington will not name a war, a Kyushu mall collapsed for the second time after being rebuilt to survive exactly this, and Manchester quietly changed mayors.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Saudi Arabia bombed targets inside Iraq today, which the kingdom's defense ministry described as retaliation, and Washington resumed strikes on Iran days after the president reportedly waved off doing exactly that. Nobody involved has used the word escalation. Officials tend to reach for procedural language right at the moment the thing they are describing stops being procedural. Jordan says it shot down an Iranian missile, tankers burned off Egypt's coast, and a general in Washington is reportedly drawing up two weeks of further strikes. File de-escalation under aspirational. Away from the war desk, Japan took a 7.1 magnitude quake to Kyushu, and an Aeon shopping mall that was rebuilt after 2016 specifically to withstand earthquakes collapsed again anyway. That is either a design flaw or the most honest thing that happened all day. Crete evacuated thousands ahead of wildfires, and the Danube hit a record low. Manchester elected a new mayor to replace the one who went to run the country. Tomorrow watch whether the fourteen-day strike plan survives contact with an actual ceasefire conversation. Plans usually do not.
