Two Navies Circle Hormuz While Putin Visits an Island Japan Still Wants Back

Washington and Tehran spent the day trading claims over who actually controls the Strait of Hormuz, while a Russian president touring disputed islands reminded everyone that borders are opinions with better lawyers.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Washington told Tehran the blockade can hold indefinitely and the economic pressure will only tighten. Tehran told Washington it controls the strait regardless. The dispute is now producing two competing tanker attack claims out of the UAE. The White House is sending another carrier to back up the point. Meanwhile Vladimir Putin flew out to islands Japan lost in 1945 and never got back, and Tokyo called the visit absolutely unacceptable, a phrase that carries real diplomatic weight right up until the plane lands anyway. Nigel Farage won a by-election he called himself, against a man dressed as a bin, which tells you something true about British politics that a thousand columns could not improve on. In Washington, someone filled the World War II Memorial's fountain with dish soap, and nobody has explained why, which is somehow the most honest thing that happened in the capital today. Trump gave American companies a green light to hack back at cybercriminals, putting Washington's rulebook closer to Beijing's and Moscow's than it has ever admitted to being. Watch the carrier's transit and whether Tokyo's protest gets anything more than a shrug from Moscow.
