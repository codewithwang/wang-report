A City Pleads Guilty, a Death Toll Passes a Thousand, and Cameras Watch Everyone

Hong Kong closed one more national security case, Nepal's flood toll crossed a grim threshold, and America argued with itself about who gets to watch whom.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Nepal's flood and landslide toll passed a thousand today. The dead were mostly in remote hill districts cut off by the same landslides that killed them. Closer to home, Joshua Wong pleaded guilty in his second national security case, which in this city no longer qualifies as news so much as scheduling. In Congo, Ebola deaths passed three thousand, with no sign the outbreak is slowing. Separately, in Washington, Massachusetts Democrats spent their primary night proving that generational change is a slogan best deployed against someone else's incumbent: eighty year old Ed Markey trounced his challenger by a wide margin. A nationwide backlash against Flock's license plate cameras sat quieter down the page, a local privacy fight over a specific product that could shape what surveillance is allowed to look like in other cities if state legislatures act on it. Iran and Washington kept trading fire overseas, and Treasury yields moved on it. Watch whether Nepal's toll keeps climbing through the week, and whether any state legislature moves a bill on license plate cameras in response to the Flock backlash.
