A King Dies, a War Criminal Dies, and a Lake Gets Renamed

Nepal's death toll kept climbing, Norway lost its monarch, and Washington spent part of the day renaming a lake instead of reading the room.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Nepal's numbers moved the wrong direction all day. Hundreds are confirmed dead and nearly 1,400 are still missing. Rescuers pulled survivors from a flooded hydropower tunnel in Rasuwa even as an overflowing glacial lake threatened to make the rescue itself the next disaster. Beijing pledged emergency aid, the correct diplomatic reflex and also a convenient audition for the rail corridor safety case it has been building for years. Norway's King Harald V died at 89. The transition was handled with quiet dignity and did not trend. In The Hague, Ratko Mladic died in custody, three decades after the crimes for which he was convicted. Hong Kong's courts spent the day on the Tiananmen vigil sentencing, the defence arguing the group's stated goal was an empty slogan rather than an actual plan; the bench gave no sign of buying it. The White House, separately, moved to ban foreign-made power grid equipment over backdoor fears, a genuinely consequential call competing for attention with the news that Washington also renamed Lake Ontario to Lake America, which Ottawa called foolishness. Watch whether Nepal's missing count keeps rising through the weekend, and whether Hong Kong hands down its sentences before the news cycle moves on.
