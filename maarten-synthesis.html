Three Tankers Turn Back at Hormuz While a Robot Maker Turns a Profit

Shipping keeps hedging against a war that has not started, the UAE cut trade with Tehran outright, and in Shanghai a humanoid robot company reminded everyone money still moves.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Three Chinese supertankers U-turned in the Strait of Hormuz today, which tells you more than any statement out of Washington or Tehran: insurers and shipmasters have already turned their ships around for a war that diplomats keep insisting is not happening. The UAE went further and suspended trade with Iran outright after the missile exchanges, a decision that reads as policy but functions as a vote of no confidence. Trump said no talks were planned. Seoul confirmed Washington asked to cut short the joint war games, a request dressed up as scheduling and understood by everyone in the region as something else. Away from the strait, Hong Kong had its own small parable in Unitree, a humanoid robot maker that surged as much as 600 percent on its Shanghai debut, proof that capital will chase a good story even while the oil market is pricing in a bad one through freight and insurance rates. HKEX posted a record quarterly profit the same day. Watch whether the Hormuz standoff produces an actual incident, and whether Unitree's debut cools once the novelty does.
