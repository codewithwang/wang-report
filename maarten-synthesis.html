Iran Says No, Trump Says Soon, Hormuz Sailors Wait Regardless

A denial from Tehran collided with an insistence from Washington today, while the people actually stranded in the strait between them got no vote in either version.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Iran told the world flatly there are no talks scheduled, and the president told the world the opposite. Neither side has decided what happens next. Meanwhile the most energetic particle ever recorded turned up in a Chinese-led detector, a finding that exceeds what physics currently says should be possible and that nobody in Beijing or anywhere else has fully explained yet. Microsoft flagged Russian state hackers riding hotel Wi-Fi to spy on traveling guests, a reminder that the most dangerous network in your life is the one with a laminated password card at reception. Twenty-five states sued to block a fresh round of tariffs they call a pretext dressed up as forced-labor enforcement, a case that could become the template for how future tariff fights get resolved, in court rather than at the negotiating table. And Sheikh Hasina's plan to address a rally in India by video reopened a rift with Dhaka that a phone call apparently could not close. The sailors stuck in the Strait of Hormuz, some for three months now, are the ones actually waiting on Tehran and Washington to agree on anything. Watch whether that particle finding gets replicated, and whether Dhaka lets the video go ahead.
