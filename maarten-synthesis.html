Norway's King Dies, Mladic Dies in Custody, and the Fed Chair Won't Stop Talking

Norway buried a monarchy's old certainty, a convicted war criminal's sentence ended with his life, and Washington explained the economy to itself all day long.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Norway's King Harald V died at 89 this morning, and his son becomes King Haakon VIII by lunchtime, a clean succession most institutions cannot manage. Ratko Mladic died in a prison cell at 84. He had been serving a life sentence for genocide. Fed Chair Warsh spent the day promising markets he will crush inflation, and the two-year yield jumped in response. Moscow is telling Western officials it wants no direct war with NATO, just the hybrid kind, an arrangement that lets both sides keep calling it peace. BYD posted its first profit growth in five quarters. Beijing rolled out a property package bigger than forecast. Anthropic beat the Pentagon in court over a supply-chain label. Overhead last night, a blood moon eclipsed itself on schedule, the one thing today that behaved exactly as advertised. Watch whether Warsh's inflation pledge survives its first real economic data, and whether Hong Kong hands down its Tiananmen vigil sentences before the week runs out.
