Trump Threatens to Bomb Oman While a Naval Software Expert Just Disappears

A president floated bombing an ally over Iran while a Chinese officer vanished from a website and Anthropic hit sixty five billion dollars, and only one of those made anyone raise their voice.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Start with the sentence that should not have needed saying today: the president of the United States threatened to bomb Oman, a country that has spent decades being useful to Washington precisely by staying out of the way, because it might get in the way. That escalation used to end careers. Today it ends up as a Tuesday news cycle item, filed somewhere between the oil market update and the earnings calendar. In Beijing, a naval combat software expert's profile was removed from his academy's website. His academy has not commented, and no state media outlet has named a reason for his disappearance. Private equity made zero new China deals this cycle, a number so clean it reads like a decision rather than a drought. Anthropic's revenue run rate hit sixty five billion dollars, up eighteen billion in two months, evidence that selling the tools for the AI buildout is paying better than betting on any single company inside it. Moscow sentenced an anti-war politician to eleven years, a sentence widely read as political retaliation rather than a response to any genuine offense. Tomorrow: watch whether Muscat responds to being threatened at all, and whether Wall Street's oil jitters survive retail earnings.
