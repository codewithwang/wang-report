Ottawa Calls Washington's Bluff, Iran Says the Quiet Part Louder

Canada matched the United States tariff for tariff, Tehran said sanctions were a confession of defeat, and Hong Kong quietly proved its healthcare reform actually worked.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Mark Carney did today what most trading partners only rehearse in private: he let a deal die rather than sign a bad one, matching Washington's fifty percent tariff dollar for dollar. Canadians are largely cheering him for it. Trump, not to be outdone on the theatrical front, promised Iran an economic D-Day. Tehran responded by pointing out that a country still fighting you with sanctions is a country that already lost the fight it wanted. NATO members are now discussing Hormuz contingencies without bothering to involve NATO. Away from the shouting, the actual news of the day was quieter and better: Hong Kong's healthcare fee reform has pushed full fee waivers up twentyfold to 280,000 patients, a number that will not trend but will change lives, while the DR Congo begins an Ebola vaccine trial against an outbreak that is not waiting for anyone's approval process. India, meanwhile, is racing China and South Korea to open Arctic shipping lanes, proof that even melting ice is now a market. Canadian exporters will feel the fifty percent tariff within weeks. Kinshasa's vaccine rollout will show results, or the virus will, before the trial's next scheduled review.
