A President Threatens to Annex a Strait While a City Just Wants a Cooler Attic

Washington floated owning the Strait of Hormuz outright, Tokyo skipped a shrine visit that still triggered Beijing anyway, and Hong Kong's subdivided flats hit a heat index that would alarm a sauna.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Every so often a president says a thing so large the room has to sit with it a second before reacting, and today that thing was Donald Trump suggesting the Strait of Hormuz simply become American territory, an idea Iran dismissed as delusion, which is a generous word for it. In Tokyo, Prime Minister Takaichi skipped the Yasukuni shrine on the anniversary of Japan's World War Two surrender, and Beijing filed a protest anyway, restraint bought Tokyo nothing this time. A Manhattan synagogue attack drew hate crime charges, the kind of story that needs no wit at all, only attention. Rachel Lam's column on Shein's IPO haircut showed mainland Stock Connect money quietly absorbing risk that returning Western capital walked away from, which tells you who actually believes in the float. And a new study found some Hong Kong subdivided flats felt like fifty two degrees inside this summer, a public health emergency, even as the city's air passenger traffic climbed nearly twelve percent, travelers arriving in a city where some residents can barely stand their own apartments. Watch whether Tehran's rejection hardens into something at sea, and whether anyone in the city's housing bureaucracy reads that heat study as an instruction rather than a footnote.
