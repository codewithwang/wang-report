Hormuz Talks Inch Forward While Washington Empties Its Shelves

Iran and the UAE traded tanker accusations as Oman edged toward a deal, Berkshire finally spent its cash pile, and a typhoon did what typhoons do to Okinawa.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

The Pentagon is burning through munitions stockpiles faster than it can replace them, and analysts in Moscow and Beijing are taking notes, since a war that drags on helps them by doing nothing at all. Iran and Oman describe a Hormuz deal as close, which is the diplomatic equivalent of a contractor telling you the kitchen is almost finished. Greg Abel put three years of Warren Buffett's cash to work with a twenty billion dollar buying spree, ending the longest stretch of Berkshire sitting on its hands under his watch. Typhoon Dolphin knocked out power to forty four thousand buildings in Okinawa and is now bearing down on the mainland, which will matter more to shipping schedules than to anyone's dinner plans tonight. The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as attorney general on a near party line vote. And Russian missiles killed three people, including a child, near Kyiv, a reminder that ceasefire talk in one theater does not pause the shooting in another. Watch whether Oman's deal survives contact with Tehran's actual demands, and whether Dolphin's landfall disrupts the mainland as badly as it did Okinawa.
