Canada Says War While Robots Steal the Track

Ottawa made the trade war formal, Washington's own auditor put the debt at forty trillion, and in Beijing a machine ran faster than Usain Bolt while nobody in government could say the same for the economy.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Mark Carney used the word war today, on the record, about a trading partner, which is the kind of sentence that used to end careers and now just ends news cycles. The fifty percent tariffs landed on dog muzzles and buoys along with everything that matters, proof that trade policy drafted in anger taxes the absurd right alongside the essential. Washington, meanwhile, is discovering that a forty trillion dollar debt and Wall Street's own bond market do not care how a tariff fight polls. Closer to home, Beijing's robot games produced a machine that beat Usain Bolt's hundred metre time, which is either the future of sport or the most honest thing a government has staged all year, since nobody rigged the outcome. Microsoft told banks a maximum severity flaw needed no action, the sort of reassurance that reassures nobody who actually reads a compliance memo. Fourteen people meditating in a Myanmar monastery died in a strike today. Separately, a $45 billion vaccine bet rests on a number still locked in a drawer until October, and the day's real theme was institutions promising certainty before they had earned it. Watch whether Ottawa's countertariffs actually bite by September, and whether that Moderna number, once unsealed in October, matches what the markets already assumed.
