A President Vanishes Into a Catering Container While Colombia Counts Its Dead

Air Force One flew on without the man it was named for, Colombia's earthquake toll climbed past two hundred, and Hong Kong quietly discussed longer trading hours as if that were the day's most urgent problem.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

The story that held today together was not one anyone wanted to admit was funny, and it stayed funny anyway. Facing a threat against his own aircraft, the president was quietly removed inside a catering container while Air Force One flew on regardless, full of staff and reporters who had signed up for a job, not a decoy assignment. Security theater usually means empty gestures staged for an audience's comfort. This was the opposite: real gestures made for an audience that was never told it was the audience. Colombia's earthquake death toll passed two hundred today, a number that will keep climbing as rescuers work through what a magnitude 7.4 leaves behind, and a helicopter fired missiles at a ship for the crime of moving oil out of Iran, the kind of sentence that once needed a paragraph of context and now just sits there, understood. Zimbabwe lost fifteen people when a boat went down on Lake Kariba. Hong Kong's exchange spent part of the day on a discussion paper about extending trading hours, while ships kept paying record fees to clear Panama regardless of what the clock said. Tomorrow: whether Colombia's toll stops climbing, and whether Washington explains the container with a straight face.
