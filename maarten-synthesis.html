A School in Thailand, a Ballroom in Washington, and a Storm Over Okinawa

The day ran from a schoolyard shooting outside Bangkok to a blocked presidential ballroom to a typhoon stranding Hong Kong travellers, and somehow all of it fit on the same wire.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

A high school outside Bangkok buried its dead today, eight of them, after a student walked in with a gun. That story made the ballroom headline out of Washington look small by comparison. An appeals court told the White House it needs Congress's permission to build the thing, a sentence about a ballroom that required three levels of the federal government to resolve. Washington also learned the economy shed 23,000 jobs in July, a number cheerful people are already calling a blip and worried people are calling a preview. Meanwhile Zelensky turned up in Belgrade, a visit that means less as spectacle than as arithmetic: a Serbia drifting from Moscow is one more country Russia can no longer assume. And Typhoon Dolphin closed Hong Kong's airport and stranded travellers in Okinawa, a smaller story than a war or a ballroom but the one most likely to have personally ruined somebody's Saturday. None of these connect except by the calendar. Watch whether the jobs number gets revised away, and whether Dolphin clears the runway by Monday.
