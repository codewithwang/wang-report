Warsh Says Calm While Robots Break Records in Beijing

A Fed governor tried to talk markets down from the ledge, and a machine ran faster than any human alive.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Kevin Warsh spent today doing the one thing officials only do when they need to: telling everyone, calmly, that the economy is fine, ahead of a Jackson Hole where he presumably intends to say it again to fewer skeptics. That is not confidence, that is triage. In Beijing, a humanoid robot beat Usain Bolt's hundred-meter record, which sounds like science fiction until you remember it is also, more immediately, a labor story, and China intends to make that point loudly and often. In Guinea, a waste mound collapsed after heavy rain and killed thirty people. Indonesia arrested seventy-two people over forest fires choking the region in haze, proof that some crises still move at the speed of smoke rather than software. European private equity is buying up the continent's asset managers at the fastest clip in decades, quietly consolidating who actually owns Europe's savings while everyone watches Washington and Ottawa shout at each other. None of these stories touched each other today. Watch whether Jackson Hole gives Warsh a friendlier room than the one he is currently placating, and whether Jakarta's haze crackdown outlasts the fires that caused it.
