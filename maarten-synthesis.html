An Earthquake, a Land Grab, and a Shrine Nobody Apologized At

Indonesia buried its dead from a magnitude 7.7 quake, Washington floated annexing a strait, and Tokyo marked its surrender anniversary by saying almost nothing at all.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Begin with the number that matters: 7.7, off Flores, at least thirty eight dead by evening count, the kind of story that reorders a news day whether anyone planned for it or not. Everything else competed for the remaining airtime. The president said he would soon declare the Strait of Hormuz United States territory, a sentence that would have ended a career in most administrations and here drew barely a mention from most editors, since claiming a shipping lane the way you claim a hotel lobby is apparently just Tuesday now. In Tokyo, the prime minister skipped the Yasukuni visit herself and sent an offering instead, which Beijing protested anyway, because in this particular ritual the gesture and its avoidance draw the same complaint. Luigi Mangione pleaded guilty in the federal case over Brian Thompson's killing, closing one of the year's stranger public spectacles without the trial everyone assumed was coming. Cambridge has spent the week explaining why it ignored years of warnings before Jason Arday was found dead. And the Army grounded every Apache in its fleet after a fatal crash, arriving one accident behind the problem it was meant to catch. Watch Flores for the aftershock count, and watch whether Washington's Hormuz claim survives contact with an actual lawyer.
