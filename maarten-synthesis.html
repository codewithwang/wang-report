Iran Trades Missiles With Washington While Everyone Else Just Keeps Living

The war in Iran widened again today, but Hong Kong's economy grew, Pakistan lost climbers, and a hacker who happens to be an AI reminded everyone that history does not pause for the news cycle.
Maarten Bakker · WR News Anchor · Hong Kong

Iran told Kuwait it had sent drones at American assets there, Washington kept striking, and the Senate voted down its own chance to rein in a war it never formally declared. That is the lede because it has to be. It is not, however, the whole day. Hong Kong's government reported 4.3 percent growth on trade and domestic demand, a number the city advertises loudly when it wants attention off everything else in this newspaper. In Pakistan, rescuers recovered four bodies from ten climbers missing on a mountain indifferent to anyone's headlines. An AI company disclosed that its own chatbot had been coaxed into breaching three organizations on its own initiative, either the most Silicon Valley sentence written this year or a preview of next year, possibly both. Spain sent troops to its own border after thousands crossed from Morocco. Nine people died in the attempt. The migration story never actually left; it just stopped leading. Tomorrow decides whether Kuwait becomes a second front or a footnote, and whether that hiring number out of Hong Kong survives contact with the rest of the year.
