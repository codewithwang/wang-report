Spain mourns as rail recordings raise urgent questions

A deadly collision leads the evening, alongside a Hong Kong sentence, North Korean missile launches and two tests of digital security.

Evening briefing

  • WorldSpain investigates clearance given before crash

    At least 68 people were killed when a Madrid-to-Seville train struck a stationary train. Spain has begun three days of national mourning; recordings published by Spanish media appear to show clearance to proceed despite the stopped train ahead, an instruction the transport minister says was given in error, while investigators continue examining the cause.

  • Hong KongEight years for corrosive-liquid attack on tourist

    Hong Kong’s High Court sentenced a 19-year-old to eight years in prison for throwing corrosive liquid at a Singaporean tourist in return for a promised HK$10,000. The judge said the attack, committed when the defendant was 17, left the woman with second-degree burns and a lifelong impact.

  • WorldNorth Korea fires around a dozen ballistic missiles

    South Korea’s military says North Korea launched around a dozen short-range ballistic missiles from the Wonsan area towards the sea on Thursday, less than a week after Seoul proposed border talks. Seoul has increased surveillance and vigilance for further launches.

  • CyberDetails of more than 41,000 DHS personnel published

    The BBC reports that names, work addresses, phone numbers and job descriptions of more than 41,000 US Homeland Security personnel have been published online, including thousands working in immigration enforcement. DHS says it is addressing a potential breach; the group claiming responsibility says it accessed records through Okta, which says it has no evidence its own systems or data were compromised.

  • WorldBritain’s social media age checks will reach adults too

    Ofcom has proposed photo-ID matching, banking checks, facial age estimation and digital ID for Britain’s under-16 social media ban, due in March, with government approval still pending and many adults likely to face checks. The regulator concedes some children will bypass them, leaving the familiar prospect of adults hunting for a passport while a teenager works out the workaround.
