Here is today's evening report. Sat, Oct 10, 18:04 HKT

  • 12:56 HKT Lai raises Taiwan defence spending while the US answers 60 Chinese moves with one word Mei Chen counts 60 Chinese moves around Taiwan in a month and one American adjective. Her reading of the two official ships is the part to keep. read more

    Mei Chen, GEOPOLITICAL

    Washington has found the word for what China is doing around Taiwan at sea, and the word is destabilising. It has not found a second sentence.

    CNA reported the US description of China's maritime actions around Taiwan as "deeply destabilising." The headline carries the adjective. Nothing we have seen carries a measure, a deployment, a change in the arms calendar.

    The same news cycle supplied the other side of the ledger. Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense tracked 21 Chinese military aircraft, seven naval vessels and two official ships between 6 a.m. Friday and the same hour Saturday. The two official ships are the line to read. Aircraft are a show. Official ships are administration.

    Two days earlier this desk recorded a Chinese maritime militia boat off Taiwan's east coast, the Pacific side that faces any US or Japanese reinforcement. A militia boat is deniable. A government vessel is not. Beijing is moving from the first to the second, and each step is smaller than the last objection to it.

    Our flashpoint map has logged 60 moves in the Taiwan Strait since September 10, the latest on October 9. That is one every half day or so. Washington's answer to 60 moves is a modifier.

    The pattern is the same one running in the Philippines. USNI News reports incidents between Chinese and Philippine forces across the South China Sea and the Luzon Strait have spiked in recent weeks, fought out with small boats. Small boats are chosen because no one has to call them war.

    Taipei is answering in the only currency it controls. President Lai Ching-te, per Firstpost, is raising defence spending to deter war as China ramps up pressure. Taipei pays in budget lines. Washington pays in vocabulary.

    Beijing reads both receipts. A coast guard that boards, inspects or escorts under Chinese administrative language will meet a US response already spent on the word it used here.

  • 12:10 HKT Going-concern disclaimers at Hong Kong listed firms now reach SFC, AFRC and HKEX at once Rachel Lam walks through how one auditor disclaimer now lands on three Hong Kong regulators at once, and why 'solely' is doing so much work. read more

    Rachel Lam, FINANCE & RISK

    On 8 October the SFC said it is acting, together with the Accounting and Financial Reporting Council and the Exchange, on auditor disclaimers of opinion that relate solely to going concern (a phrase that carries a good deal of weight in "solely"). Two days earlier, HKEX announced disciplinary action against six directors of Huisen Shares Group, stock code 2127, and against the former Chief Financial Officer and Company Secretary of CHK Oil, stock code 632.

    So the sequence on the page is this. Two disciplinary announcements land on Tuesday. A joint statement from the securities regulator, the auditor regulator and the exchange lands on Thursday. The Edge Singapore, reading the same week, headlined it as a crackdown on distressed listed stocks.

    The disciplinary announcements and the disclaimer statement are separate documents, and nothing in the notices ties them to each other. What they share is an audience. The Exchange disciplines the people who run the issuer. The AFRC regulates the auditors who sign the report. The SFC holds the Securities and Futures Ordinance, including Part XIVA on disclosure of inside information. A disclaimer that rests only on going concern touches all three at once.

    Look, a company that cannot show its auditor enough to support a going-concern view has already told the market something. What changes is who reads it afterwards. For an issuer's company secretary, the Chapter 13 continuing obligations in the Main Board Listing Rules are the document that matters this month. For an auditor, the question is whether a going-concern disclaimer still closes the file or now opens one.

    A lender or fund holding paper in a thinly capitalised listed name should assume the next disclaimer in its portfolio is read by three desks, and the next HKEX disciplinary announcements will show whether the CFO and the company secretary are the ones named.

Trump Announces US Will Import Russian Diesel Before the Midterms

A president facing high fuel prices and an election next month has chosen cheaper diesel over a sanctions posture, and the cost of that choice is landing on Kyiv.

President Trump has announced that the United States will import Russian diesel to bring down fuel prices, AP reports. The decision reverses years of American pressure on Moscow, and it was made with an election about a month away.

The motive is not hidden. The BBC reports that high pump prices and next month's midterms are pushing Trump to ease his party's pain at the pump, even at the cost of a sharp turn in Russia policy. The squeeze is real. Delta reported a third quarter earnings miss and cut its guidance, with fuel costs up 62% from a year ago, according to Yahoo Finance. The Kyiv Independent says Trump described the supply as millions of tons, available to the US and to global markets.

Then came the reaction. President Zelensky called letting Russia sell diesel to the US an 'investment in war,' Reuters reports. Axios cites a US official saying Trump struck the deal with Putin after Zelensky ignored his requests. CNBC reports the arrangement has been accused of contradicting Russia sanctions law. Canberra, for its part, said it neither needs nor wants Russian fuel and will keep its own purchases barred.

The reporting from our desks today is elsewhere, and it is worth your time. Mei Chen's Geopolitical desk has the Taiwan Strait, where Taipei logged 21 Chinese aircraft, seven warships and two official ships in a day, and where Washington's answer was one adjective. Rachel Lam's Finance and Risk desk explains how a single kind of auditor disclaimer at Hong Kong listed firms now reaches the SFC, the AFRC and HKEX together.

Back to the diesel. A sanctions policy works because buyers stay out of the market, and the largest buyer has just said it will walk in when the price at home gets uncomfortable. Moscow now has proof that American pressure has a fuel price attached to it, and Kyiv has been told, in public, where it ranks against the American pump.
