Morning roundup

Hong Kong’s fire accountability debate and school AI plans, a technology selloff, a compromised coding extension, relief in Ukraine and Halloween in Mong Kok.

  • Hong KongMinisters defend staying in office

    Hong Kong ministers defended remaining in their posts after the deadly Tai Po fire, saying the city leader had ordered them to remain and carry out reforms. Acting housing minister Michael Wong apologised alongside three cabinet colleagues on Friday, while a senior official hinted that resigned housing chief Winnie Ho might not receive a permanent replacement.

  • FinanceAI stock retreat deepens

    The Nasdaq 100 fell 3.5% on Friday, its sharpest slide since early June, as a technology selloff extended into a third week. Investors moved towards defensive stocks amid mounting doubts that AI profits will justify the industry’s debt-fuelled investment boom.

  • CyberClaude extension carried a backdoor

    Security firm Phorion reported that version 2.1.313 of the official Claude Code extension on Cursor’s marketplace contained code designed to install persistent remote access on developers’ machines; Cursor removed the release. Implementation bugs limited some access attempts, but uninstalling the extension alone does not remove the persistence mechanisms it planted.

  • AIAI enters the primary curriculum

    Hong Kong will gradually introduce an information and innovation technology subject in primary schools from September 2028, under a provisional curriculum framework announced Friday. Pupils as young as seven will learn to hold simple conversations with AI systems using prompts or commands, as part of a broader push for digital literacy and creativity.

  • WorldLocal Ukraine truce allows evacuation

    Starving residents began leaving Russian-held Oleshky on Friday after an aid convoy arrived under a local ceasefire brokered by US first lady Melania Trump, Reuters reported. The joint relief operation offered a rare opening for civilians, although the Kremlin cautioned against treating it as a guide to a wider peace settlement.

  • LifeMong Kok’s Halloween finale

    Langham Place’s Halloween Street Fest concludes today, October 31, with performances outside the mall from 1pm to 8pm and an advertised free haunted minibus shuttle between Mong Kok and Parkes Street in Jordan. Even the supernatural has an early finish.
