Trump, Xi and Putin back official immunity in warning to international courts
The Executive Brief, once a month, free. What it is

Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin agreed to pursue nuclear dialogue, expand refined fuel supplies and defend state consent and official immunity.

Their November 19 declaration from Shenzhen establishes common ground on managing nuclear risks, energy supply and international accountability. It announces no agreed Ukraine settlement.

The nuclear commitment has substance: the three countries will conduct dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation. The declaration supplies neither arsenal caps nor verification arrangements. Talks are the announced outcome; negotiated restraints remain ahead.

On energy, the leaders plan to increase production and supply of refined petroleum products. They give no quantities or timetable. That leaves the scale and timing of additional fuel supplies unspecified.

The declaration also gives international courts a warning. The leaders call state consent and the immunity of state officials paramount, and say the International Criminal Court and similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction.

Three governments have adopted a shared political position on the reach of international accountability. The statement changes no law and constitutes no court ruling.

China’s separate account goes further in describing Xi’s preferred relationship among the powers. It records his call for advance communication on sensitive issues, respect for each other’s red lines, and crisis communication and prevention mechanisms.

Those are Xi’s proposals as presented by China’s foreign ministry. The jointly issued declaration does not adopt that full programme.

The Chinese account also says the leaders discussed Ukraine. That establishes its reported place in the meeting, qualifying earlier expectations that Beijing sought to keep it off the agenda. The declaration’s silence on Ukraine supplies no evidence of an agreed outcome.

In Berlin, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz described settlement principles with direct consequences for Ukrainians: sovereignty, the line of contact as the starting point for territorial negotiations, continued European integration, reconstruction assistance and strong security guarantees after a ceasefire.

According to Ukrinform, Merz said negotiations must include Ukraine, Russia, the United States and Europe. He also said gradual relief from some sanctions could be considered under conditions. He announced no enacted relief, and the supplied documents establish no bargain connecting sanctions to Shenzhen’s fuel plan.

For Ukrainians, the consequential terms concern where territorial negotiations begin, who participates and what protection follows a ceasefire. Berlin has stated principles on those questions. Acceptance by the Shenzhen leaders remains unannounced.
