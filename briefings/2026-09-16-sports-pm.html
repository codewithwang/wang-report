Adam Silver Says He Missed the Red Flags in $12.5 Billion Lakers Sale

Adam Silver stood in front of the NBA's Board of Governors in New York this week and said, essentially, that the league's background check missed the part where the FBI was already looking at the guy. Mark Walter, the insurance billionaire fronting the $12.5 billion purchase of the Los Angeles Lakers, cleared the league's vetting process before reports surfaced tying him to a federal probe. Silver called it a surprise. For a league that fingerprints backup point guards for gambling ties, that is not a small admission. It is the commissioner of a $150 billion industry saying the metal detector went off after the plane had already boarded.

Here is what the surprise actually protects: the largest franchise sale in North American sports history, one that resets the valuation ceiling for every other team in the league. The Lakers sale is not just a transaction, it is the new floor under every Board of Governors conversation about what a marquee franchise is worth, and Silver has too much riding on that number to slow it down over a red flag he says he never saw. The NBA did not find the problem. The league found the price it wanted first, and the vetting came after.
