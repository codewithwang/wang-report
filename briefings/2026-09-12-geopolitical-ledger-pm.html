China Fires Flares at Philippine Aircraft Over Reef

China fired flares at a Philippine military aircraft this week, according to Manila's account carried by the South China Morning Post, the latest escalation in a dispute the Chinese foreign ministry has, since September 11, been recasting as a Philippine information operation rather than a maritime one, having accused Manila's defense secretary of leaking a diplomatic note and "grandstanding." The China Coast Guard has not disputed the flare account itself. It has disputed who gets to narrate it.

The distinction matters to a reader who prices Philippine risk, because the Bureau of the Treasury in Manila is mid-cycle on its 2026 external borrowing programme, and the flare incident does not move that calculus the way a blockade or a collision would, or, more precisely, it moves the *narrative* risk premium without moving the physical one, since no vessel was damaged and no crew was injured. Beijing's choice to fight the story rather than the reef, the same posture its foreign ministry took toward the Taiwan VP's EU trip this week, tells the Treasury's external funding desk that the coast guard budget is running the confrontation and the foreign ministry is running the cleanup. The two offices are not yet reading from the same page, and until they are, Manila's sovereign spread absorbs the noise, not the risk.
