Uefa backs Diallo for Fifa presidency as Infantino remains favourite
The Executive Brief, once a month, free. What it is

Uefa has finally found someone to run against Gianni Infantino. Finding enough people to vote for him is the considerably less ceremonial part.

According to BBC Sport, European football’s governing body unanimously endorsed French federation president Philippe Diallo through its executive committee, less than 48 hours before Wednesday’s nomination deadline. The election is in March. Arsene Wenger, whom senior European figures had hoped to recruit, declined to stand.

Infantino remains the favourite among Fifa’s 211 voting associations, the BBC reports, with support thought to extend across most of Africa, Asia and South America. Diallo says he already has backing beyond Europe, but the report provides no tally establishing a winning coalition.

The money underneath this election goes directly to the associations casting those votes. Infantino proposed increasing Fifa’s worldwide football-development funding by £2.6 billion. The BBC reports that Asian Football Confederation president Sheikh Salman subsequently endorsed him, having previously joined European and Concacaf leaders in accusing him of breaking trust. That sequence establishes a political recovery; it does not establish an exchange of funding for support.

For associations relying on development funds, the campaign concerns the resources available to build football as well as the person running it. Diallo’s manifesto and identifiable support outside Europe are the useful next evidence. Europe’s applause supplies neither.

World Rugby has delivered a more immediate consequence for South Africa coach Rassie Erasmus. The Guardian reports a six-international-match suspension after he questioned officials’ integrity following defeat by France.

The restrictions reach well beyond an empty seat in the coaches’ box. Erasmus is barred from direct or indirect match-day contact with players and management, entering the stadium and communicating with match officials. He will miss the Ireland match and South Africa’s finals-weekend fixture.

South Africa Rugby has requested a full misconduct hearing to contextualise his remarks. The suspension takes effect immediately. Players preparing for those matches already have to work around it.
