Myanmar disputes genocide ruling as 1.2 million Rohingya wait to return
The Executive Brief, once a month, free. What it is

Myanmar’s response to the genocide judgment leaves Rohingya refugees waiting for the government to decide when conditions permit them to return.

The Associated Press reports that the International Court of Justice, the United Nations’ top court, found Myanmar responsible for genocide against the Rohingya in its 2017 military campaign. The reported remedies include reparations, safe return for refugees and punishment of perpetrators.

Myanmar’s Foreign Ministry disputed the evidence on Friday. Its statement committed to none of those remedies, according to AP.

Instead, it promised repatriation “as and when situations permit,” while invoking sovereignty. That wording preserves the government’s discretion over when return can happen.

About 1.2 million Rohingya refugees live in Bangladesh, primarily in crowded camps in Cox’s Bazar. AP describes fire hazards, infectious diseases, monsoon landslides, inadequate sanitation and strict restrictions on work. For those residents, an indefinite condition means continued exposure to specific dangers.

The authority receiving the judgment also carries a direct connection to the campaign. Min Aung Hlaing commanded the military in 2017 and is now president, AP reports.

Safe return faces another obstacle inside Myanmar. The country is fighting a civil war. In Rakhine, the Arakan Army is battling the military and has itself been accused of targeting Rohingya, according to the report.

The next measurable change would be a commitment to implement the remedies. The government has so far promised to study the judgment. Refugees have been offered no timetable for going home.
