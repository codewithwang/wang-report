GEOPOLITICAL Mei Chen Geopolitical Desk, Senior Correspondent Sunday, September 27, 2026
Xi's Independence Demand Outlasted the Summit Pomp

Xi Jinping used his three days in Washington to press one specific request: that Washington state formally that it opposes Taiwan independence, not merely that it does not support it. That distinction has sat unresolved since Xi raised it on September 25, and the summit's official output, framed by both governments as a rapport exercise on trade and artificial intelligence, contained no answer to it. U.S. News reported the visit closed on personal chemistry rather than agreements. The unresolved sentence was the agreement Beijing came for.

Within 48 hours, Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense logged five Chinese military aircraft, six naval vessels and five official ships operating around the island between Friday and Saturday morning, a routine patrol tempo that continued straight through the summit without pause. Washington's own ambassador to Beijing said this week that the pending $14 billion interceptor sale runs on "Trump's own schedule," not Beijing's clock and not Taipei's. Xi did not get the sentence he asked for. He also did not get a Trump commitment to keep the interceptors moving. A summit that resolves nothing for either side just resets the deadline to the next one.
