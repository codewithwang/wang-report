Beijing Sends Warplanes East, Not Just Toward Taiwan's Southwest

Taiwan's defense ministry logged 27 PLA aircraft and 11 vessels operating around the island in the 24 hours to Friday, and the geography is the story: sorties east of Taiwan, not concentrated in the southwest air defense identification zone that has anchored PLA activity since 2020. The Global Taiwan Institute flagged the same eastward shift this week. Taiwan's east coast holds the mountain-shielded missile and radar sites Taipei has spent two decades building precisely because the terrain blocks a first strike from the west. An eastern approach does not threaten those sites today. It rehearses reaching them.

Beijing paired the flights with a warning through Global Times naming Taiwan independence advocates as targets, language that tracks the extraterritorial jurisdiction law Beijing activated against individual Taiwanese this month rather than the broad coercion rhetoric of prior years. The east-coast sorties, read against a law built for named individuals over blockade theater, mark a shift from demonstrating capability against the island to mapping the approach that would remove its second-strike option. The next signal to watch is whether the People's Liberation Army Eastern Theater Command holds that eastern posture through August or reverts to the southwest ADIZ pattern once the current drill window closes.

By Vincent LaiGeopolitical Desk, Occasional ContributorSat, Aug 1
Beijing's East-Coast Shift Puts a Live Wire Through the Bond Desk

Taiwan's east coast is where the mountain-shielded second-strike sites sit, and it is also, or more precisely, less directly but no less mechanically, the geography that Taipei's central bank and the island's exporters treat as the assumption underneath every dollar-hedging decision they make. The People's Liberation Army logged 27 aircraft and 11 vessels around the island in the 24 hours to Friday, concentrated east of the island rather than the southwest air defense identification zone that has anchored PLA activity since 2020, according to Taiwan's defense ministry and confirmed independently by the Global Taiwan Institute this week. Global Times paired the sorties with language naming Taiwan independence advocates as targets, tracking the extraterritorial jurisdiction law Beijing activated against individual Taiwanese this month.

The desk that has to price this before the Central News Agency does is the Bank of Taiwan's foreign exchange trading room, which manages the reserve position Taipei uses to defend the New Taiwan dollar against exactly the kind of capital-flight scenario an eastern approach implies, not a same-week move, since Taipei has weathered southwest ADIZ incursions for six years without triggering one, but a repricing of the tail risk the reserve position exists to absorb. Premier Li's economic working group in Beijing has spent since March signaling that PBOC easing is contingent on a tariff settlement the diplomatic track has not produced, a timeline that runs independently of whatever the Eastern Theater Command decides to do with its August posture. Watch whether the Eastern Theater Command holds the eastern pattern past the current drill window; if it does, the Bank of Taiwan's reserve desk has a decision to make well before the PBOC's November fixing does.
