Taipei's Han Kuang Drills Now Rehearse Losing Fixed Assets

The Han Kuang exercises running this week moved past troop mobilization into a category Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense had not previously drilled in public: relocating arms production. Reuters reported units practicing the dispersal of weapons manufacturing sites, the kind of infrastructure a first strike targets before troops ever cross the strait. A defense ministry under Minister Wellington Koo, who took the post in 2024 under President Lai Ching-te, does not stage that rehearsal for an audience that isn't already assuming the factories get hit.

The assumption embedded in that drill matters more than the drill itself. Dispersing arms plants only makes sense if Taipei has stopped planning around deterrence-by-denial at the border and started planning around continuity-of-production after the border is breached. That is a different war plan than the one Taiwan's public messaging has sold for a decade, and it is being rehearsed the same week Beijing sent a rocket on a trajectory crossing Taiwan's airspace and the PLA ran new patrols the island's defense ministry logged as pressure, not routine. If the arms-plant relocation drill becomes an annual fixture rather than a one-off, Taipei has quietly redefined what surviving the opening days of a conflict requires.

PBOC Faces November Fixing Test Amid Fujian Drills

The Han Kuang exercises Taiwan is running this week are drawing Chinese naval patrols close enough to the strait that Taipei's defense ministry logged them as pressure rather than routine, and a Chinese rocket is set to cross Taiwan's airspace on a trajectory Focus Taiwan reported this week. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange, or, more precisely, the cross-border capital flow desk inside SAFE that monitors southbound and northbound Bond Connect volumes daily, treats a strait crisis differently than the newsroom does: it is a liquidity event before it is a headline, because every prior escalation cycle since 2022 has produced a measurable widening in onshore-offshore CNH spreads within the same trading week.

The PBOC's open-market desk has not had to defend the yuan against a Fujian-drill-driven outflow spike since the August 2022 Pelosi visit, when the offshore rate gapped more than 400 pips against the fix inside three sessions. Mei Chen's read of the Han Kuang arms-relocation drills as a rehearsal for continuity-of-production after a breach is the Taipei-side signal; the Shanghai-side signal is whether the PBOC's daily fixing on August 3 and the days after holds within its usual band or widens to absorb capital flight, and that fixing, not the drill calendar, is the number the desk in Shanghai is actually watching this week.
