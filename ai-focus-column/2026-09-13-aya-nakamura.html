Moonshot's Revenue Story Has A Claude-Shaped Hole

Moonshot built a $2 billion revenue story and a $50 billion valuation on Kimi K3, and Anthropic's forensic evidence says part of that output was Claude wearing Moonshot's name.
中村 彩 · Aya Nakamura AI Desk, Senior Correspondent Sunday, September 13, 2026

The Growth Numbers

Moonshot AI told TechCrunch on September 11 that its Kimi chatbot is on pace for $2 billion in annualized revenue by the end of the year, up from roughly $1 billion in August and about $300 million in June. That is not growth measured in years, it is a business roughly doubling every couple of months, built on Kimi K3, the model Moonshot shipped this year to go head to head with the biggest US chatbots. The company has been raising money at a $50 billion valuation and pushing toward a Hong Kong stock listing, a valuation that has roughly doubled since its last funding round as the listing timeline moved into view.

One day earlier, a different set of numbers landed. On September 10, Anthropic published a forensic report tracing roughly 300,000 Kimi customer requests that were secretly routed, over a 10-day window, through a network of about 5,000 to 5,380 fake accounts into Claude, Anthropic's own chatbot. The requests went out as questions to Kimi. They came back as answers from Claude. Kimi served them to its own users as Moonshot's work. Some slice of the traffic sitting inside that $2 billion revenue number, in other words, was never Moonshot's model at all.

Six Firms, One Advisory

The finding didn't arrive on its own. On September 8, the NSA, FBI and CISA jointly named six Chinese AI companies, DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI, in an advisory alleging systematic extraction of 'billions of tokens across millions of exchanges' from Claude, OpenAI's GPT, Google's Gemini and xAI's Grok since late 2024. The practice is called distillation: querying a rival's finished model over and over and using its answers as training material to build a cheaper copy, without redoing the research that produced the original. It needs an API key, not the export-controlled chips Washington has spent years trying to keep out of China.

China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning rejected the accusation the next day, said China's AI progress reflects self-reliance, and called on Washington to honor prior presidential-level understandings rather than smear China. A model that has to borrow another company's answers to hit its growth target is not exactly the self-reliance Beijing keeps advertising. Anthropic's report, filed a day after Mao Ning's denial, put numbers next to it: across five distillation campaigns totaling nearly 200 million exchanges, Alibaba generated 151 million exchanges between May and July, peaking near 3 million a day across 3,500 accounts, aimed at its own Qwen models. DeepSeek's campaign ran past 12 million attempts across 14 days in July. Moonshot's was the smallest in volume and the most damaging in kind, because it wasn't training data harvested offline, it was live customer traffic resold as Moonshot's own product.

What The Bankers Priced

The people who now have to answer for this are Moonshot's IPO bankers, the ones who built a pitch around $2 billion in revenue and a Kimi K3 model that beat US benchmarks. If a meaningful share of that output was Claude relabeled, the growth curve underwriters marketed to Hong Kong investors describes a product Moonshot didn't fully build. Users who query Kimi daily were, for some share of their queries, talking to Claude without knowing it, and the company that sold them the product can't yet say how much.

For Moonshot's defense to hold, the company would need to show the roughly 5,000 to 5,380 accounts were an unauthorized workaround, an engineering team padding output quality without sign-off, and that the practice ended before the K3 metrics underpinning the $2 billion pitch were compiled. Anthropic's report doesn't settle that; it only establishes the traffic pattern. Distillation via API key bypasses the chip embargo entirely, no chip rule written for export controls touches a company that just needs a login and a query loop.

Nothing has stopped. No account ban, no confirmed IPO delay, just Anthropic's numbers sitting next to Moonshot's own two weeks before Xi Jinping's planned September 24 Washington visit, where a technical dispute over borrowed outputs becomes exactly the kind of irritant a summit is built to either bury or escalate. Moonshot's IPO clock keeps running against evidence nobody has resolved, against Kimi K3's still-unresolved composition.
