By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Aug 16

Amazon is drawing political fire in New York. Washington is drawing a line around Apple's chip sourcing. Neither headline moved the market, and by the close the S&P, the Nasdaq, and the Hang Seng offered no clean read in either direction. Rate anxiety has investors rotating toward dividend shelter instead, with income funds outpacing the plain vanilla S&P tracker as portfolios lean defensive without leaving the table.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated holding, is where the noise is landing. Chip supply lines are back in the headlines, this time with Washington blocking Apple from a Chinese memory source. Nvidia's stake in Intel has quietly turned into a windfall many times its size, the kind of trade that makes "strategic investment" sound almost modest. Consumer names are softer: Lululemon sits at a six-year low. Barclays finds a silver lining in the new chief executive, hardly a ringing endorsement, more a hopeful footnote.

Her advisors have been busy. They are trimming Nvidia in stages over the coming weeks and redirecting part of the proceeds into a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway, a swap from momentum toward patience. Healthcare and international exposure are next, still under discussion, not yet committed. On Intel, the instruction is blunt: decide, don't drift. AMD, meanwhile, just borrowed nearly five billion dollars in its largest bond sale ever. Even chipmakers need financing, not just fans.
