By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Aug 25

Wall Street opened the week with the usual pageantry of certainty and the usual absence of it. The major indices traded without a clean directional story, chipmakers under visible pressure ahead of Nvidia's earnings, while a fresh six billion dollars found its way into S&P 500 index funds in a single session. When in doubt, apparently, buy the whole haystack rather than hunt for the needle.

Her technology book, the portfolio's largest and most concentrated position, felt that ambivalence directly. AMD slid alongside peers in the pre-earnings jitters, while a Nvidia manager's alleged role in a chip-smuggling scheme to China added a headline the sector did not need. Adobe was the exception, jumping on raised guidance and a reminder that "AI push" still works as a phrase on an earnings call. Consumer names had their own week. Amazon drew political fire from New York's mayor-elect, and Lululemon got a public nod from Michael Burry, who called the stock cheap and Apple, by contrast, permanently expensive. Apple itself is trimming staff as its leadership transition approaches. Okta disclosed a dashboard breach tied to a vendor incident.

Her advisors have been busy. They are trimming Nvidia down to a smaller share of the book, staged over several weeks, with proceeds funding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Healthcare and international exposure are next, alongside hard rules for the portfolio's more speculative names: no adds, half off on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. Intel remains the one position without a verdict. Her advisors intend to end that limbo soon, which is corporate speak for "we're still arguing about it."
