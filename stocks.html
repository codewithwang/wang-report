By Charmaine LoNews AnchorMon, Aug 24

Amazon nudged its device prices higher today, a reminder that in retail as everywhere else, margin comes first and the customer reads about it later. NVIDIA did the same on AI chips, up more than fifteen percent, and the Vanguard fund that tracks the broad market now leans on it more heavily than on Apple. A first. Rate mood stayed steady, the tape absorbed both moves without complaint, and Apple answered with its largest buyback under Cook, which is one way to keep a chart calmer than the news around it.

Technology carried the portfolio again, NVIDIA's price hikes cutting two ways for a concentrated book that owns the chip and the fund that owns the chip through someone else. Adobe traded at a rare discount as questions about AI growth and leadership circled, the kind of name value investors notice before growth investors do. AMD picked the future over the multiple, committing significant capital to Taiwan chip packaging with TSMC. Consumer names split: Lululemon drew a regulatory inquiry in Texas over chemicals in its fabric, a headline that moves slower than most and matters more.

Her advisors have been trimming the largest position, NVIDIA, in stages over the coming weeks, and opening a first stake in Berkshire Hathaway funded from that trim. Momentum for ballast, in other words. They are also weighing healthcare and international additions, drafting hard exit rules for the portfolio's smallest, most speculative names, and deciding, finally, what to do with Intel, a stake Crew Capital just trimmed on its own account. Her advisors keep saying commitment beats limbo. Someone should tell that to the Intel file.
