By Charmaine LoNews AnchorMon, Sep 14

Markets were closed for the data pull today, so the tape itself has nothing to report: no print on the S&P, the Nasdaq, or the Hang Seng. That silence is its own kind of signal, a reminder that portfolios sit still even when their owners do not, and that today's real activity happened not on an exchange but around a table.

With no fresh index levels to lean on, the story in this book runs through positioning rather than price. The technology sleeve remains the dominant force here, a concentrated book anchored by the largest names in chips and software, running alongside a broad market index holder, an energy fund, and a leveraged semiconductor vehicle that amplifies whatever mood the chip sector is in. Fixed income sits quietly to the side in the form of a Hong Kong government savings bond, doing exactly what it is supposed to do, which is not much.

The advisors were busy even if the tape was not. They discussed trimming the NVIDIA position down from its outsized share, doing so gradually across several tranches over the coming weeks, with early proceeds earmarked to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Looking further out, they floated adding a healthcare name, either UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, alongside international diversification through a broad ex-US fund. On the smaller, more volatile names, SoundHound AI and Unusual Machines, the guidance was blunt: stop adding, start writing rules, trim into strength, exit if the thesis breaks. Intel remains a name under review, a decision the advisors still have to make, not a position they're content to hold indefinitely.
