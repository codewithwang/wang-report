By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Sep 29

Index levels are missing from today's tape. The S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng all read n/a, and no rate or economic data arrived with them, so the mood comes from the headlines. Nvidia announced a buyback billed as the biggest in market history. Meta's weakness dragged the broad index lower, with VOO down 1.0%. Apple was hit with a $5.7 billion patent bill.

*CL*

The portfolio is mostly a technology book, and that is where today's movement sits. Nvidia's buyback and AMD's $8.2 billion purchase of World Labs, which sent AMD shares down 4%, both landed in semiconductors. The book also carries a leveraged semiconductor fund, so those moves count for more here than in most portfolios. Consumer cyclicals had a quieter day: Amazon won an AWS deal with CSL, and Lululemon signed Chris Lee and Lewis Hamilton for its outerwear line. Citizens lifted its Okta target to $225. Adobe is projecting a record US holiday online shopping season. The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF, the index holding, slipped with the market.

*CL*

Nvidia is the name in focus, and the advisors have discussed trimming it toward a smaller share of the portfolio, in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. They have also discussed adding healthcare through UNH or JNJ and international exposure through VXUS. For SoundHound and Unusual Machines they suggest hard rules: no adds, half off on a double, a full exit if the thesis breaks. Intel needs a decision, cut or commit. A company that buys back $150 billion of its own stock and a portfolio that wants to hold less of it are, at least, in agreement about something.
