By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Sep 2

The FTC's suit against Amazon over ad overcharges landed today. The broader tape gave investors little else to work with, majors closed mixed, and rate expectations did most of the talking. Traders spent the session parsing earnings rather than macro data. The mood was less about direction than about which companies could still surprise to the upside.

The technology book, the most crowded shelf in this portfolio, split along old lines and new ones. Chipmakers caught a lift from Nvidia's newly disclosed thirty five billion dollar cloud arrangement with Anthropic, a deal that reminded the market Nvidia's backlog is now measured in hyperscaler commitments, not just gaming quarters. Adobe's four billion dollar Saudi AI investment produced a shrug from Wall Street. Not every AI headline moves a stock anymore. Software infrastructure had the day's loudest moment, with Okta surging on blowout earnings tied to demand for AI agent security, a category that barely existed as a line item two years ago.

Apple drew headlines for governance rather than product, with word that Tim Cook is stepping aside for John Ternus. The advisors are watching the transition for continuity risk rather than trading around it. On positioning, they have discussed trimming Nvidia down from its outsized perch in stages over the coming weeks, using early proceeds to open a position in Berkshire Hathaway. They have also flagged building out healthcare and international exposure in the months ahead, and drafting exit rules on the portfolio's more speculative names before conviction is tested again. Intel remains an open question. Cook's successor gets a honeymoon. Intel does not.
