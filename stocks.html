By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Aug 25

Chip stocks took a step back today, AMD sliding as investors braced for Nvidia's earnings, while the equal weight trade in the S&P 500 crossed a hundred billion dollars, a sign the rally is finally inviting more than seven guests to the party. The broader tape stayed watchful rather than jittery, rates in the background, everyone waiting for one semiconductor company to set the mood for the rest of the year.

The technology book, this portfolio's largest and most concentrated holding, carried the day's real drama. Adobe jumped on AI guidance that beat expectations. Nvidia drew a headline out of Taiwan involving a manager and a chip smuggling probe. Amazon raised device prices sharply, citing a memory shortage, which is a very technical way of saying everyone wants the same chips at once. Consumer names split the difference: Michael Burry called Lululemon cheap in public, then called Apple expensive in the same breath, just ahead of reports of a foldable phone priced like a small vacation. Okta had the day's least welcome headline, its dashboard turning up in stolen data from a breach at another company entirely. Guilt by association is still guilt, as far as customers are concerned.

Her advisors have been trimming the Nvidia position in stages and using the proceeds to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway, a fairly on the nose statement about risk appetite. They are also weighing healthcare and international additions, sketching firmer rules for the portfolio's smaller speculative names, and deciding, finally, what to do with Intel. Commit or cut. No more limbo.
