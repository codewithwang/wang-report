By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Oct 3

The index screens were blank on the macro tape today, so there are no closing levels for the S&P 500, Nasdaq or Hang Seng, and no fresh read on rates or the economy. The tone we do have was firm. The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF rose 0.7% as a Tesla rally lifted the market, and AI leaders kept carrying it. Nvidia touched a record high, its market value closing in on six trillion dollars, and AMD finished a September that gained 30%.

For this portfolio, technology is the day, and a concentrated technology book feels it in both directions. Semiconductors are running, which matters for the leveraged semiconductor fund as much as the chip names. Software is more argumentative. Adobe is stuck in a debate over the AI threat, while analysts lifted Okta's consensus price target by 16.5%. Consumer cyclical was uneven. Lululemon sank to a 52-week low as its selloff deepened, and Amazon is moving to take billions in Nvidia chips off its balance sheet. Apple locked down Mac disk access against AI agents, so even the hardware makers are now defending their hardware from the software.

Her advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in three tranches over four to six weeks, and funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position from the first. Next in line are healthcare through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, plus an international fund. They also want hard rules on SoundHound and Unusual Machines, and a decision on Intel: cut or commit, no limbo. Selling the winner to buy the insurer is a plan. Writing rules for the speculative names is where it gets tested.
