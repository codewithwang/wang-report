By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Aug 4

Bezos selling four billion dollars of Amazon stock made headlines the same week Amazon and Microsoft added a trillion dollars in combined market value in two days. That is the tape right now: a market that shrugs at insider selling as long as the index math cooperates. Big tech carried the major indices again, and the ETF language around this portfolio's core holding put it plainly: built for days when a handful of names do the heavy lifting while the rest of the market watches.

The technology sleeve, the largest concentration in this portfolio, had a mixed session under the surface. Chip names traded on competing signals: encouraging ahead of earnings for one manufacturer, more cautious on a warning from a rival's chief scientist about capacity limits down the line. Software security continues to consolidate, with acquisition activity extending platforms rather than narrowing them. Consumer names split hard. One retailer sits near its highs. An apparel name sits at a multi-year low.

Lululemon drew the most individual attention, a six-year low that one bank framed as a bottom rather than a warning, calling the new chief executive the reason to stay. NVIDIA remained the center of portfolio conversation. The advisors have discussed trimming that position down to a smaller share of the book over several weeks, redirecting early proceeds into a new position in Berkshire Hathaway. They have also flagged Intel for a decision rather than a holding pattern, and want firm exit rules written for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names before the next earnings cycle complicates the conversation. Rules written in advance are rules that survive contact with a bad quarter.
