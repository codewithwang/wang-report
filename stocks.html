By Charmaine LoNews AnchorMon, Aug 17

Amazon's delivery fight and a chip warning to Apple made the headlines, but the tape underneath was quiet: indices flat, rates steady, nothing from the majors worth calling a signal. The real movement was inside individual names. AMD raised debt to fund AI buildout. Nvidia turned its Intel stake into a paper windfall the same week Intel's own chips drew a US warning shot over China exposure. CrowdStrike sold off despite a beat that, on paper, should have been welcomed.

Technology, the largest sleeve in this portfolio by a wide margin, carried the day's drama. Palantir called its own quarter otherworldly and raised guidance, a bit of self-praise usually left to the analysts covering it. AMD popped on the financing news. Intel rode Nvidia's coattails higher without doing much of anything on its own. CrowdStrike showed that beating expectations and satisfying the market are no longer the same trade. Consumer names had their own headaches: Lululemon shares fell overnight on brand backlash ahead of a CEO transition, and Amazon got squeezed between City Hall and its own delivery workers in New York.

Inside the technology concentration, the advisors are trimming the Nvidia position in staged tranches over the coming weeks, with proceeds seeding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Beyond that, the conversation turns to balance: a healthcare allocation under consideration, international exposure through a broad ex-US fund, and firm exit rules being drafted for the portfolio's smaller, more speculative names. Intel is the one the advisors want settled. A decision, they say, not more waiting.
