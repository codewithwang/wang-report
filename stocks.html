By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Sep 3

Wall Street's data cupboard was bare today. No fresh print on the S&P, the Nasdaq, or the Hang Seng, which left traders to entertain themselves with rate speculation and the usual assortment of corporate headlines. The market wants a rate cut and has not proven it deserves one, so the absence of numbers became its own kind of news. No data, and still an opinion on everything.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest by a wide margin, had plenty to chew on even without a macro print. Nvidia's move to buy Hugging Face for thirteen billion dollars reframed the AI trade as infrastructure shopping, not just chip demand. AMD eased despite a strong quarter, a reminder that "beat and raise" no longer guarantees applause. Software names split down the middle: Okta doubled this year on hopes that AI agents extend its relevance, while Intel's foundry ambitions drew fresh skepticism over how captive its customer base really is. Consumer names took hits of their own, Apple facing a substantial UK lawsuit over tracking practices, Lululemon bracing for a leadership reset under its incoming brand chief.

Her advisors have been busy with the technology book specifically. They are trimming Nvidia down to a smaller share of the portfolio in stages over the coming weeks, redirecting the first proceeds into a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also drafting hard rules for the portfolio's more speculative names, SoundHound and Unusual Machines: no further adds, discipline on doubles, an exit if the thesis breaks. Looking further out, the advisors are weighing healthcare and international diversification, a decision on Intel one way or the other, and a currency hedge tied to the Hong Kong dollar peg.
