The index tape was not available at filing time, so there are no S&P 500, Nasdaq or Hang Seng levels to read out, and no rate signal worth quoting. The mood has to come from the headlines. Nvidia hit a record high and is closing in on a six trillion dollar valuation. Amazon's plan to buy Nvidia chips is being read as a bill for the AI boom. An analyst downgrade of Okta argued that cybersecurity as a sector is overheated. Money is still moving toward AI, and the people writing the cheques are starting to itemize them.

In this portfolio, technology is the story, and it is a concentrated technology book. Nvidia's record sets the tone for the chip names, including the leveraged semiconductor fund. Apple has an event reportedly set for next week. Adobe is being pitched as a value play despite AI worries. AMD promised OpenAI and Meta 320 million shares for a penny each, which is one way to secure a customer. In consumer cyclical, Lululemon fell 19% on its pivot to selling other brands' leggings. The S&P 500 fund is in the news only because a cheaper twin now exists.

On the names in focus, her advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in three tranches over four to six weeks, funding a new Berkshire Hathaway position from the first one. Healthcare and international exposure would come next. They also want hard rules on SoundHound and Unusual Machines. Intel gets a choice: cut or commit. Limbo is the one option the advisors will not allow, which is a rare policy for a portfolio and a rarer one for a committee.
