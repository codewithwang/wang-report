By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Aug 14

Wall Street's chatter today circled one number: Nvidia, with some on the Street now sizing the company at six trillion dollars by the end of next year, a figure that used to be a rounding error for entire economies. Adobe spent the day explaining a breach instead of a roadmap, after hackers found their way into a critical flaw in its Commerce platform. Vanguard expanded its footprint while the rest of the market argued about valuations.

The portfolio's technology bench is doing most of the talking. Okta drew an upgrade on the theory that AI security spending is about to accelerate, welcome news for a name in the software infrastructure sleeve. AMD is preparing a five billion dollar bond sale to fund its own AI buildout, a reminder that even chipmakers riding the boom still have to borrow to build it. Amazon's decision to train AI on Twitch streams by default, opt out or get used, drew attention in the consumer cyclical corner, less for the strategy than for the consent model. Someone at Amazon decided silence counts as a signature.

Her advisors have been talking succession, not panic. The plan on the table trims Nvidia down from its outsized perch in gradual tranches, recycling part of the proceeds into a new Berkshire Hathaway stake. Healthcare and international exposure are next on the list, still theoretical. On the speculative names, SoundHound and Unusual Machines, the advisors want rules written down now: no adds, half off on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. Intel remains the one holding nobody has decided about.
