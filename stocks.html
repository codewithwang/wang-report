By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Jul 29

Amazon's slide alongside the broader Google-driven tech pullback set the tone, and the tape carried that caution through the session, with the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng all trading without conviction. Rate expectations stayed the backdrop, not the story. What moved markets today was concentration risk in AI-linked names, and that showed up squarely in a portfolio built around exactly those names.

The technology book, the largest and most concentrated part of this portfolio, had a split day. Apple crossed five trillion dollars in market value, only the second company to do so, while AMD fell sharply as traders narrowed the AI chip trade down to a single winner. Adobe posted a record quarter with AI revenue tripling past half a billion dollars. The AI trade still rewards proof of revenue over promise of it. Consumer names traded on their own logic: Lululemon down hard over the past year and drawing early comeback arguments, while the index and energy funds moved with the broader market rather than against it.

Individual names dominated the advisors' attention. NVIDIA remains the conversation, with guidance now leaning toward trimming the position down in stages over the coming weeks, proceeds earmarked to open a new Berkshire Hathaway stake. Okta drew scrutiny after researchers exposed a vishing kit built around its platform. The vulnerability sits in the human layer, not the code. The advisors have also been sketching rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names, and weighing whether Intel deserves a decision rather than more patience.
