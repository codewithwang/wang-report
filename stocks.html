By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Oct 6

The index tape was not available at filing time, so there are no levels to read for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq or the Hang Seng. Rates and the wider economic backdrop were not in today's materials either, and I won't guess at them. What can be said is that the stories moving today concern the cost of the artificial intelligence build-out, not the central banks.

The sectors in this portfolio mostly moved together. Technology, the concentrated core of the book, had a strong day. Nvidia is closing in on a market value no company has reached, and AMD has crossed the trillion mark on demand for processors that run AI agents. One report notes that Vanguard's technology-heavy fund carries far more exposure to the same chip names than the plain S&P 500 fund does, which makes the two funds one trade with different labels. An analyst downgraded Okta, calling the cybersecurity sector overheated. Amazon is reported to be spending as though the company depends on it, which it does.

On the names in focus, her advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in stages over several weeks and using the first tranche to open a Berkshire Hathaway position, then adding healthcare and international exposure. They also want written exit rules for SoundHound and Unusual Machines, and a decision on Intel: cut or commit, no limbo. Apple's patent bill and Burry's defence of his Lululemon sale are noted, and a review of currency exposure and of US estate tax is on the list. Taking profits from the market's favourite stock to buy its least exciting one is a plan. It is also a mood.
