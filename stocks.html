By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Jul 30

Wall Street traded without a clear macro headline today, leaving the tape to sort itself out sector by sector. The bigger story sat inside technology, where a memory-chip earnings miss overseas rippled straight into the AI trade, pulling NVIDIA, Micron and AMD lower even as AMD separately signed a data center pact worth mentioning in its own right. Apple moved the other way entirely, crossing five trillion dollars in value on the same day the AI trade was supposedly being sold, which is either a contradiction or just Tuesday. Adobe touched a two month high on regulatory news, and the index fund tracking the broader market did what it always does: compound quietly while everyone else argues.

Consumer names split. Amazon heads into earnings with AWS growth as the question everyone claims to have already answered, and Lululemon sank to a six year low even as one bank called the new chief executive a reason for optimism, a headline that reads like a eulogy with a silver lining stapled to it. Okta dropped sharply off its highs, and more than one analyst framed the pullback as an entry point rather than a warning sign.

Her advisors have been circling NVIDIA, discussing trimming the position down from its outsized share of the technology book in stages over the coming weeks, with proceeds earmarked to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Further out, they have flagged building a healthcare allocation and adding international exposure, while drafting firm exit rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names. Intel remains an open question the advisors need to resolve one way or the other, and a currency hedge tied to the Hong Kong dollar peg is under review as well.
