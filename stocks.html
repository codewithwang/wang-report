Wall Street closed out the session in a familiar posture: calm on the surface, concentrated underneath. Rate expectations held steady enough that traders found other things to worry about, chiefly how much of the rally now depends on a small handful of names. Chipmakers extended their advance on a fresh round of bullish analyst calls, and the broader tape took its cues from them. Two companies now carry an index. That is either efficient or alarming, and today it managed to read as both.

The portfolio's technology sleeve did the heavy lifting again. Semiconductor exposure benefited from upgraded analyst sentiment, cybersecurity holdings advanced on new cloud partnerships and AI deal flow, and the smaller drone and audio-AI positions posted an overnight spike, the sort that flatters a chart without telling you a thing about the business underneath it. The consumer cyclical and index-fund sleeves moved more quietly, tracking the broader market's mood rather than setting it. Energy exposure sat this one out.

Apple drew attention on unexpected desktop demand tied to AI workloads, this cycle finding yet another corner to touch. The memory name in the technology book got a rare presidential shoutout, not typically how portfolio theses are built, but noted all the same. Her advisors have discussed trimming the largest position in the technology book in stages, using early proceeds to open a position in a diversified holding company, while setting strict, no-exception rules for the more speculative names. Healthcare and international diversification remain under active discussion. Nobody has signed anything yet.
