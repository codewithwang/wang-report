By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Aug 18

Amazon rewrote its terms today to make it harder to sue, one way to reduce litigation risk. Okta got an upgrade from Wells Fargo, who see AI upside the market hasn't priced in, and somebody is about to be right or embarrassed. The broader tape moved with the usual rate-mood undertone, and VOO's four-year winning streak over VTI is reportedly close to breaking. Even index funds have rivalries.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated holding, had a mixed session. NVIDIA backed a hundred-and-five-billion-dollar OpenAI data center financing deal in Ohio, a headline that makes semiconductors sound less like chips and more like infrastructure policy. AMD picked up a design partnership with Google on next-generation AI silicon. Apple is dealing with a wave of spyware alerts hitting its users. An Adobe veteran of twenty-five years was laid off and is now driving a school bus, which is either a personal essay or a labor market indicator. Consumer names were softer: Lululemon lost two more senior executives amid leadership chaos.

On names, the advisors have discussed trimming the NVIDIA position toward a smaller share of the book in staged tranches over the next month and a half, using early proceeds to open a new position in Berkshire Hathaway. They have also flagged building healthcare and international exposure over coming weeks, writing firm exit rules for the portfolio's smaller speculative names, and deciding, one way or the other, what to do with Intel. An FX and estate tax review round out the list.
