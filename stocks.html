By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Aug 2

Good afternoon. NVIDIA reclaimed the title of world's most valuable company today, a crown Apple surrendered the same week Tim Cook warned of a memory shortage that sent the stock down ten percent. One Cupertino warning, one Santa Clara coronation. The macro tape offered less. Major index levels were not cleanly read today, so investors took their signal from single names instead of the broader mood.

Technology carried the portfolio's story, and it was a mixed one. NVIDIA's ascent and AMD's confirmed August price hike on Radeon suggest chipmakers are still setting their own terms on pricing. Apple's slide and a critical, noick vulnerability disclosed in Adobe's Campaign Classic software argued the other side: scale invites scrutiny. Consumer cyclical ran its own split screen. Amazon surged on a standout AWS quarter, while Lululemon sank on doubts about its new CEO, a name Michael Burry has taken to calling a patience trade. Software infrastructure moved on strategy rather than sentiment. Okta's acquisition of Permiso, which extends threat detection beyond identity logs, read as a statement of intent, not just a headline.

On individual names, the advisors have been circling discipline. They have discussed trimming NVIDIA in tranches over the coming weeks and redirecting part of the proceeds toward opening a position in Berkshire Hathaway. They have also flagged the case for adding healthcare and international exposure, and for writing firm rules around the portfolio's smaller speculative names before conviction fades. Intel, the advisors agree, needs a decision, not more patience.
