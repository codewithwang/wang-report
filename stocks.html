By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Sep 3

Markets went without a clear macro print today, so the tape sorted itself out on stock-specific news instead of the usual rate chatter. Big Tech carried the mood. Nvidia extended its climb as its AI customer base kept widening. Adobe pushed further into workflow tools by wiring Photoshop and Firefly directly into Slack. And Okta's stock has doubled this year on the AI agent boom, which is either a remarkable product story or proof that "AI agent" now works as a magic word on an earnings call. AMD drew fresh attention after Stanley Druckenmiller opened a new position, the kind of headline that does half the marketing department's job for free.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated holding, sat at the center of the day's action. The consumer cyclical sleeve absorbed news that Amazon faces FTC claims it overcharged advertisers for years, even as Amazon and Lululemon both prepare for incoming chief executives this month. Apple added its own leadership headline: Tim Cook is stepping down, with Ternus taking over. Through it all, the fixed income sleeve, held via the Hong Kong Government iBond, stayed the quiet ballast.

Her advisors have been discussing trimming the Nvidia position down from its outsized share, doing it in stages over the coming weeks, and using the proceeds to open a new Berkshire Hathaway stake. Next up for consideration: adding healthcare exposure, international diversification, firm exit rules on the portfolio's more speculative names, and a decision on Intel one way or the other.

Limbo, the advisors note, is not a position.
