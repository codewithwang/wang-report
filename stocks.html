By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Aug 7

Amazon and Apple carried today's newsflow, and the tape gave them room to move. The Vanguard S&P 500 sleeve, still the book's largest concentration, remains a proxy for a market that keeps getting called a tech bet in disguise, and this book is not the one to argue.

Semiconductors stayed the center of gravity. Nvidia is said to be reconsidering memory specs for its next architecture, and AMD moved to fold AI model design directly into chip silicon through an acquisition. The leveraged semiconductor sleeve and the energy sector fund round out a portfolio still built on compute.

Software infrastructure had a rougher session. Okta underperformed the broader market, while Adobe found a new growth angle by wiring its creative suite directly into ChatGPT. Consumer names split down the middle: Amazon cut the price of GLP-1 drugs through its pharmacy arm for Medicare patients, a genuine expansion of reach, and Lululemon's Georgia store lost seventeen thousand dollars in merchandise to a ram raid, which is one way to hit a same-store sales target.

Apple drew a legal headline as OpenAI moved to dismiss its trade secrets suit, a dispute the portfolio is watching, not trading. The advisors have been busier than the tape lets on. They are trimming the Nvidia position in stages over the coming weeks and using the proceeds to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also adding healthcare and international exposure, drafting firm exit rules for the portfolio's smaller speculative names, and weighing what to do with Intel, calling the position untenable as it stands.
