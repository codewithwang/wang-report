By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Oct 1

The market tape is blank this evening. The feeds carried no readings for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq or the Hang Seng, so there is no honest number to give you on where the indices closed or how the rate mood is leaning. What can be said is that the conversation is about artificial intelligence spending, and nearly every headline touching this portfolio traces back to it.

That shows most clearly in technology, the dominant sector in this book. Apple rose on a report of a smart home hub for its October 13 event. Adobe surged as AI partnerships piled up. Amazon lifted its 2026 capital spending guide to $220 billion, and AMD agreed to buy World Labs for $8.2 billion. Okta is pitching its AI-agent framework as an open industry standard. Elsewhere, Lululemon and Chip Wilson pushed back the board seat deadline again, which is now less a deadline than a tradition. The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF drew a warning signal for tech-heavy portfolios.

On the names, Wall Street is pushing back on Nvidia's plan to have its chips finance the AI boom, and the advisors have already discussed trimming that position, sold in stages over several weeks. They would use the first of those proceeds to open Berkshire Hathaway Class B. Next in line are healthcare and international exposure. The advisors also want hard rules on SoundHound and Unusual Machines, and a decision on Intel: cut or commit, no limbo. Limbo, one notes, is the one position no broker charges a fee to hold.
