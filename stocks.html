By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Aug 1

Amazon led the tape higher after Andy Jassy made the case for AI spending to a market that has spent all year demanding exactly that justification. The rest of the session was less accommodating. NVIDIA slipped even after Jensen Huang made an unscheduled appearance at a Morgan Stanley roadshow to reassure investors directly, a level of hands-on customer service that did not move the stock. Apple shed close to half a trillion dollars in value on a soft forecast. Adobe fell nearly six percent to the cheapest multiple in its peer group.

Technology carried the portfolio's weight today, and not gently. The chip and software names that anchor the book, NVIDIA, AMD, Adobe, Micron, Palantir among them, traded with the same nerves as the broader semiconductor tape, while the leveraged semiconductor exposure amplified every move in both directions. Consumer names split hard: Amazon's rally offset a Lululemon slide of nearly forty percent that has investors debating whether the retailer is a value opportunity or a warning sign. Okta bought its way into AI security with a Permiso acquisition. Even the software desk is spending like the chip desk now.

Her advisors are trimming the largest technology position in stages over the coming weeks, redirecting part of the proceeds into a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also discussing building out healthcare and international exposure over the same window, drawing firmer rules around the portfolio's smaller speculative names, and weighing what to finally do with Intel, which one advisor put simply: cut it or commit to it, but stop leaving it in limbo.
