By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Aug 16

Amazon's arbitration clause and NVIDIA's trimmed OpenAI guarantee are both, in their own way, the sound of a company deciding it does not want to be sued as much. The broader tape held its footing today, with the S&P and Nasdaq treading water and money still finding its way into index funds, SPY leading the ETF inflow tables for the week. Rate mood stayed muted, no fireworks from the macro desk, which left individual names to do the talking.

Technology carried this portfolio's weight, and it carried it unevenly. AMD jumped on a debt deal that priced clean, semiconductors got a lift, and the leveraged chip fund rode along. NVIDIA cooled after word it had quietly scaled back its data center guarantee to OpenAI, a reminder that even the biggest compute story in the market has fine print. Apple faces fresh pressure from Washington over Chinese memory chips, a supply chain headache with no easy substitution. Consumer names split hard. Lululemon craters on brand backlash timed badly against an incoming CEO, while Amazon presses its legal advantage over shoppers rather than its retail one.

Her advisors have been busy. The near-term plan trims NVIDIA down to a smaller share of the book across several tranches over the coming weeks, funding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway from the first sale. Waiting in the wings: healthcare exposure through UNH or JNJ, some international diversification, and hard rules for the portfolio's more speculative names, no adds, discipline on doubles. Intel remains a decision deferred, not a decision made.
