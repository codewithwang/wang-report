By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Aug 19

Nasdaq futures aside, the tape today came without its usual headline act. No fresh print on the S&P, the Nasdaq, or the Hang Seng to anchor the mood, so investors traded on company news instead of the macro script. A mercy, frankly. Bond yields did the talking for the rate crowd, spiking enough to knock the chip complex around and remind everyone that semiconductors answer to the ten-year before they answer to anything else.

Technology carried both the week's optimism and its bruises. Nvidia found a crack of daylight in China, where regulators eased the path for H200 chip sales. AMD sat in the group getting hit alongside Nvidia and Broadcom on the yield spike, proof that even the best product roadmap answers to the bond market first. Adobe surged on a large new position from BlackRock. Consumer names split down the middle: Amazon expanded its drone delivery ambitions toward a million-order run rate, while Lululemon fell on a soft outlook paired with legal trouble, the sort of pairing that ages a stock overnight.

Apple settled its App Store dispute with the EU by overhauling its fees, closing a chapter that had run long. Okta drew a price target increase from Jefferies.

The advisors have been discussing trimming the portfolio's largest position, Nvidia, in stages over the coming weeks, and using the proceeds to open a new position in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also weighing a healthcare allocation, an international fund add, firmer rules around the portfolio's more speculative names, and a decision on Intel. Commit or cut. No more sitting on the fence.
