By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Sep 2

Wall Street spent the session negotiating with itself over what the bond market was actually saying, and the answer arrived as a chip selloff. AMD, Intel, and NVIDIA slid together as yields pressured the names that had done most of the carrying this year. The broader tape absorbed it without panic. That, too, is a verdict.

The portfolio's concentrated technology book felt that directly. The fixed income sleeve and the energy allocation sat apart from the chip drama entirely, and consumer names split on their own news rather than the macro. The index fund holding now sits inside a trillion-dollar vehicle that Wall Street keeps noting has never been this concentrated in the same handful of mega-caps driving the chip trade. Diversification and concentration have quietly become the same bet.

Apple confirmed a change at the very top. Tim Cook is stepping aside, Ternus is taking the chief executive chair, and it is a transition of considerably more consequence than the usual reshuffle. NVIDIA's position was already under review before Anthropic's financing arrangement raised eyebrows over money moving in a circle between the two companies. The advisors have discussed trimming that stake in stages toward a smaller share of the book, redirecting some of the proceeds into a new position in Berkshire Hathaway.

They have also flagged Okta's newly bullish coverage, floated adding healthcare and international exposure in the coming weeks, and proposed firm discipline on the portfolio's more speculative names: no further buying, and clear rules for when to sell.

Intel remains open. The advisors say it deserves a decision, not more waiting.
