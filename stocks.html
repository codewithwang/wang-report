The macro tape is the one thing this desk cannot read today. The feeds for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq and the Hang Seng came through blank, so there is no index level to report and no rate mood to attribute. The headlines are unanimous: artificial intelligence, from Nvidia closing in on a record valuation to Amazon weighing a large chip purchase as AI costs mount.

The portfolio's sectors tell that story from different angles. Technology, the largest and most concentrated part of the book, rides the chip rally, with Nvidia at a record high while AMD's share grants to OpenAI and Meta draw attention. Consumer Cyclical had a rougher session: Lululemon fell 19 percent before pivoting to selling other brands' leggings. Software Infrastructure faces a downgrade on Okta, with the call that cybersecurity looks overheated. The index fund holds steady, though one report notes a rival ETF is far heavier in the same chip names. A company that falls 19 percent and responds by stocking the competition has, at least, diversified.

On individual names, her advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in three tranches over four to six weeks, using the proceeds to open a Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Next, they would add a healthcare name such as UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, and an international fund. They have also floated hard rules for SoundHound and Unusual Machines, and a cut-or-commit decision on Intel. Adobe draws a value pitch, and Apple's event is reportedly set for next week.
