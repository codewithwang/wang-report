By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Aug 29

Amazon rose on analyst upgrades linking AI to shopping behavior, and semiconductor names surged premarket after a blowout Nvidia report lifted the whole chip complex. Wall Street cheered AI infrastructure spend and stayed nervous about what happens when a company that dominates a category pauses its own deal-making. Nvidia did exactly that today, halting revenue-sharing arrangements with AI cloud partners. Even the biggest name in the trade can still move the tape by changing its mind.

Inside this portfolio, the technology sleeve, still the largest position by a wide margin, ran at two temperatures. Chip exposure ran hot, with AMD catching the Nvidia updraft and leverage amplifying every tick. Software infrastructure ran hotter: Okta jumped sharply on earnings, investors deciding that a company selling protection against AI-enabled threats is a good place to hide from AI-enabled threats. Consumer names were mixed. Amazon's AI tailwind sat awkwardly next to Lululemon, where a leadership transition has the market bracing for a lowered outlook.

Apple held attention on product news rather than price action. The iPhone Air drew praise despite soft early sales, a review that amounts to: the phone is important and nobody is buying it. Adobe's day was more sobering. A twenty-five-year veteran was laid off and is now driving a school bus, a detail the earnings call will not mention. Her advisors have been trimming the Nvidia stake in stages while opening a position in Berkshire Hathaway, weighing new healthcare and international allocations, and setting firmer rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names.
