By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Aug 9

Apple opened its Mac lineup in China to Alibaba's Qwen AI today, a small line in the filings that says more about where the growth story lives now than any index move does. The major benchmarks stayed quiet, but under the surface software names took a real hit, Adobe among them, dragged down after Figma flagged the cost of running all this AI in the first place. Someone, somewhere, is finally getting the bill for the technology everyone agreed to be excited about.

The portfolio's technology sleeve, the largest book here by a wide margin, told two different stories this week. The chip side got a jolt from a SpaceX-Nvidia supply deal that rattled the neocloud operators who'd assumed they were next in line, while the software infrastructure side saw a vote of confidence, Fidelity disclosing a stake near twenty million shares in Okta. Consumer names moved on their own logic entirely: Amazon pushed through a data center approval without the public vote critics wanted, and Lululemon spent the week fighting theft rings rather than tariffs, for once.

Her advisors have been circling the concentration question, and the plan is now current: trim the Nvidia position down in stages over the coming weeks, and use the early proceeds to open a position in Berkshire Hathaway, a deliberate step toward ballast. Healthcare and international exposure are next on the calendar for the coming month. And the advisors want firm exit rules written now, before conviction, for the portfolio's smallest and most speculative names, so nobody has to decide anything under pressure later.
