From the desk · Daily read
By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Jul 30

Trading was thin on the macro read today. No clean signal from the S&P 500, Nasdaq, or Hang Seng to hang a headline on, which left the semiconductor complex to do the talking after SK Hynix missed earnings and dragged Nvidia, AMD, and Micron lower in sympathy. The AI trade now moves as one crowded room, not several separate stocks. Software fared better: Adobe touched a two month high after the FTC fast tracked its Topaz Labs deal, and several desks are now calling for the S&P 500 to run another eight to twenty percent by year end.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated position, took the semiconductor selloff directly on the chin. AMD offset some of the damage with a fourteen billion dollar data center agreement with Core Scientific, and Okta gave back recent gains, though analysts called the pullback a buying opportunity rather than a warning. Consumer names had a rougher day. Amazon confirmed it is scaling back its AI division after what the company called sustained failure. Lululemon joined Nike in a lawsuit over discount practices regulators call deceptive. Apple drew a warning shot from senators over its use of Chinese memory chips.

Her advisors have been busier than the market. They are trimming Nvidia down from its outsized share of the book in staged tranches over the coming weeks, using the proceeds to open a new position in Berkshire Hathaway, sensible money's version of decaf. Healthcare and international exposure are next, alongside firm exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names. Intel is the one decision still on the desk, and the advisors say it needs an answer soon.
