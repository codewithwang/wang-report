By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Oct 7

The macro tape is thin this morning. Index readings for the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng were not available at filing, so there is no honest number to give you, and no read on rates either. The headlines do show money still flowing into artificial intelligence. Nvidia is closing in on a $6 trillion valuation after a record high, and AMD has printed a fresh record of its own.

Technology is carrying the day, which matters for a concentrated technology book. Okta, in software infrastructure, reached a three year high on agentic AI enthusiasm. Consumer cyclical is split. Lululemon plunged 17% on a growth warning and a securities fraud probe, while Amazon is putting autoplay video ads on Echo Shows, which turns the kitchen counter into a bus shelter. Adobe is under pressure, with analysts debating how much further it can fall. Apple is teaming with LG on smart home products. The leveraged semiconductor fund rides the same tide as Nvidia and AMD, in both directions. Energy and fixed income had no headlines of note.

On the names in focus, her advisors have been plain about Nvidia. They want it trimmed toward a smaller share of the portfolio and sold in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Next in line, they discussed adding healthcare through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, plus international exposure through VXUS. SoundHound and Unusual Machines get hard rules: no adds, sell half on a double, exit fully if the thesis breaks. Intel gets a decision, cut or commit. The advisors also raised a currency review and a US estate tax check.
