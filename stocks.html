By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Aug 22

Nvidia is trimming a stake from the technology book. The advisors set the exit in tranches over the coming weeks, and the first proceeds are already earmarked for a new position in Berkshire Hathaway. Call it a fund manager reading the room rather than a fund manager losing his nerve. Nvidia gave the market its own reason to stay interested, paying six billion dollars and poaching over a hundred staff from Poolside, an AI deal large enough that "blockbuster" barely covers it. Adobe added music, voice and sound to its Firefly studio: a small extension of the same story, the AI arms race moving from who has the best model to who has the most senses.

The rest of the technology book is doing its usual work of cancelling itself out. Apple gutted its Siri and Vision Pro teams in fresh layoffs, the sort of housekeeping a company does quietly and calls restructuring. Okta won a two hundred million dollar vote of confidence, proof enterprise security still sells in a jumpy market. Intel remains the name nobody wants to defend out loud, Stanley Druckenmiller among them, having dumped both Intel and Micron in favor of AI robotics. The advisors are treating Intel as a decision, not a mood: cut it or commit to it.

Elsewhere the discipline is administrative rather than dramatic. The advisors are drafting hard exit rules for the portfolio's smaller, speculative names, sketching a healthcare allocation, and weighing a slice of international exposure. Unglamorous plumbing. It is also the difference between a concentrated book and a liability.
