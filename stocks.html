Fed funds stayed parked, traders parsing every syllable for a cut, but the tape held its footing. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq traded steady, the Hang Seng joined the calm, and nothing in the macro print gave anyone reason to move. Call the rate mood patient, watchful, unresolved.

The portfolio's center of gravity is still technology, and that book moved in step with the broader chip and software complex, amplified further by the leveraged semiconductor sleeve. Consumer names tracked retail's usual chop, with software infrastructure holding steadier than the rest. Energy and the index fund sleeves offered ballast, and the government iBond did what fixed income is supposed to do: nothing dramatic, on purpose.

Most of today's conversation centered on NVIDIA, still the largest position in the technology book. The advisors decided to trim it in stages over the coming weeks rather than all at once, redirecting part of the proceeds into a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They flagged healthcare and international exposure as areas to build out gradually, and named Intel a position that needs a decision, not a holding pattern. On the more speculative end, the advisors want exit rules written down now, before the next swing makes that harder.

Portfolio management with its eyes open. Fed funds parked, NVIDIA getting trimmed, and somewhere a spreadsheet just got a new tab for Intel.
