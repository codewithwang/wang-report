From the desk · Daily read
By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Oct 9

The macro tape is thin this evening. The desk has no readings for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq or the Hang Seng, and nothing on rates or the economy worth quoting, so there is no call on direction. The portfolio is a concentrated technology book with a Hong Kong government bond for ballast, and a move in chips will hit it harder than a move in the broad market.

Technology is carrying the news flow. Apple is reported to have cut iPhone 18 Pro orders on soft demand. Nvidia has joined six financial giants to fund AI compute, which makes the world's leading chip supplier its lender of last resort as well. AMD is courting Samsung's foundry to diversify its manufacturing. Adobe is watching a developer clone its software for free. In consumer cyclical, Lululemon has hired a former Nike chief executive to rebuild its leadership, and Amazon's Alexa+ shopping tool faces its first big October test. Okta, in software infrastructure, is up 161% in a run, and the stock now has to justify the price.

Her advisors have said the largest technology position, Nvidia, should be trimmed in three tranches over several weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B stake. Healthcare and international exposure come next, along with fixed rules for SoundHound and Unusual Machines, with no adding. On Intel, they want a decision, cut or commit, and no limbo. Limbo, to be fair, is the one position no one has ever been asked to size.
