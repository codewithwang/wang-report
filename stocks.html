By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Aug 27

Wall Street's rally showed no sign of fatigue, index makers printing fresh highs on a tape now carrying the kind of altitude that S&P 500 historians tend to flag rather than celebrate. Semiconductors led the noise: government cash commitments sent AMD, Intel and Micron sharply higher, Intel's own rally wrapped in commentary about Washington now holding equity in the company it once merely regulated. Software told the opposite story, with AI anxiety hitting Adobe and its peers as investors ask which programs survive being automated by the thing they sell.

Inside this book, the growth engine and the AI infrastructure trade are pulling in different directions today. Amazon's commitment to two million Nvidia chips reinforced the AI buildout thesis that underwrites the technology sleeve, and Nvidia's own move to acquire Hugging Face extended that story further up the stack. Apple drew a smaller, stranger headline: users grumbling about ads inside Maps, hardly a threat to the franchise, but a reminder that monetization creep tests goodwill. Lululemon fell hard on a board shakeup and a soft outlook, the one clean loser of the session.

Her advisors are trimming the largest position, Nvidia, in stages over the coming weeks, using the proceeds to open a position in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also planning a healthcare allocation, international exposure through Vanguard's ex-US fund, and firm exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names. Intel remains a decision still pending, not one they're willing to leave undecided indefinitely.
