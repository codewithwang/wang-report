By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Sep 1

Markets held a familiar shape today. The major US benchmarks and the Hang Seng traded without a clean headline to explain themselves, which in this business counts as a good day. Rate mood stayed the dominant undertone. The market's real conversation continues to happen inside a handful of technology names large enough to move the index by themselves, and the fund world is now openly worried about that concentration: roughly a third of the S&P 500 sits in ten companies. That is either efficient markets at work or one very crowded elevator.

Technology carried the portfolio's weather today, and the chip and infrastructure names moved on real news rather than sentiment. Nvidia's ecosystem got a jolt from a large cloud computing commitment tied to Anthropic. Adobe advanced an AI licensing arrangement in the Gulf. AMD drew fresh buy calls from Wall Street desks after its post-earnings pullback. Software security had the sharpest single move of the day. Energy and the broader index sleeve traded quietly in the background, doing the unglamorous work index funds are paid to do.

Okta was the standout, surging on earnings that beat expectations and on demand tied to securing AI agents, a category that barely existed a year ago. Apple drew attention less for the stock than for a leadership change atop its App Store business. Lululemon heads into earnings this week under a cloud of caution.

On positioning, the advisors have discussed trimming the Nvidia stake gradually across several tranches, using early proceeds to open a position in Berkshire Hathaway, and building out healthcare and international exposure in the weeks ahead. They are also drafting firm exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names. On Intel, they want a decision, one way or the other.
