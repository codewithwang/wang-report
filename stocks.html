By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Aug 30

Wall Street starts the week with no fresh macro print to lean on, and the tape is treating that silence the way it treats most silence lately, as an excuse to drift higher on hope. Rate mood stays gently optimistic. Chip demand is doing the heavy lifting for growth conviction, and the S&P 500 is quietly finding a cheaper twin: fifty seven billion dollars just moved into a lower fee rival ETF rather than the original.

Technology is the loudest room in the portfolio, and it is having a loud week for contradictory reasons. Semiconductor exposure got a jolt of confidence when Nvidia committed nearly thirteen billion dollars to Hugging Face, its largest deal yet, even as AMD slid on no clear catalyst, the kind of unexplained drop that sends traders hunting for a reason after the fact. Software infrastructure had its own moment, with Okta surging as identity tools ride the AI agent wave. Consumer cyclical was mixed to soft. Lululemon absorbed a new chief executive and a lowered outlook on the same day. Energy and the leveraged semiconductor sleeve moved with the broader chip narrative rather than against it.

Apple handed the keys to a new chief executive just ahead of what Wall Street traditionally treats as its roughest month. Bold or accidental, the market has not decided which. The advisors have discussed trimming the largest position in the technology book, Nvidia, in stages over the coming weeks, and redirecting part of that toward a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also weighing a healthcare allocation, an international fund add, firmer exit rules on the portfolio's smaller speculative names, and a decision on Intel one way or the other.
