The tape arrives without index levels today. The S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng readings were not available at filing, so there is no honest number to read you. What the day does offer is mood. The broad US market gets a modest lift, with the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF up 0.4% as Palantir swings the index, and AI valuation nerves keep surfacing. Nvidia-backed Firmus scrapped a $5.5 billion IPO, which is a polite way of saying buyers asked for a discount and the sellers declined to attend.

The sectors in this portfolio pull in different directions. Technology, the dominant theme, is split. Apple fell 3% on reports of trimmed iPhone 18 Pro component orders, and AMD slid despite record revenue, a rare case of a company being punished for its own good news. Software infrastructure is firmer: Okta climbs on AI security demand and stronger guidance. In consumer cyclical, Lululemon is hunting a turnaround, while Amazon is invoking national security to ease local resistance to its AI data centers. Adobe has the odd headline, with its Creative Suite reportedly reverse engineered and posted online.

Her advisors are focused on concentration. They have discussed trimming Nvidia in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Next in line are a healthcare addition, either UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, and international exposure through VXUS. The advisors also want hard rules on SoundHound and Unusual Machines, and a decision on Intel: cut or commit, no limbo. A currency review and a US estate tax check are also on the list.
