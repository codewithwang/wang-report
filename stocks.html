From the desk · Daily read
By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Sep 25

Markets slowed into the long weekend, and Wall Street mostly stood around waiting for someone else to move first. Desks read it as patience rather than conviction: no fresh catalyst on rates, no scramble in either direction, index action that amounted to a shrug both ways. Traders are pricing the same rate mood they have priced for weeks, cautiously optimistic and in no hurry to prove it.

Inside the portfolio, concentration is meeting diversification in real time. The technology book remains the largest and most crowded corner of the sheet, anchored by the chip and AI-adjacent names. The index and energy funds keep doing the quieter work of ballast. Fixed income, in the form of the HK Government iBond, sits apart from all of it, not every instrument here is meant to be exciting. Consumer names and the lone software infrastructure holding round out a book that is, by design, heavier in one direction than most.

NVIDIA is generating the most conversation this week. The advisors have discussed trimming the position down to a smaller share of the book, staged across several tranches over the coming weeks, with proceeds earmarked to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Beyond that, they have flagged healthcare and international exposure as areas to build out gradually, and drawn a hard line on the portfolio's more speculative names: no further adds, and firm rules for when to trim or walk away entirely. Intel remains the one holding that gets a decision this week, not another round of debate.
