By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Oct 10

The index numbers are not in hand this evening. The S&P 500, the Nasdaq and the Hang Seng all came through as unavailable, so the tape stays unread and nothing here will pretend otherwise. The day's headlines leaned on single names and company news rather than on rates or the broader economy. Big Tech split in the process, with one software name jumping while a rival slid on an immigration ban. That is the macro picture for now, and it is thinner than usual.

The sectors in this portfolio told different stories. Technology, the dominant sleeve by a wide margin, had a mixed session. Apple sank on reports that iPhone 18 Pro component orders were being cut, Adobe jumped, and AMD made an acquisition to push into physical-world AI. In software infrastructure, Okta climbed on upbeat guidance as investors piled into AI security. Consumer cyclical drew attention for Amazon's AI agent ban and a bullish case on Lululemon. The broad index fund drew only a long-term holder's defense. A concentrated technology book rises and falls together, until it doesn't.

Among the names in focus, Nvidia is being eyed as the first ten trillion dollar company, which is flattering for a position her advisors want smaller. They have discussed trimming it in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B stake. Healthcare and international exposure are next on their list, along with firm rules for SoundHound and Unusual Machines. The advisors also want a decision on Intel, cut or commit, with no limbo. Limbo, to be fair, is the one position every committee eventually holds.
