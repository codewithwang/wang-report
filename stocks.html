By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Aug 28

Amazon's headline growth math tripped the market today. Two million more GPUs, and the tape treated it as spending, not strategy. VOO traded quietly by comparison, the kind of session that lets a passive core do its job without commentary. The market cannot tell the difference between capital expenditure and capitulation, and today it did not try.

Software carried the desk. Adobe advanced alongside a broader rally in the group, and Okta had its best session in recent memory, up nearly thirty percent as buyers decided that a company selling identity protection deserves a premium precisely because everyone is worried about identity theft. The chip complex split. Nvidia's acquisition of Hugging Face reset the AI narrative even as a prominent growth investor trimmed a large AMD stake, and the semiconductor-adjacent names in the portfolio took their cue from that division.

Apple trimmed jobs in the South Bay. Lululemon drew a warning from UBS over softening demand in two of its largest markets. Her advisors have been busiest around the technology book: trimming the Nvidia position in stages over the coming weeks, using early proceeds to open a stake in Berkshire Hathaway, and drafting exit rules for the portfolio's smaller, more speculative names. Healthcare and international exposure are under consideration for addition. Intel remains a name the advisors say needs a decision, not a defense.
