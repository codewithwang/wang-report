Wall Street opened the week without much of a story to tell itself. That is its own kind of story. Rates are still the quiet variable sitting under every index move, and the major benchmarks are trading in the narrow band traders call consolidation and everyone else calls nothing happening yet. Inflows kept coming into broad market funds regardless, proof that when in doubt, money still buys the index.

Inside this portfolio, technology is doing the talking, and not gently. Nvidia is drawing scrutiny after quietly trimming the scale of a much-discussed data center commitment to OpenAI. Adobe is fending off a zero day already under active attack. A decade-old flaw in AMD chips has researchers describing full hardware control as up for grabs. Consumer names offered a lighter note, with Amazon's Jeff Bezos joining a consortium buying into Liverpool FC, a reminder that even the world's most efficient logistics mind occasionally just wants a football club.

Her advisors have been busy. The plan now underway trims Nvidia down from the portfolio's largest position in staged sales over the coming weeks, using the early proceeds to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Waiting in the wings are a healthcare allocation and an international fund, along with firmer rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names. Intel remains the one the advisors say cannot stay undecided much longer.
