Wall Street opened without a print from the major indices today, and that silence didn't stop anyone from talking. Sentiment leaned on chip supply chains, where Micron's run toward the thousand-dollar mark has traders asking whether Apple is next in line for an AI re-rating, and on unease that surfaced first overseas, where Indian banking stocks logged a third straight losing session. Rate mood stayed in the background, doing what it always does: nothing, loudly.

The portfolio's technology block, still its most concentrated exposure, carried the day's real signal. NVIDIA's chief executive raised his multi-year sales forecast to a trillion dollars, a number that used to be a punchline and is now a guidance range. Software infrastructure got its own lift, with Okta upgraded and its price target raised, while Adobe drew attention for the opposite reason: a commerce platform flaw under active exploitation, which has nothing to do with earnings and everything to do with patch schedules. Consumer names split down the middle. Amazon drew a bullish long-range call on its cloud business, while Lululemon lost its AI chief eleven months into the job, proof that some transformations move faster than the transformers.

Her advisors have been trimming the largest position in the book, NVIDIA, in stages, redirecting early proceeds toward opening a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Discussion has also turned to building out healthcare and international exposure over coming weeks, setting firm exit rules on the portfolio's smaller speculative names, and resolving where Intel sits. In or out, with no more middle ground.
