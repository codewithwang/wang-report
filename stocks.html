By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Jul 28

Amazon and Microsoft each committed roughly $400 billion toward AI infrastructure this week, and the market re-rated the entire chip complex on top of it. Apple retook the title of world's most valuable company from Nvidia, which is itself reportedly in talks to backstop $250 billion of OpenAI's data center financing. Eighteen months ago that sentence would have read as satire. Adobe jumped as Wall Street decided its AI growth case pencils out, and AMD restructured its next-generation server chip lineup into three families, wagering the company's near-term identity on AI demand holding.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated exposure, sits at the center of that trade, alongside a leveraged semiconductor position that amplifies every one of these headlines in both directions. Okta drew a fresh bullish call from at least one analyst who thinks its rally still has room to run. Lululemon did not have as good a week. Texas regulators opened a probe into toxic chemicals in its activewear, a reminder that consumer names carry their own headline risk without a chip shortage anywhere in sight.

Her advisors have been trimming the largest technology position down over several weeks, redirecting part of the proceeds into a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also planning to build out healthcare and international allocations in the coming weeks, and have proposed firm exit rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names. Conviction, not hope, will decide when those trades end.
