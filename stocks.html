From the desk · Daily read
By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Aug 27

Wall Street stayed quiet today, no fresh print on the S&P, the Nasdaq or the Hang Seng, but the earnings tape did the talking instead. Nvidia doubled revenue to ninety six billion dollars and cleared every estimate on the desk, sending cash flows rippling into AMD, Intel and Micron overnight. Adobe, meanwhile, got caught in the blast radius of an Intuit selloff. Guilt by sector association, which is its own kind of Wall Street justice.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest concentration by a wide margin, is having a mixed morning under all that chip enthusiasm. Consumer cyclical names felt more of it. Lululemon slid on a warning out of Dick's that also rattled Nike, proof that a bad retail read travels fast and rarely travels kindly. Software infrastructure had the standout move, with Okta surging on the theory that identity security is the next AI arms race, a thesis that seems to strengthen every time a headline puts the words "threat" and "artificial intelligence" in the same sentence.

Individually, Amazon tripled down on its AI buildout with an order for two million Nvidia chips, while Apple trimmed engineering headcount at headquarters even as its own chip suppliers rallied. The advisors have flagged Nvidia for trimming toward a smaller share of the book over several weeks, with proceeds seeding a new Berkshire position. They're watching Intel closely too, favoring a decision over further limbo.
