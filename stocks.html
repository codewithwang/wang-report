By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Aug 5

Bezos is filing to sell four billion dollars of Amazon stock. The market noted it and moved on, which is its own kind of verdict on where sentiment sits. Chipmakers had a rougher day: AMD's earnings drew a sharp selloff on worries that the problem runs deeper than one quarter, while Musk committed SpaceX's chip business exclusively to Nvidia and started talking about space-based AI servers, which is either the future of computing or the most expensive line item in the history of aerospace.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated holding, is trading through that split screen. Nvidia strength on the Musk news, AMD weakness on its own results, and Adobe now a security story as much as a software one, with fake Adobe and Zoom update files being used to plant remote access malware in the wild. Consumer names carry their own subplots: Lululemon reportedly at odds with itself internally, and Okta shoring up its identity business with the Permiso acquisition. Vanguard's own index confirms how concentrated "diversified" has become, with seven stocks now accounting for well over a quarter of the S&P 500 fund.

Her advisors are trimming the Nvidia position in tranches over the coming weeks and redirecting the first proceeds into a new Berkshire Hathaway stake. They're also adding healthcare and international exposure, drafting hard exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names, and deciding, finally, what to do with Intel. No more limbo, they say. Apple, for its part, is now seeking an injunction against OpenAI in a trade secrets fight. Even the lawsuits have roadmaps.
