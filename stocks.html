By Charmaine LoNews AnchorMon, Aug 31

Wall Street's math today is doing more heavy lifting than the ledger admits. Two names are carrying the S&P to new highs while the rest of the index rides along, and Nvidia has managed the rare feat of posting a ninety six billion dollar quarter and getting treated like a company with a supply problem. Because it has one. Chips are the story investors cannot stop telling, and the analyst community spent the session upgrading AMD in the sort of wave that suggests conviction, or at least a shared spreadsheet.

Within the portfolio, technology is where the action lives, and it split cleanly. Semiconductor sentiment stayed firm on Nvidia's results even with the capacity constraint attached, and AMD rode the upgrade wave higher. Security and enterprise software had their own good day, Okta among them, as the market rewards anything that can plausibly claim an AI security angle. Consumer cyclical was the discount bin: Lululemon took a real hit on brand and product concerns under a new chief executive, while Amazon absorbed a round of Wall Street rotation toward other AI winners, cooling rather than collapsing. Apple made news of a different kind, a leadership handoff to Ternus with AI now the stated top priority, a phrase companies reach for right before they explain why it wasn't the priority sooner.

Her advisors have been unusually specific this week. The instruction is to trim the largest position, the concentrated Nvidia holding, down toward a smaller share of the technology book, selling in stages over the coming month and using the proceeds to open a new position in Berkshire Hathaway. Under discussion for the near term: a healthcare allocation, some international diversification, and exit rules for the portfolio's smaller speculative names. Intel remains an open question the advisors want closed one way or the other.
