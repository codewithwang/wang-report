By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Jul 31

Amazon's AI spending is now showing up as negative free cash flow, even with revenue accelerating, and the market has not decided whether that is discipline or the first crack. Apple lost six percent on a supply crunch warning, a reminder that hardware still sits on a factory floor somewhere. Semiconductors took the worse of it: SK Hynix's results set off a broad AI trade unwind, and the chip complex fell together.

The technology sleeve, the largest and most concentrated part of this book, felt that unwind directly. NVIDIA, Micron and AMD moved in lockstep off a single Korean earnings print, which tells you how much of the sector is now trading as one name. Software fared better. Okta bought an AI security company for roughly two hundred million dollars, and Adobe touched a two month high after regulators cleared its Topaz Labs deal. Consumer names were mixed, and Lululemon is facing a lawsuit over discount practices that were, on the evidence so far, more theoretical than actual.

Her advisors are trimming the NVIDIA position in stages over the coming weeks and redirecting part of the proceeds into a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also building out healthcare and international exposure, and writing firm rules for the portfolio's smaller speculative names, so a double is no longer a decision made on the fly. Intel remains open, and the advisors intend to close it one way or the other. A currency review is on the list too, treating the Hong Kong dollar allocation as insurance against peg stress, not a trade.
