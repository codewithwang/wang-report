By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Oct 9

The index numbers did not arrive this morning, so the tape is blank for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq and the Hang Seng. The mood is cautious. Okta climbed while the broader market slipped, and one headline calls Nvidia's wobble a critical warning sign for the AI trade. A tape with no numbers and a wobble is still a tape.

Technology is where the portfolio lives, and today it pulls in different directions. AMD slid despite record revenue as data center demand surged, a creative way to be punished for good news. Intel's foundry hopes took a hit after Musk turned Terafab toward TSMC, though its chief vows to stay in the game. Amazon is retiring its Fire tablets for Alexa+ Android slates. Lululemon has rebuilt its top team with former Nike hands, and Jefferies says work remains. Apple has sent invitations for an October 13 smart home event. Adobe is in the news after a developer cloned Photoshop and Premiere for free following a cancellation fee dispute. The fixed income, energy, index and leveraged semiconductor holdings had no headlines today.

Her advisors have been plain about Nvidia. They discussed trimming it toward a smaller share of a concentrated technology book, selling in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Soon, they would add a healthcare name, either UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, plus international exposure through VXUS, and set hard rules for SoundHound and Unusual Machines: no adds, half off on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. On Intel, the advice is to cut or commit, no limbo. They also raised an FX review and a US estate tax review.
