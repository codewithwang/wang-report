By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Sep 3

Markets stayed calm enough today that the loudest number in the building came from a lawsuit. The Federal Trade Commission sued Amazon, alleging advertisers were overcharged for years, a claim large enough to make "trust the algorithm" sound like a punchline rather than a pitch. Elsewhere the tape offered its usual mix of triumph and asterisk. Apple did something it hasn't managed in two decades. AMD drew a call from a Fool prediction placing it in the two trillion dollar club by 2030, a forecast with roughly the shelf life of produce.

Technology stayed the loudest room in the portfolio. NVIDIA jumped on reports of a nearly thirteen billion dollar Hugging Face deal, Adobe pushed its AI tools straight into Slack, and Okta drew a substantial price target hike on AI agent growth. Consumer names split the difference: Lululemon faces a market expecting it to deliver on earnings. The index fund book got a footnote of its own. Vanguard's trillion dollar S&P 500 fund has never been this concentrated, which is one way of saying the market has picked its favorites and stopped pretending otherwise.

On individual names, the advisors have been busy. They discussed trimming the NVIDIA position toward a smaller share of the book in staged tranches over the coming weeks, using early proceeds to open a position in Berkshire Hathaway.
