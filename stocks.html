By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Aug 21

Wall Street closed the books on another session of the same argument it has been having for weeks: whether the rate mood justifies the multiples the market is paying for it. The major indices traded without much conviction today, which is a polite way of saying investors are waiting for someone else to make the first move. Adobe added AI audio tools to Firefly, a sentence that would have sounded like science fiction three years ago and now barely registers as news.

The technology sleeve, the largest and most concentrated part of this book, had a mixed and busy day. AMD landed a chip deal with Google that puts it further into Nvidia's territory, and Okta drew a price target increase from Morgan Stanley built on what analysts are calling the agentic identity boom, a phrase that will look either prescient or embarrassing within the year. Consumer names were noisier. Lululemon lost another senior executive, the third in a row, which is no longer a departure so much as a pattern, while Amazon spent the day explaining why a Jason Statham movie was available before anyone meant it to be.

Her advisors have been discussing a trim of the Nvidia position down to a smaller share of the portfolio, executed in stages over the coming weeks, with part of the proceeds seeding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Beyond that, the conversation has turned to healthcare and international diversification, firm exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names, and a decision, finally, on what to do with Intel.

No limbo, the advisors said. Hong Kong bureaucracy could learn something from that.
