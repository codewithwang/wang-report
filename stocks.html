By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Oct 3

The index screens were blank at filing, so there are no Nasdaq, S&P 500 or Hang Seng levels to read, and no fresh rate data. The one broad signal is the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF, up 0.7% as a Tesla rally lifted the wider market. Nvidia closed at a record, its market value approaching six trillion dollars. The tape was firm and narrow, led by the same handful of names.

The portfolio's sectors split along that line. Technology, the largest and most concentrated part of the book, had a strong day. Cybersecurity led it, with CrowdStrike and Okta both reaching one year highs as AI lifted the sector. AMD agreed to pay 8.2 billion dollars for World Labs, a bet that buying beats building. Consumer cyclical was weaker. Lululemon slid to a fifty two week low as its selloff deepened, while Amazon is reportedly looking to move 8 billion dollars of Nvidia chips off its balance sheet. Amazon would like to sell Nvidia chips on the day Nvidia hits a record. Timing is a skill.

On individual names, her advisors have been consistent. Nvidia comes first, with a trim in three tranches over four to six weeks; the first would fund a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Next come healthcare exposure through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, and international exposure through VXUS. On SoundHound and Unusual Machines the advisors want hard rules: no adds, and a full exit if the thesis breaks. On Intel, cut or commit, with no limbo. Also under consideration are a currency review tied to the Hong Kong dollar peg and a US estate tax check.
