By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Jul 25

Amazon's credit outlook drew fresh scrutiny today. Moody's flagged the pace of AI capital spending as a rating risk, even as the broader tape held its composure. The Nasdaq's AI infrastructure story kept running in parallel: Nvidia and SK Group put half a trillion dollars behind a new data center and memory buildout, and AMD surged after hours on Intel's stronger than expected results, dragging Arm along with it.

Technology is still the dominant weather system in this portfolio, and today it delivered both vindication and vulnerability. The AMD and Nvidia news reads as validation for a concentrated semiconductor book. Adobe's disclosure that a phished support agent leaked thirteen million records is the reminder that infrastructure risk in this sector isn't only about chips, and Okta's move to lock down AI agents with new identity controls speaks to the same nerve. Consumer names had a quieter day: Lululemon's league-wide MLB collection is brand marketing, not a balance sheet event, and Apple closed the book on its Siri settlement.

Her advisors have been discussing trimming Nvidia, currently the portfolio's most concentrated holding, in tranches over the coming weeks, with proceeds seeding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Healthcare and international exposure are next on the list, alongside firm exit rules for the portfolio's smaller speculative names. Intel is on the desk for a decision, not a delay.
