By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Oct 6

The index tape is thin today. Figures for the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng were not available at filing, so there is no honest number to read you, and no rate or economic data point was supplied either. The headlines do show a market still paying up for chips. Nvidia hit a record high and is closing in on a six trillion dollar market cap, while AMD's chief executive says demand is outrunning capacity. Software is the weaker corner, with strategists hunting for footing.

The portfolio's sectors pull in different directions. It is a concentrated technology book, with semiconductors as the engine and a leveraged semiconductor fund riding on top of the single names. Software infrastructure is the soft spot, where one analyst calls the agentic AI enthusiasm around Okta overdone. Consumer cyclical is mixed: Amazon is getting a bullish read on its chip plans, while Lululemon is down sharply and now selling other brands' leggings. The index fund and the energy fund sit quietly in the background. The iBond, as ever, does nothing, which is its job.

On the names, her advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway position. Next, they would add healthcare through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, and international exposure through VXUS. They want hard rules on SoundHound and Unusual Machines, and a decision on Intel: cut or commit. Currency and estate tax reviews are also on the list. The portfolio's biggest winner is being sold to buy a conglomerate. Nobody said it was dull.
