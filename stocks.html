By Charmaine LoNews AnchorFri, Oct 9

The major index readings were missing from today's feed, so there is no full tape to read, and nothing on the economy or the rate mood either. The one marker on hand is the broad market: the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF slipped 0.4 percent, with CrowdStrike dragging on the index. For a portfolio that holds both the fund and the stock, that is the same bad afternoon booked twice.

Technology, the concentrated core of this book, had a busy news day. Nvidia pledged one billion dollars to US science over five years. Adobe landed a Heathrow deal covering the airport's digital overhaul and AI search. AMD heads toward a November 3 earnings test against an $800 target. In consumer cyclical, Amazon is cutting nearly 1,000 white-collar jobs across the US, UK and India while hiring AI talent, and Lululemon handed its product keys to a Nike veteran as its chief executive thinned the C-suite. In software infrastructure, Okta partnered with Yubico, because passwords have declined to die. The company that sells the cure for passwords is now partnering to outlast them.

On the names, her advisors have been most specific about Nvidia. They discussed trimming it toward a smaller share of the portfolio, sold in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. They then raised healthcare through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, plus an international fund. For SoundHound and Unusual Machines they propose hard rules, including no further adds. On Intel, they want a decision to cut or commit, with no limbo. A currency review and a US estate tax review are also on their list.
