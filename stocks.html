By Charmaine LoNews AnchorMon, Oct 5

The tape is thin on numbers tonight. The feed carried no readings for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq or the Hang Seng, so there is no index move to report and none to invent. Nothing in today's headlines addresses the economy or the rate mood either. What they do show is stocks sorted into winners and losers by story rather than by index, with artificial intelligence on both sides of the ledger.

Across the sectors this portfolio holds, technology is the bright spot. Nvidia is set for a record close on strong supplier commentary, and AMD has passed one trillion dollars in value under Lisa Su. Apple is the exception: an iPhone 18 Pro Max recall has put Qualcomm in the crosshairs. Consumer cyclical is soft. Amazon sank on agentic AI fears, and Lululemon fell with Nike, Adidas and Under Armour in a sportswear rout. In software, Okta's consensus price target jumped as analysts turned more bullish. Adobe still has a quarter of the company left to buy back, which is one way to express confidence in your own product.

Her advisors are focused on the technology concentration. They have discussed trimming Nvidia in stages and using the first tranche to open a Berkshire Hathaway Class B position, with a healthcare name such as UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson and an international fund through VXUS next in line. For SoundHound and Unusual Machines, they want hard rules: no adds, partial profit on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. Intel needs a decision, cut or commit. So the portfolio is being asked to reduce its exposure to its best performer on the day that performer sets a record.
