By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Aug 13

Wall Street traded without a clear signal today, and the mood matched it: rate expectations still unsettled, indices searching for conviction rather than finding it. The tape did not crash. It did not celebrate either. It waited, the way markets do when the next data point outranks the last one.

The portfolio's technology weight, still its dominant character, moved on two different stories at once. NVIDIA drew fresh attention over a very large financing arrangement, one that has rivals eyeing custom chip alternatives. Dominance invites imitation. AMD moved on its own AI infrastructure ambitions, while Adobe's commerce platform took a reputational hit after hackers exploited a flaw in it. Consumer names split too: Amazon pressed further into AI by training models on Twitch streams, opt-out required, not opt-in, while Lululemon slid on a weak outlook that has investors questioning the turnaround story. Okta, by contrast, got an upgrade on optimism about AI-driven security spending.

Her advisors have been busiest behind the scenes. They are trimming the NVIDIA position gradually, in tranches, and redirecting part of the proceeds into a new Berkshire Hathaway stake. Healthcare and international diversification are next on the list, alongside firmer rules for the portfolio's smaller, speculative names. Intel is the one position advisors say cannot stay undecided much longer. Even patient money has a deadline.
