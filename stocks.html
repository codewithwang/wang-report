The macro tape is quiet. Index levels for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq and the Hang Seng were not available at filing time, so there is no closing call. The rate mood and the broader economy have no fresh data point in today's file, and the sensible course is to say so rather than invent a narrative.

Sector by sector, the picture is mixed and tilted toward technology. Semiconductors are in full voice, with Nvidia pressing toward a six trillion dollar valuation and a fresh record high, which matters a great deal to a concentrated technology book and its leveraged semiconductor holding. Software is split: Okta has reached a three-year high, while Adobe is still being asked how much further it can fall. Consumer cyclical had the rougher day. Lululemon plunged on growth troubles and a securities fraud probe, and Amazon faces questions over a cloud growth statistic. Apple is widening its smart home push with LG. The broad index fund draws attention for the artificial intelligence engine beneath it.

Among the names in focus, the advisors are discussing a measured trim of Nvidia, sold in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Next in line, they point to healthcare exposure through UnitedHealth or Johnson and Johnson, plus an international fund. Soundhound and Unusual Machines would get hard rules: no adds, half off on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. On Intel, the advice is to cut or commit, with no limbo. They also float a currency review and a US estate tax check. Nvidia is at a record high, and the advisors want to sell some of it. Discipline, it turns out, is easiest at the top.
