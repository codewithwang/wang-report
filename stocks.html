By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Aug 22

Amazon opened its wallet to memory prices this week, and closed it again on hardware margins, lifting Echo, Fire TV and Kindle prices as much as sixty percent. That is not a company chasing growth. That is a company passing a bill along and hoping no one reads the itemization. Okta drew a two hundred million dollar vote of confidence that reads as enterprise identity spend holding up, while Apple trimmed Siri and Vision Pro staff, a signal that its most ambitious bets are being quietly resized. Every S&P 500 fund owner, meanwhile, now holds more Nvidia than Apple, a sentence that would have sounded like science fiction three years ago and now just sounds like Tuesday.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated position, carries the week's real tension. Nvidia is in exclusive talks to back a Cloverleaf Power developer, extending its reach into the energy that feeds its own chips, even as AMD investors brace for a major announcement due August 26. Amazon's price increases sit against a Lululemon leadership departure in communications: two companies, two different kinds of exposure.

Her advisors have been trimming Nvidia toward a smaller share of the book, selling in tranches over the coming weeks, with proceeds seeding a new Berkshire Hathaway position. They are also mapping healthcare and international allocations to build over the next month, and drafting hard rules for the portfolio's smaller speculative names: no adds, half off on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. Intel is a decision still owed, not a position to hold indefinitely.
