By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Aug 6

Bezos filed to sell four billion dollars of Amazon stock today, and the market sold first and asked questions never. That single filing moved the tape more than most of this week's macro data, dragging the stock down even as the broader mega cap trade stayed calm. Chipmakers had their own turn, with Nvidia and Micron among Wall Street's most active names, while AMD's Lisa Su walked through weak earnings with the composure of a hostage negotiator.

Software had the better day. Okta rallied alongside Salesforce, HubSpot, Workday and Datadog, proof enterprise software can still catch a bid when the mood turns. The index trade split too: Amazon and Microsoft added a trillion dollars between them over two days while Apple and Meta gave back half that. Even the market's favourite trade argues with itself. Security software drew less happy attention after researchers disclosed critical flaws in Adobe's campaign tools, flaws that let attackers run code without so much as a password.

Inside the technology book, the advisors have been discussing trimming Nvidia from its place as the largest position, doing it patiently, in stages over the coming weeks, and recycling the proceeds into a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They have also flagged Apple's fresh headache over departing staff and OpenAI, floated adding healthcare and international exposure in the months ahead, and agreed on one thing without qualification: Intel needs a decision, not another quarter of waiting.
