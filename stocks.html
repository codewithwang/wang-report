By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Jul 26

Amazon's credit quality is under fresh scrutiny, Moody's flagging the pace of AI spending even as the broader tape leans on that same spending as its reason to keep climbing. The Vanguard S&P 500 book benefits from that logic more than anyone: the tech and momentum trade has been carrying the index, and the honest answer to whether that continues is that no one really knows. That has not stopped anyone from acting like they do.

Inside the portfolio, the technology sleeve is doing most of the talking. NVIDIA's memory partnership with SK Group is more evidence for the AI-infrastructure case, and Lisa Su's comments on hyperscaler returns give the AMD position the same tailwind. Apple and Micron find themselves on opposite sides of a Washington-Beijing chip dispute they did not ask to referee. Adobe spent part of the day explaining why a Chrome PDF extension was reading people's WhatsApp chats, a sentence no software company wants to say twice. Okta's pullback has traders debating entry points while its own forecasts stay bullish. Lululemon picked up a retail partnership with Fanatics and Major League Baseball.

The advisors have been busy. Guidance now calls for trimming the NVIDIA position in staged tranches and using the proceeds to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Next up for discussion: adding healthcare and international exposure, tightening the rules around the SoundHound and Unusual Machines positions, and finally forcing a decision on Intel. Buy or walk away. No more sitting on the fence.
