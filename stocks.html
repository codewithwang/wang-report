By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Sep 16

Markets offered no fresh print today, so the tape traded on positioning, with rates still the current running under every index move. That is why portfolio construction, not headline-chasing, is doing the work this week.

The technology book remains the largest position by a wide margin, and it is also where the most activity sits. Chipmakers and software infrastructure names are trading in tandem with the broader AI narrative, while the leveraged semiconductor exposure amplifies whatever direction that group takes. Energy and the core index holding sit quieter, doing their job as ballast rather than driver. Consumer cyclical names are tracking discretionary spending sentiment more than any single catalyst.

The advisors have been flagging concentration risk in the technology book. The headline move: trim the NVIDIA position down to a smaller share of the portfolio, executed in three tranches over four to six weeks rather than all at once. Proceeds from the first tranche open a new Berkshire Hathaway stake, ballast against the semiconductor weighting. Also on the table: building healthcare exposure through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, adding international diversification via a broad ex-US fund, and writing exit rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names before emotion writes them instead. Intel remains a decision pending, and the advisors have said plainly it is time to choose a side. A currency hedge and an estate tax review round out the list. Even a technology-heavy book eventually has to think about paperwork.
