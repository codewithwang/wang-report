By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Aug 20

The chip selloff hit the tape hard today, and the portfolio felt it. Semiconductors led broad market losses as AMD and Intel shares dropped together, part of a rotation the Street is calling money fleeing chips for software and streaming names. Adobe caught a bid on exactly that flow, trading up alongside Netflix and Salesforce as investors reached for safer growth. Rate expectations stayed the dominant conversation on desks watching for the next signal on when cuts arrive.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated position, bore the brunt of the selloff. NVIDIA drew particular attention even as Beijing signaled a partial opening for its H200 chip in China. Okta stood apart, with Jefferies setting the Street's highest price target yet on the stock. Consumer names were mixed. Lululemon lost another senior executive, its third departure. Amazon dealt with a less flattering headline: scammers using its own order-confirmation emails as a phishing template. Apple, meanwhile, found itself the subject of a report on old financial obligations tied to a government intelligence contract, a story with more appeal to reporters than to shareholders.

The advisors have been busy. They are trimming the NVIDIA position down to a smaller share of the book, doing it in stages over the next month or so, and using the proceeds to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Healthcare and international diversification are next on the list, alongside firm new rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names: no more adding, and a plan to cut losses if the thesis breaks. Intel remains a decision still pending, and the advisors say it is time to commit one way or the other.
