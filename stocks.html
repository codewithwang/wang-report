By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Oct 1

The index screens were blank at filing time, so there are no S&P 500, Nasdaq or Hang Seng levels to report and no rate commentary worth citing. The tape did offer mood, and the mood was constructive for technology. Buybacks and price-target upgrades did most of the talking.

Technology led the sectors in this portfolio. NVIDIA jumped on a record buyback, Apple rose on talk of a smart home hub at its October 13 event, Adobe rallied on AI partnerships, and AMD announced its purchase of Fei-Fei Li's World Labs. In software infrastructure, Okta climbed after Morgan Stanley raised its price target. Consumer cyclicals were quieter. Amazon is planning smart glasses for delivery drivers, and Lululemon and its founder extended a board seat deadline, so the standoff continues. The index fund conversation was the familiar VOO versus QQQ. Energy, fixed income and the leveraged semiconductor fund had no headline of note.

Her advisors are focused on the largest position. They have discussed trimming NVIDIA in stages over several weeks, using the first stage to open a Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Soon, they would add healthcare exposure through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, international exposure through VXUS, and firm rules for SoundHound and Unusual Machines: no adds, and a defined exit if the thesis breaks. Intel gets a decision, cut or commit, with no limbo. Currency and US estate tax reviews are on the list too. The stock is up, and the advisors are discussing selling it. That is either discipline or an unusually polite way of taking profits.
