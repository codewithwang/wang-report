Today’s briefing supplies no economic releases, interest rate signals or index readings for the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng. It offers no basis for calling markets higher or lower, or declaring a change in the rate mood. Company headlines give a clearer account of competition for customers and computing demand. They cannot establish market direction.

Technology supplies most of the news across the portfolio’s sectors. AMD’s Anthropic headline points to a lift. Intel has reportedly regained CPU market share, and Nvidia is plotting a comeback with allies, according to the FT. Apple’s glasses ambitions depend on Siri and the iPhone, while Adobe faces questions over Firefly’s “safe AI” pitch. The marketing department cannot certify that one. In consumer discretionary, Amazon is pursuing shopping searches and Canadians are turning to Lululemon resale. Broader sector moves remain unverified.

The advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in three tranches over four to six weeks, using the first sale to fund a new Berkshire Hathaway position. They also propose healthcare exposure through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, alongside international diversification through VXUS.

For SoundHound AI and Unusual Machines, they propose no further buying. Sell half if the price doubles; exit if the thesis breaks. Intel needs a cut or commit decision. Hong Kong dollar exposure and US estate tax treatment await review. All of these remain proposals. No trades are confirmed.
