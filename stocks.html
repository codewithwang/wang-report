By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Oct 4

Index data is not in the feed today. The S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng all came through as n/a, so there is no honest read on the tape, and I won't invent one. The rate mood and the broader economy are likewise missing from the brief. The headlines show chip names making most of the noise on artificial intelligence.

The sectors in this portfolio moved unevenly. Technology had the loudest day: AMD reached a trillion dollars in market value, and Nvidia clawed back its crown after a trillion-dollar wipeout. Intel has more than doubled this year on chip demand, good news for a concentrated technology book with a leveraged semiconductor fund in it. Apple said it will replace AT&T iPhone 18 Pro Max units hit by cellular failures. Consumer cyclical split. Amazon began sending Prime refunds under the expanded FTC settlement, while Lululemon fell with Nike, Adidas and Under Armour in a sportswear rout. Software infrastructure was quieter. Okta's partner ecosystem passed 25, as AI gateways become identity's new enforcement layer.

On the names, her advisors want NVIDIA trimmed toward a smaller share, sold in three tranches over four to six weeks, with the first tranche funding a new Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Next, they have discussed healthcare through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, plus an international allocation through VXUS. SoundHound and Unusual Machines get hard rules: no adds, half off on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. On Intel, the advisors say cut or commit, no limbo. A portfolio trimming its best performer to buy a conglomerate is, at least, consistent about not chasing.
