By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Aug 20

Rate paths on all three indices sat out today's feed, which left the tape to be read through positioning rather than price. What moved was rotation talk: strategists flagged that the S&P 500 now carries a technology weighting north of a third of the fund, which says less about market risk than about what "diversified" has come to mean this cycle.

Inside the book, the split was clean. Chips took the worst of it, Intel and AMD both sold off hard in a broad semiconductor pullback. Software found a bid instead. Adobe rallied as money rotated out of chips and into names Wall Street had been ignoring. Okta went the other way even as the broader market rallied, proof that one rough quarter can outweigh a good week. NVIDIA, meanwhile, stayed in the headlines for reasons that had nothing to do with its stock price, reportedly in talks to bankroll an AI startup at a twenty billion dollar valuation. That is one way to remind the market who is still writing the checks, even while trimming its own position.

Her advisors have opened discussions on trimming the largest holding, NVIDIA, in stages over the coming weeks, with proceeds seeding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They are also weighing new healthcare and international allocations, drafting hard exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names, and deciding, once and for all, what to do with Intel. No more limbo, they say.

Intel, for its part, does not appear to have gotten the memo.
