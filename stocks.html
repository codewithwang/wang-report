By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Aug 5

OpenAI's chip demand set the mainboard's tone this week, AI infrastructure spending still the dominant macro story even as broad index levels held steady. Nvidia got a fresh vote of confidence when Musk confirmed SpaceX is going all in on its chips over AMD. AMD's own results told a split story: strong guidance on AI demand, shares lower anyway, because Wall Street had already bet on more. Rate mood stayed the quiet undercurrent this week. Earnings did the talking.

The portfolio's technology book, its most concentrated exposure by some distance, was where the action concentrated too. Apple is fighting OpenAI in court over trade secrets and getting called "oddly personal" for its trouble, hardly the tone of a company that wanted a quiet lawsuit. Adobe is dealing with attackers disguising malware as its own software updates. Consumer names had their moment as well. Amazon shares jumped sharply on earnings, and Lululemon picked up a lawsuit over its reference pricing on the way up.

Bezos took the Amazon rally as his cue to sell four billion dollars of stock, which the advisors read less as alarm and more as portfolio math. On Nvidia, the largest position in the book, the advisors have discussed trimming it down in stages over the coming weeks and using the proceeds to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They have also flagged building out healthcare and international exposure, writing firmer rules around the portfolio's smaller speculative names, and finally forcing a decision on Intel rather than leaving it undecided.
