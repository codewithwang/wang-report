By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSun, Aug 23

Trading was quiet enough today that the tape itself became the story, which is another way of saying nobody blinked. Rate mood held steady, the major indices held their line, and the portfolio's own tilt did the heavy lifting: a technology book that dominates by design, leaning hard into chips and infrastructure while a single fixed income holding, the iBond, sits there as the adult in the room.

Underneath, the sector story split. Technology carried mixed headlines. Apple trimmed Siri and Vision Pro staff even as its shares sit near the portfolio's largest position. AMD extended its x86 share gains at Intel's expense ahead of a flagged announcement next week. CrowdStrike lost its chief technology officer mid-security-push. Consumer names had their own week: Amazon raised device prices as memory costs climbed, and Lululemon picked up a Texas inquiry into its fabric chemistry. The index and energy sleeves stayed out of the headlines entirely, which on a day like this counts as an achievement.

Individually, the advisors have been busiest around the edges. They have discussed trimming Nvidia down from its outsized perch in stages over the coming weeks, using the proceeds to open a new Berkshire Hathaway position, and building out healthcare and international exposure they currently lack. They have also proposed firm exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names, SoundHound and Unusual Machines, and want a clear decision on Intel rather than continued indecision. A currency hedge and an estate tax review round out the list. The quiet days, it turns out, are when the real planning happens.
