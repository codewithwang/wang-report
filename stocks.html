By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Aug 5

Hong Kong equities held little sway today with headline benchmarks unreported, but the tape underneath told its own story. Chipmakers stayed the center of gravity: AMD posted revenue up fifty percent, doubled its data center sales, and sold off anyway. The market had already priced perfection and demanded a second helping. Nvidia found support elsewhere, with SpaceX disclosing a large compute commitment built on its chips, even as Adobe users were warned that a fake update prompt circulating online is actually malware wearing a trustworthy logo.

The portfolio's technology and leveraged semiconductor exposure took the day's real volatility, AMD's results a reminder that a beat is no longer a floor, just an admission price. Consumer names split hard: Amazon drew a New Jersey lawsuit over driver pay and control, while Lululemon fell to a six year low before Barclays turned constructive on the incoming CEO. Software infrastructure had a defensive session. Okta moved to acquire Permiso and shore up identity threat detection rather than wait to need it.

Apple drew fire from OpenAI, which called its lawsuit careless and unusually personal, a rare case of Silicon Valley's lawyers sounding more wounded than its engineers.

On positioning, the advisors have been discussing trimming the largest position in Nvidia in stages over the coming weeks, using early proceeds to open a new Berkshire Hathaway stake. Intel, meanwhile, was flagged as a name that needs a decision. A holding pattern is not a strategy, it is a delay with a ticker symbol.
