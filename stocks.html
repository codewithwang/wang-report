By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Aug 26

Markets closed flat with no clear read on the tape, but the mood underneath was anything but quiet. Adobe surged after lifting its 2026 AI guidance, the kind of upgrade that makes a software name look like a growth stock again, while Nvidia spent the day answering to a smuggling case implicating both its own staff and Supermicro's, a reminder that chip export controls have a human dimension prosecutors are now happy to test. AMD drew fresh attention too, cast as the challenger with room to run against Nvidia's dominance. Retail had its own reckoning: Dick's Sporting Goods slid 25 percent and pulled Lululemon down with it, guilt by sector association.

The portfolio's center of gravity remains technology, a concentrated book spanning chipmakers, software infrastructure and AI-adjacent names, with a leveraged semiconductor position amplifying whatever the sector decides to do next. Consumer cyclical exposure through Amazon and Lululemon is now contending with real read-through risk from the broader retail selloff. Fixed income and the broad index and energy funds sat quietly by, doing what ballast is supposed to do.

Her advisors have been circling a rebalancing story more than a single stock story: trimming Nvidia down from its outsized perch in stages, using the proceeds to open a position in Berkshire Hathaway, and eyeing healthcare and international funds as the next additions. They have also drawn hard lines around the portfolio's smaller, more speculative names. No news, they've made clear, is good enough news to add to them. Apple's headquarters layoffs and Okta's exposure in the ReliaQuest breach claim were both noted, filed for now under things to watch rather than things to act on.
