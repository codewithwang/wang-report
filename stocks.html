By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Sep 2

Wall Street opened the session leaning on a familiar question: whether the rate mood justifies the multiples technology has already priced in. The tape traded without conviction, index levels doing less talking than the stories underneath them, and the underneath was busy. Regulators went after one of the portfolio's own consumer names, a chipmaker signed a European supercomputer deal, and a cloud security name posted the kind of quarter that reminds everyone why the sector still gets paid a premium.

The portfolio's sectors split along a line that keeps reappearing. Technology, the book's largest and most concentrated position, absorbed a mixed set of signals: an AI infrastructure deal here, a leadership succession there, an earnings beat in software infrastructure that argues the AI buildout still has paying customers on the other end of it. Consumer cyclical had a rougher day, with an antitrust suit landing on one name and earnings-day nerves gripping another. The energy and index exposures traded as ballast, doing what ballast does, which is not much and on purpose.

Among individual names, Okta's quarter was the standout, up sharply on enterprise demand for AI agent security, a category that barely existed at last year's board meeting. NVIDIA drew fresh validation from a large cloud computing commitment, even as the advisors have discussed trimming it toward a smaller share of the book, in tranches, with proceeds seeding a new Berkshire Hathaway position. Adobe's Saudi AI investment left the stock nearly untouched. Separately, the advisors have been discussing a healthcare allocation, international diversification, hard exit rules on the portfolio's most speculative names, and a decision on Intel, one way or the other.
