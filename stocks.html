The index tape did not arrive with today's file, so there are no levels for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq or the Hang Seng to quote, and no read on rates either. Rather than guess at a number, the desk leaves the macro picture blank and lets the individual names carry the day.

Among the sectors this portfolio holds, the signals are uneven. Sportswear had a hard session, with Nike, Adidas, Under Armour and Lululemon all caught in the rout. Technology is where the energy is. AMD has reached a trillion dollar market cap and has Nvidia's crown in its sights, while Amazon is reportedly looking to move billions in Nvidia chips off its books, which points to a change in how AI is financed. In software infrastructure, Okta says its identity ecosystem now counts more than two dozen partners as AI gateways become the new enforcement layer. A company that sells chips and a company that sells locks are both having a good week, which is one way to describe an arms race.

On the names in focus, Nvidia is back in the valuation argument because Michael Burry has reopened it. Her advisors have been discussing a gradual trim of that position, sold in three tranches over several weeks, with the first tranche funding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway's Class B shares. After that, they would add healthcare exposure through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, plus an international fund. They also want firm rules on SoundHound and Unusual Machines: no adds, a partial exit on a double, a full exit if the thesis breaks. Intel gets a decision, cut or commit, with no limbo. Currency and estate tax reviews are also under consideration.
