By Charmaine LoNews AnchorSat, Aug 1

Fed Chair Powell's press conference kept its promised tone this week, patient, watchful, and giving markets little to grab onto either way. Rate expectations barely budged, and equities took their cues from earnings instead of the Fed, a rotation that rewards stockpickers over index-watchers. That backdrop suits a portfolio built the way this one is: concentrated, opinionated, and willing to be wrong loudly rather than right quietly.

Technology is doing the talking again, but not with one voice. NVIDIA reclaimed the title of world's most valuable company the same week Apple fell nearly ten percent after Tim Cook flagged a memory shortage. Even the biggest names answer to their supply chains. Chip stocks rallied premarket even as the group heads for its worst month since 2002, and Intel's surge added another twist to a sector that cannot decide if it is celebrating or bracing. Consumer names split too. Amazon soared after AWS delivered what one headline called a home run quarter, while Lululemon was named in a lawsuit over so-called phantom discounts, a pricing scheme that always sounds better in a boardroom than in a courtroom.

Okta's acquisition of threat detection startup Permiso drew notice inside the technology and software infrastructure sleeve. Her advisors have been trimming the largest position, NVIDIA, in stages, with proceeds earmarked to open a new Berkshire Hathaway stake, while healthcare and international exposure are queued for the weeks ahead. Intel remains a decision still pending. Not yet a conviction.
