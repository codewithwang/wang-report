By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Aug 13

Amazon opened its Twitch stream library to AI training on an opt-out basis, Adobe customers had their accounts hijacked through an unpatched Commerce flaw, and Nvidia's half-trillion-dollar financing arrangement has Alphabet reportedly rattled about its custom silicon ambitions. Chipmakers rallied anyway. AMD climbed alongside Intel and Nvidia on a broad AI earnings sweep, one of those sessions where the sector's winners and its risks shared the same headline.

The portfolio's technology book, its largest and most concentrated position, sits at the center of today's cross-currents. AMD's rally lifts the semiconductor names directly, and the leveraged semiconductor exposure amplifies that move in both directions. Okta was upgraded on the view that AI security is now a durable growth line, which flatters a software infrastructure holding that has otherwise sat quietly. Consumer names split: Amazon absorbed its Twitch news without much drama, while Lululemon slid on a weak outlook that revived turnaround questions the stock has faced for months.

Apple is reportedly willing to pay publishers to fix Siri's AI shortcomings. Call it a negotiation with its cover blown. On positioning, the advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in stages over the coming weeks and using early proceeds to open a Berkshire Hathaway stake, alongside plans to build out healthcare and international exposure and to write firm exit rules for the portfolio's more speculative names. Intel, Micron and CrowdStrike remain in view without new guidance today.
