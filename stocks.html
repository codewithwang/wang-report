By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Sep 15

Markets closed flat today, no clear signal from the majors. Rate expectations have settled into a holding pattern, with traders waiting on the next data print before committing to a direction. Volumes were the real tell, thin enough that a handful of large orders could have moved the tape and mostly didn't. When nobody blinks, nobody trades.

Inside the portfolio, technology remains the dominant story by a wide margin. The semiconductor complex does the heavy lifting through Nvidia, AMD, Micron and Intel, amplified further by the leveraged semiconductor fund sitting alongside them. Consumer names in Amazon and Lululemon tracked the broader risk mood, while the fixed income sleeve, anchored by the Hong Kong Government iBond, kept doing its usual job as ballast. Energy and index exposures rounded out the picture without much drama, which on a day like today counts as a compliment.

The advisors have been circling the concentrated technology book, discussing a staged trim of the Nvidia position, sold down gradually over several weeks rather than all at once, with proceeds earmarked to open a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Separately, they have flagged a healthcare allocation, weighing UnitedHealth against Johnson & Johnson, and floated adding international exposure through Vanguard's ex-US fund. On the smaller, more volatile names, SoundHound AI and Unusual Machines, the advisors want firm rules written down before the next swing, not after it. Intel stays on notice. Commit or cut, no more waiting.
