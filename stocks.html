By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Aug 19

Markets traded without a clear read today, and the mood matched it: rate anxiety sitting alongside a fresh round of AI capital commitments, as if no one in Silicon Valley checked the bond market before signing. Yields moved enough to unsettle the chip trade, and the portfolio's technology book, concentrated and not a small thing to steer, felt it most directly.

The sector story here is really one sector wearing several hats. Technology dominates this book, and today it split into winners and worriers. Nvidia extended its footprint by backing a data center commitment in Ohio large enough to make Columbus sound like the new Cupertino, while AMD sat on the losing end of the same yield spike that made leverage, and this portfolio holds a 3X semiconductor bet, look considerably less comfortable than it did last week. Consumer cyclical had a bad afternoon. Software infrastructure had a good one.

Apple drew unwelcome attention over a spyware alert wave affecting its users, a headline that pairs oddly with Adobe drawing a large new institutional bet the same day. Lululemon slid after a prominent fund exited its stake. Okta got an upgrade and a higher price target. Amazon, meanwhile, was accused of shredding rare books to train AI, which is either a metaphor or literally the news, and possibly both.

Her advisors have been discussing trimming the Nvidia position in stages while starting a Berkshire Hathaway stake, adding healthcare and international exposure over coming weeks, and drawing firm rules around the portfolio's more speculative names before the market draws them instead.
