From the desk · Daily read
By Charmaine LoNews AnchorThu, Sep 17

Markets held steady with no fresh catalyst, and rate watchers stayed cautious rather than convicted. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq traded without sharp moves, the Hang Seng offered nothing dramatic either, and investors read patience into the pause. Sector rotation has been the real story lately, and today was no exception, with money still weighing where growth ends and value begins.

In this portfolio, technology carries the weight. Apple, NVIDIA, Adobe, AMD, Intel, CrowdStrike, Palantir, Micron, SoundHound AI, and Unusual Machines form a concentrated technology book. Software infrastructure, represented by Okta, sits alongside consumer names Amazon and Lululemon, while leveraged semiconductor exposure and broad market and energy ETFs round out the picture. Fixed income, through the HK Government iBond, is the ballast.

The advisors have been talking through what comes next for the largest position. The plan: trim NVIDIA down to a smaller share of the book, in tranches over the coming weeks, with proceeds seeding a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. They have also flagged building out healthcare and international exposure over time, and drafting hard rules for the more speculative names, SoundHound AI and Unusual Machines, on when to trim and when to walk away entirely.

Intel remains under review. The advisors are framing it plainly: a call the portfolio has put off long enough, and one they now have to make.
