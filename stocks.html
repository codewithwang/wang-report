By Charmaine LoNews AnchorWed, Oct 7

Charmaine Lo, News Anchor

The macro tape is thin today. Index levels for the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng did not come through, and there is no fresh read on rates or the economy, so this desk will not pretend to a number it does not have. The headlines leaned on artificial intelligence demand: SpaceX is reportedly seeking $40 billion to buy Nvidia chips, and AMD's Lisa Su promised substantially more supply in 2027.

For the sectors in this portfolio, the day was uneven. Technology, the concentrated core of the book, drew supportive news. Apple is pushing into the connected home with LG, and chip supply is the story of the week. Software infrastructure had the better day, with Okta at a fresh three year high. Consumer cyclical split sharply. Amazon began sending refund payments, while Lululemon fell 17% as law firms opened securities fraud probes over its growth disclosures. Adobe is being asked how much downside is left, a question stocks rarely enjoy. The broad index fund faces the old comparison with the Nasdaq, which the Nasdaq is winning.

Among the names in focus, her advisors want Nvidia, the largest position, trimmed in stages over several weeks, with the first proceeds opening a Berkshire Hathaway Class B stake. Soon after, they would add healthcare through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, add international exposure through VXUS, and write firm rules for SoundHound and Unusual Machines: no adds, partial profits on a double, a full exit if the thesis breaks. Intel gets an ultimatum, cut or commit. The advisors also raised a currency review and a US estate tax review. The portfolio, in short, is about to acquire rules.
