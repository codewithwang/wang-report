By Charmaine LoNews AnchorTue, Aug 18

Wells Fargo thinks it has found the future in identity verification, and NVIDIA has decided the future is worth a hundred and five billion dollars in Ohio, which is either visionary infrastructure or the most expensive way anyone has ever built a data center next to a cornfield. The broader chip trade kept its momentum, memory names surging on AI demand and dragging the semiconductor complex up with them, while Apple drew fresh attention on speculation about a deeper Nvidia tie-in of its own. Retail told a rougher story. Amazon spent the day explaining why it recycled rare books after digitizing them, and Lululemon lost two more senior executives, the kind of pattern that stops looking like coincidence once it reaches three.

Inside the portfolio, the technology book, by far the largest piece of it, is doing the heavy lifting again, chipmakers leading, software names like Adobe managing the transition to AI-driven products with rather more grace than the engineer who got laid off after twenty-five years and now drives a school bus. Consumer names are the softer patch, Amazon's publicity trouble and Lululemon's leadership churn sitting awkwardly next to a technology sleeve that mostly cannot stop rising. Energy and the broad index holding sit quietly in the background, doing what they are supposed to do, which today mostly meant not making news.

The advisors have been busy. They are trimming the NVIDIA position down to a more modest share of the book, doing it patiently across several tranches over the coming weeks, and steering the proceeds toward a new stake in Berkshire Hathaway. Looking further out, they have flagged building a healthcare allocation, likely through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, and adding international exposure through a broad ex-US fund. They are also drafting firmer rules for the portfolio's smaller, more volatile names, and want a clear decision on Intel one way or the other. Limbo, they note, is not a position.
