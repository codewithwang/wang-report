Index readings for the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Hang Seng did not arrive with today's tape, so there is no honest call on direction. Nothing in hand covers the economy or the rate mood either, and this desk will not invent either one. What the day does offer is company news, and plenty of it.

Technology, the concentrated part of the book, carries most of it. Apple rose on a report of an all new smart home hub ahead of its Oct. 13 event. Adobe surged midweek on AI integrations. AMD agreed to buy Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2 billion in stock. Nvidia authorized a $150 billion buyback, lifting total repurchase capacity to $235 billion, which is one way to say the company's best remaining investment is itself. In consumer cyclical, Amazon plans 20,000 smart glasses for delivery drivers by 2027, while Lululemon and Chip Wilson pushed back a deadline for a new board member. Okta won over Wall Street at its investor day. The index fund holding drew attention for how top heavy the big S&P funds have become.

On the names, her advisors have discussed trimming Nvidia in stages and using the first stage to open a Berkshire Hathaway Class B position. Next, they would add healthcare through UnitedHealth or Johnson & Johnson, and international exposure through VXUS. They want firm rules on SoundHound and Unusual Machines: no adds, half off on a double, full exit if the thesis breaks. On Intel, cut or commit, because limbo is not a strategy. A currency review and a US estate tax review are also under consideration.
