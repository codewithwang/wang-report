OpenAI Slows Release Pace as Chinese Rivals Cut Prices

OpenAI told staff it is deliberately slowing how fast it ships new models, a policy it's calling voluntary pacing, according to reporting Friday. The timing lands next to a separate move: OpenAI and Anthropic have cut API prices this week in response to Chinese competitors, most visibly Z.ai, whose new open-weight model release has drawn attention from security researchers for how capable it is at offensive cybersecurity tasks straight out of the box. Both are the same story told from two ends. A US lab that has spent three years shipping on a "ship it before the next lab does" clock is now choosing to slow down at the exact moment a Chinese lab is giving its model away for anyone to download and run.

Open-weight means Z.ai published the model's actual parameters, the numbers that encode what it learned, not just an API that lets you query a hosted version. Anyone with enough GPU hardware (Nvidia H100s or the Chinese-made Huawei Ascend equivalent) can download it, run it themselves, and never touch a Z.ai server again. That is why OpenAI's price cut and pacing decision arrive together: a free, downloadable competitor breaks the old model where OpenAI charged for API access and controlled the release calendar. OpenAI can slow its own launches, but it cannot slow Z.ai's, and every week that gap holds, more developers in Singapore, Bengaluru, and Seoul build production tools on the free model instead of the metered one. The number that will actually decide this: how many enterprise contracts OpenAI's Q3 earnings show renewed at the new price versus lost to a self-hosted Z.ai deployment.
