One Paper, Explained
Sep 20
Don't Mask the Environment: Observation Supervision Changes How Agents Explore Under RL
Juzheng Zhang, Disha Makhija, Manoj Ghuhan Arivazhagan

The paper shows that when reinforcement learning agents get their view of the environment masked or simplified during training, they explore worse and learn brittler policies than agents shown the full, unmasked observation. The mechanism: instead of hiding environment state to make training cleaner, let the agent see everything and supervise on that full observation, which naturally rewards exploration that finds new states. For a team building agentic systems with RL post-training, this reframes a common shortcut: don't sanitize what the agent perceives during training just because it simplifies reward design. Full observability during exploration is a lever for better-generalizing agents, not just an implementation detail. Teams tuning tool-using or web agents with RL should check whether their observation pipeline is quietly starving the agent of signal it needs to explore properly, before blaming the reward function or algorithm choice for weak exploration behavior.
