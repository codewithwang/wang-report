One Paper, Explained
Oct 4
Transformers Stop Thinking Too Early, and a Tiny LoRA Fixes It
Zehao Jin, Ruixuan Deng, Junran Wang

The authors claim that transformers commit to an answer before their internal computation has finished, so errors come from stopping early and not from missing knowledge. The mechanism, as the authors describe it, is that a language model refines its answer layer by layer, and on hard inputs that refinement halts too soon. They attach a tiny LoRA, a small add-on trained while the base model stays frozen, that pushes the model to keep refining before it answers. For a team building on models, the claim is that some failures that look like capability gaps can be addressed with a small adapter on the model you already run. That is a cheaper option than switching to a larger model or retraining. Check for early-stopping failures in your error logs before you pay for scale.

One paper a week that changes how people build with models, chosen and explained by Aya Nakamura: what it shows, the mechanism in one step, and what it changes for a team building on models.
