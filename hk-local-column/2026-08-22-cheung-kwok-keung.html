AFCD's Dog Chip System Doesn't Check What It Registers

Hong Kong's dog microchip system records whatever breed an owner claims without checking it, and that gap let a suspected fighting-breed dog register as a mongrel before it killed someone.
張 國 強 · Cheung Kwok-keung HK Desk, Senior Correspondent Sunday, August 23, 2026

The Chip Believed Him

A dog killed Jojo Wong at her own pet hotel in Ta Kwu Ling on August 20, in an attack that took about a minute. She was 33, and she ran the place, Pawsgate, a licensed pet hotel and training centre. The dog had arrived nine days earlier, brought in by a volunteer for adoption. One year old. Thirty kilos. A metre long. On paper it was tong gau, a local mongrel, filed in the same category as every soi dog sleeping under a market stall.

Except AFCD's own director, Mickey Lai Kin-ming, came out afterward and said the dog looks like it has fighting-breed ancestry.

Here's the part nobody wants to say straight: Hong Kong's microchip system doesn't test anything. Nobody swabs the dog. Nobody checks its teeth. Nobody looks at it and says, no mate, that is not a mongrel. You bring the dog in, you tell them what it is, and the chip just records your answer. AFCD's own licensing process has no inspection step to verify a declared breed against the animal in front of them.

A Review, Or A Patch

Wong's death was not a one-off. In the same week, four other dog attacks were reported. On August 17 an unleashed mastiff, fifty to sixty kilos, killed two small pet dogs near Yoho Mall in Yuen Long, and police arrested the 52-year-old owner. A cyclist got bitten in Shek O the next day. A woman got bitten in Jardine's Lookout the same afternoon Wong died.

The department has run 679 prosecutions over dog attacks since 2023, handled 1,183 dogs in the same stretch, and currently licenses 29 dogs as fighting breeds while flagging another 18 as known dangerous dogs. The Dangerous Dogs Regulation has banned importing or breeding Pit Bulls, Japanese Tosas, Dogo Argentinos and Fila Brazileiros since 2000. All that machinery, and it still could not stop a suspected fighting dog walking into a licensed pet hotel wearing a mongrel's chip.

Kent, who runs Paws Guardian Rescue Shelter, says there is a black market smuggling fighting-breed puppies in and misdeclaring them before the adult features show, standing next to Lai's admission that this dog looks like it has fighting-breed ancestry despite being registered as a mongrel. The government now says it will review the definition of a fighting dog.

So which review do we get? One that fixes how a dog gets registered in the first place, with someone actually looking at the animal instead of ticking a box. Or one that just adds a heavier fine for the next Jojo Wong, after the fact. The Dangerous Dogs Regulation has stood since 2000 and got us here. AFCD has not said when its review of the fighting-dog definition will conclude or who will sign off on the next breed classification.
